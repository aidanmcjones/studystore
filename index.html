Short answer

Define Km.

The substrate concentration at which v = Vmax/2. A rough inverse measure of substrate affinity.

Cloze

When [S] << Km the reaction is [...] order in substrate; when [S] >> Km it is [...] order.

Cloze

When [S] << Km the reaction is first order in substrate; when [S] >> Km it is zero order.

Enzyme is saturated at high [S], so adding substrate does nothing.

Multiple choice

A Lineweaver-Burk line crosses the x axis at -0.5 and the y axis at 0.1. Km and Vmax?

B. Km = -1/(-0.5) = 2; Vmax = 1/0.1 = 10.

Fig. 1 · Enzyme kinetics, drawn as you scroll

The inhibition table, in ten seconds of scrolling.

01

Michaelis-Menten

v = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S]). Velocity climbs, then flattens as every enzyme is saturated.

02

Read Km and Vmax

Km is the [S] at half of Vmax. Low Km means tight binding.

03

Competitive

Binds free enzyme at the active site. Apparent Km rises; Vmax unchanged. More substrate wins.

04

Noncompetitive

Binds E and ES equally. Vmax falls; Km unchanged.

05

Uncompetitive

Binds only ES. Km and Vmax both fall, so Lineweaver-Burk lines run parallel.

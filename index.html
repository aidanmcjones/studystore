Q140A person grows fonder of an unfamiliar song after hearing it several times. This is the:

C. Repeated exposure increasing liking is the mere exposure effect. The halo effect is about attractiveness, matching is about pairing by looks, and reciprocity is liking those who like us. From the 8C pack: Social Interactions and Relationships

Q141A person does not help at an accident scene because many others are watching and each assumes someone else will act. This is:

D. The belief that others will act spreads out personal responsibility. Pluralistic ignorance is misreading calm as no emergency. From the 8C pack: Social Interactions and Relationships

Q142A patient reports intense sadness during an interview but shows a blank face and monotone voice. Which statement is accurate?

C. Feeling sadness (cognitive) without expression (behavioral) means components can dissociate. The patient clearly has a cognitive component. Mood and emotion are different, and nothing shows that physiology is absent. From the 6C pack: Emotion and Stress

Q143A runner says she feels excited because she notices her heart pounding at the starting line. Which theory does this most directly illustrate?

D. Perceiving bodily change as the source of the feeling is James-Lange. Cannon-Bard has parallel output, Lazarus has appraisal first, and Yerkes-Dodson concerns arousal and performance. From the 6C pack: Emotion and Stress

Q144A patient with bilateral amygdala damage would most likely show

D. The amygdala is central to threat detection and fear. Declarative memory formation relies on the hippocampus, circadian rhythm on the hypothalamic suprachiasmatic nucleus, and facial movement on motor pathways. From the 6C pack: Emotion and Stress

Q145A patient has low cortisol and elevated ACTH. Where is the most likely problem?

D. Low cortisol with high ACTH means the pituitary is responding appropriately and the adrenal cortex fails to respond (primary adrenal insufficiency). A pituitary or hypothalamic failure would give low ACTH, and the medulla does not make cortisol. From the 6C pack: Emotion and Stress

Q146A town surveys all residents in a single year and finds that every age group reports lower trust in banks than in the previous survey, after a banking crisis. This is best explained as a

C. A change shared by all ages at one moment is a period effect. A cohort effect would differ by birth era. An age effect repeats at a stage of life in every cohort. A selection effect is a bias in who is sampled, which is not suggested. From the 9B pack: Demographics and Demographic Change

Q147A company hires only through referrals from current employees, who are mostly of one race, and the workforce stays uniform though no manager says anything biased. This is best described as

A. A neutral-looking policy that yields unequal results is institutional discrimination. Interpersonal racism by managers would need a person's bias or acts, and none is shown. Symbolic ethnicity is occasional identification with an ancestral culture. Amalgamation is the blending of groups into a new culture. From the 9B pack: Demographics and Demographic Change

Q148A man who identifies as straight sometimes has sex with men. Which concept best explains the mismatch?

C. Orientation, behavior, and identity are separate dimensions that need not agree. Orientation is not chosen day to day. Behavior alone does not set orientation. Identity is a label a person uses, not a biological trait. From the 9B pack: Demographics and Demographic Change

Q149In a country's pyramid, a deep notch appears at ages 80 to 84. What is the most likely cause?

D. A notch is a small cohort, often from a past event that cut births. A baby boom would show a bulge. Young immigrants would affect the middle. Women's longevity widens the female side but does not carve a notch. From the 9B pack: Demographics and Demographic Change

Q150Which is the best example of an intervening obstacle in Lee's model?

A. Fees and border barriers lie between origin and destination. A shortage of jobs is a push factor. Higher wages and relatives abroad are pull factors. From the 9B pack: Demographics and Demographic Change

Q151A stem reads: "Which of the following findings, if true, would most weaken the author's claim that tutoring raises exam scores?" What kind of question is this?

B. B is correct. The stem brings in a hypothetical finding to test the argument, which is the signature of new information questions. A (Misclassified question type): Main idea stems ask what the passage is about as a whole, with no hypothetical finding. C (Misclassified question type): Function stems ask why the author mentions something already in the passage. D (Misclassified question type): Tone stems ask about the author's attitude, not about outside evidence. From the CARS pack: CARS Strategy and Reasoning Skills

Q152In the sentence "Since wages rose, spending must have risen," the clause "wages rose" is:

C. C is correct. Since marks the following clause as support for the claim that spending rose. A (Reversal): The conclusion is that spending rose, which follows the comma. B (Distortion): Nothing is granted to an opponent. D (Distortion): No opposing view is presented. From the CARS pack: CARS Strategy and Reasoning Skills

Q153Evidence: every employee who attended the seminar later received a promotion. Conclusion: the seminar helps careers. Which is a required assumption?

D. D is correct. If another factor produced the promotions, the seminar's effect is not shown. Denying this assumption breaks the argument. A (Extreme language): The argument does not need such a broad claim. B (Extreme language): The argument needs only that promotions count as career success, not that they are the best measure. C (True but irrelevant): Enjoyment is not the link between attendance and promotion. From the CARS pack: CARS Strategy and Reasoning Skills

Reading the percentile table

The AAMC publishes one table that converts each scaled score to a percentile rank, the percentage of test takers in a reference group who scored at or below that score. The current table is built from every MCAT result in 2023, 2024 and 2025 combined and takes effect each May 1. Because the scores are bunched near the middle, a point near 500 moves your percentile much more than a point near 525.

The total score is the sum of four section scores, each from 118 to 132, so the lowest possible total is 472 and the highest is 528. When you enter a target percentile, the tool returns the lowest total score that reaches it and shows one balanced set of section scores that adds up to that total. It does not convert practice-test raw scores to scaled scores, because AAMC does not publish such a conversion.

Questions

Where do the percentile numbers come from?

From the AAMC table Summary of MCAT Total and Section Scores, Percentile Ranks in Effect May 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027, based on all MCAT results from 2023, 2024 and 2025. StudyStore transcribed the numbers and checked them against the source.

What does a percentile rank mean?

It is the percentage of scores equal to or less than a given score. For example, AAMC notes that 74 percent of total scores were at or below 508 in the reference group.

Can this convert my practice test raw score to a scaled score?

No. AAMC does not publish a raw-to-scaled conversion, so this tool does not estimate one.

How often is the table updated?

AAMC updates percentile ranks each May 1. This page states the date range its numbers are valid for and shows a warning once that range has passed.

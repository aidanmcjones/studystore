Ksp is the equilibrium constant for a slightly soluble salt dissolving. It tells you how much dissolves and whether mixing two solutions makes a precipitate. The common ion effect is Le Chatelier's principle applied to it.

Writing Ksp

For AmBn(s) ↔ mAn+ + nBm-, Ksp = [An+]m[Bm-]n. The solid is left out of the expression.

SaltDissolves toKsp in terms of molar solubility s
AgClAg+ + Cl-s2
CaF2Ca2+ + 2F-(s)(2s)2 = 4s3
Ag2CrO42Ag+ + CrO42-(2s)2(s) = 4s3

Only compare Ksp values directly for salts with the same ion ratio.

Q vs Ksp

ComparisonResult
Q < KspUnsaturated, more dissolves
Q = KspSaturated, at equilibrium
Q > KspSupersaturated, precipitate forms

Common ion effect

Adding an ion the salt already produces pushes the equilibrium toward the solid, so solubility falls. AgCl is less soluble in 0.1 M NaCl than in pure water. Acidic solutions raise the solubility of salts whose anion is a weak base, such as fluorides and carbonates, because H+ removes the anion.

Worked example

AgCl has Ksp = 1.8 x 10-10.

  • In pure water: s = √(1.8 x 10-10) = 1.3 x 10-5 M.
  • In 0.10 M NaCl: [Cl-] is about 0.10, so s = Ksp/0.10 = 1.8 x 10-9 M.
  • Solubility drops by a factor of about 7000.

Common traps

  • Including the solid in the Ksp expression.
  • Forgetting stoichiometric powers, and the factor in 2s, when solving for s.
  • Comparing Ksp across different ratios to rank solubility.
  • Using the pure water s when a common ion is present.

One-minute recap

Ksp = product of ion concentrations raised to their coefficients. Q > Ksp precipitates. A common ion lowers solubility. Acid raises solubility of salts with basic anions.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is the Ksp expression for CaF2?

Ksp = [Ca2+][F-]^2.

2. Q for a mixture is larger than Ksp. What happens?

A precipitate forms until Q equals Ksp.

3. How does adding NaCl change the solubility of AgCl?

It decreases, by the common ion effect.

4. A salt AB has Ksp = 4 x 10^-10. What is its molar solubility?

2 x 10^-5 M, the square root of Ksp.

5. Why does acid raise the solubility of CaCO3?

H+ reacts with carbonate, lowering its concentration and pulling the equilibrium toward dissolving.

Cannon-Bard: a stimulus triggers physiological arousal and the feeling of emotion at the same time, in parallel, through the thalamus. Schachter-Singer (two-factor): arousal comes first, then you label it using the situation, and the label decides the emotion.

Side by side

TheoryOrderKey idea
James-LangeStimulus, arousal, emotionEmotion is the perception of body changes
Cannon-BardStimulus, arousal and emotion togetherSame arousal can go with different emotions
Schachter-SingerStimulus, arousal, cognitive label, emotionContext interprets arousal

Example

You hear a loud noise at night. Cannon-Bard: heart pounding and fear start together. Schachter-Singer: heart pounding, you note you are alone in a dark house, you label it fear. In a crowded concert the same arousal is labelled excitement.

Related ideas

The Lazarus theory says appraisal comes before arousal. The facial feedback hypothesis says expression changes feeling.

Common traps

  • Calling Cannon-Bard sequential. It is simultaneous.
  • Forgetting the label step. Schachter-Singer requires a cognitive interpretation.
  • Mixing up James-Lange. There the body response comes before the feeling.

One-minute recap

  • Cannon-Bard: parallel. Schachter-Singer: arousal plus label.
  • James-Lange: body first.
  • Lazarus: appraisal first.

1. Which theory says arousal and emotion occur simultaneously?

Cannon-Bard.

2. What is the second factor in Schachter-Singer?

The cognitive label given to the arousal.

3. Which theory puts physiological change before the emotion with no label step?

James-Lange.

4. Which brain structure does Cannon-Bard emphasize?

The thalamus.

Lazarus.

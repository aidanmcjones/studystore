Oxidative phosphorylation turns NADH and FADH2 into ATP. Electrons pass down the chain in the inner mitochondrial membrane, protons are pumped into the intermembrane space, and ATP synthase lets them flow back to make ATP (chemiosmosis).

The chain

ComplexTakes electrons fromPumps H+
I (NADH dehydrogenase)NADHYes
II (succinate dehydrogenase)FADH2No
III (cytochrome bc1) via ubiquinoneI and IIYes
IV (cytochrome c oxidase) via cytochrome cIIIYes

O2 is the final acceptor, forming H2O. Complex IV needs O2, so no oxygen stops the whole chain.

P/O ratios

  • NADH: about 2.5 ATP (older texts: 3).
  • FADH2: about 1.5 ATP (older texts: 2). It enters at complex II and skips complex I.

Counting one glucose

StageDirect ATP/GTPNADHFADH2
Glycolysis (net)220
Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA (x2)020
Citric acid cycle (x2)262
Total4102

Using 2.5 and 1.5: 10 x 2.5 + 2 x 1.5 = 28 from the chain, plus 4 substrate-level (2 net from glycolysis plus 2 from the cycle) gives about 30. If the glycolysis NADH enters by the malate-aspartate shuttle, it keeps its full value and the total is 32. With the older ratios (3 and 2) the total is 36 to 38.

Shuttles

  • Glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle (muscle, brain): delivers electrons as FADH2, so cytosolic NADH is worth about 1.5 ATP.
  • Malate-aspartate shuttle (heart, liver): delivers NADH, worth about 2.5 ATP.

Uncouplers and inhibitors

  • 2,4-dinitrophenol and thermogenin (UCP1): let protons leak back, so the chain runs but little ATP is made and heat is released.
  • Cyanide, carbon monoxide: block complex IV.
  • Oligomycin: blocks ATP synthase; the gradient builds up and the chain slows.

Common traps

  • Giving FADH2 the same value as NADH. It skips complex I.
  • Forgetting to say which number the question wants. Use the ratio the question gives.
  • Saying the chain makes ATP directly. ATP synthase makes it from the proton gradient.

One-minute recap

  • NADH about 2.5 ATP, FADH2 about 1.5.
  • One glucose gives about 30 to 32 ATP (36 to 38 with older ratios).
  • Uncouplers dissipate the gradient and make heat.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Why does FADH2 yield less ATP than NADH?

It enters at complex II and bypasses complex I, so fewer protons are pumped.

2. What is the final electron acceptor?

O2, which becomes H2O at complex IV.

3. What does an uncoupler do?

It lets protons leak across the membrane, so O2 use continues but less ATP is made and heat is released.

4. Which shuttle gives cytosolic NADH full value?

The malate-aspartate shuttle.

5. What does oligomycin block?

ATP synthase.

kcat (turnover number) is how many substrate molecules one enzyme active site converts to product per second when saturated: kcat = Vmax / [E]total. Catalytic efficiency is kcat/Km, which combines speed (kcat) with how well the enzyme grabs substrate (low Km means high apparent affinity). Bigger is better for both.

Reading the pieces

TermMeaningWhere it comes from
VmaxMaximum rate at saturating substratePlateau of the curve; 1/y-intercept on Lineweaver-Burk
Km[S] at half VmaxLower Km means tighter apparent binding
kcatTurnover per active site per secondVmax divided by enzyme concentration
kcat/KmCatalytic efficiencyRate at low [S] per unit enzyme

At low substrate, v ≈ (kcat/Km)[E][S]. That is why kcat/Km is the number to compare when substrate is scarce, as it often is in cells. The theoretical ceiling is diffusion, roughly 108 to 109 M-1s-1, and enzymes near that limit (such as carbonic anhydrase) are called catalytically perfect.

Worked example 1: finding kcat

An assay with 2.0 nM enzyme reaches Vmax = 6.0 μM/s.

kcat = (6.0 × 10-6 M/s) / (2.0 × 10-9 M) = 3,000 s-1.

Worked example 2: comparing two enzymes

Enzymekcat (s-1)Km (mM)kcat/Km (M-1s-1)
A1000.52 × 105
B40085 × 104

B turns over four times faster when saturated, but A is four times more efficient at low substrate because its Km is 16 times lower. At [S] well above both Km values, B wins. At low [S], A wins.

Effect of inhibitors

  • Competitive: Km rises, Vmax and kcat unchanged, so apparent efficiency falls.
  • Noncompetitive: Vmax falls, Km unchanged.
  • Uncompetitive: both Vmax and Km fall.

Common traps

  • Equating low Km with fast. Km is about affinity, not speed.
  • Forgetting units. kcat is s-1, kcat/Km is M-1s-1.
  • Using total enzyme mass. kcat needs the concentration of active sites.
  • Treating Km as a true Kd. They match only when product release is slow relative to binding.

One-minute recap

kcat = Vmax/[E]total is turnover per active site. kcat/Km is catalytic efficiency, the best single number for comparing enzymes at low substrate, and diffusion caps it near 108 to 109 M-1s-1.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. How do you calculate kcat?

Divide Vmax by the total concentration of enzyme active sites.

2. An enzyme at 5 nM gives Vmax = 10 uM/s. What is kcat?

(10 x 10^-6) / (5 x 10^-9) = 2,000 per second.

3. Which quantity best compares two enzymes at low substrate concentration?

Catalytic efficiency, kcat/Km.

4. What are the units of kcat/Km?

M^-1 s^-1.

5. A competitive inhibitor changes which parameters?

It raises apparent Km and leaves Vmax (and kcat) unchanged.

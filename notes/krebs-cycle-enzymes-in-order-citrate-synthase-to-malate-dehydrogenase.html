Krebs Cycle Enzymes in Order, From Citrate Synthase to Malate Dehydrogenase

By StudyStore · Published 2026-11-09 · 1 min read · 189 words

The cycle runs citrate synthase, aconitase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, succinyl-CoA synthetase, succinate dehydrogenase, fumarase, malate dehydrogenase.

The eight steps

  1. Citrate synthase: acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate gives citrate.
  2. Aconitase: citrate to isocitrate (isomerization).
  3. Isocitrate dehydrogenase: isocitrate to alpha-ketoglutarate. Makes NADH and releases CO2.
  4. Alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase: to succinyl-CoA. Makes NADH and releases CO2.
  5. Succinyl-CoA synthetase: to succinate. Makes GTP (or ATP).
  6. Succinate dehydrogenase: to fumarate. Makes FADH2. Embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane (complex II).
  7. Fumarase: adds water, fumarate to malate.
  8. Malate dehydrogenase: malate to oxaloacetate. Makes NADH.

Per turn of the cycle

3 NADH, 1 FADH2, 1 GTP, 2 CO2. Per glucose, double it.

Regulated steps

Citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase. High NADH and ATP slow them; ADP and Ca2+ speed them.

Common traps

  • Putting FADH2 at the wrong step. Only succinate dehydrogenase.
  • Counting CO2 at the wrong steps. Only steps 3 and 4.
  • Forgetting the doubling per glucose.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Which enzyme makes FADH2 in the cycle?

Succinate dehydrogenase.

2. Which steps release CO2?

Isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.

3. Which step makes GTP?

Succinyl-CoA synthetase.

4. Which enzyme regenerates oxaloacetate?

Malate dehydrogenase.

5. How many NADH form per turn of the cycle?

3.

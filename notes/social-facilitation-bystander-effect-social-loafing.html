Several MCAT group-behavior terms sound alike. Sort them by what changes when other people are present.

The comparison

TermWhat happensMechanism
Social facilitationPerformance changes on a task in front of othersArousal strengthens the dominant response
Social loafingLess individual effort in a groupDiffused accountability when output is pooled
Bystander effectLess likely to help when more people are presentDiffusion of responsibility
DeindividuationLoss of self-awareness, norm breakingAnonymity in a crowd
Group polarizationGroup view becomes more extreme than the average starting viewPersuasive arguments and social comparison
GroupthinkPoor decisions from pressure for harmonyCohesion, isolation, strong leader

Social facilitation in detail

Others' presence raises arousal. Arousal improves easy or well-learned tasks (the dominant response is correct) and impairs hard or new tasks (the dominant response is wrong). This is the Yerkes-Dodson style pattern, applied to audiences.

Bystander effect

Darley and Latane studied it after the Kitty Genovese case. Larger groups lower the chance that any one person helps. Helping rises when the emergency is clear, a person is directly asked, or the helper feels competent.

Social loafing

It occurs when individual contributions cannot be identified. It shrinks when each person's work is tracked, the task is meaningful, or the group is small.

Common traps

  • Using facilitation for all audience effects. Hard tasks get worse.
  • Mixing loafing and bystander effect. Loafing is effort on a shared task; bystander is helping in an emergency.
  • Treating polarization as moving to the middle. It moves to the extreme.

One-minute recap

  • Facilitation: audience helps easy tasks, hurts hard ones.
  • Loafing and bystander effect: diffusion of responsibility.
  • Polarization: group pushes the shared view further out.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. When does an audience impair performance?

On difficult or unfamiliar tasks, via social facilitation.

2. What mechanism explains the bystander effect?

Diffusion of responsibility.

3. What reduces social loafing?

Making individual contributions identifiable.

4. What is group polarization?

A group's shared view becomes more extreme after discussion.

5. What does deindividuation involve?

Reduced self-awareness and norm breaking under anonymity.

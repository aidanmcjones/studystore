No, cAMP is not the inducer. The inducer of the lac operon is allolactose, an isomer of lactose that binds the lac repressor and removes it from the operator. cAMP is a different kind of signal: it binds CAP (catabolite activator protein) and helps RNA polymerase initiate transcription.

Two separate controls

SignalBindsEffectType of control
AllolactoseLacI repressorRepressor leaves the operatorNegative, induction
cAMPCAPCAP-cAMP binds near the promoter and boosts transcriptionPositive, activation

How glucose enters the story

When glucose is high, cAMP is low, so CAP is not active. When glucose is low, adenylate cyclase activity rises, cAMP is high, and CAP-cAMP binds DNA. This is catabolite repression: the cell prefers glucose, so it does not make strong lac operon output while glucose is available.

Four-condition table

LactoseGlucoseRepressorcAMP-CAPlac output
AbsentHighBoundInactiveOff
AbsentLowBoundActiveOff
PresentHighReleasedInactiveLow
PresentLowReleasedActiveHigh

You need both: the repressor off the operator and CAP-cAMP active for high output.

Common traps

  • Calling cAMP an inducer. Inducer means it inactivates a repressor. Allolactose does that.
  • Thinking glucose acts on the repressor. Glucose changes cAMP, which acts through CAP.
  • Forgetting that lactose alone gives only low output. High output also needs low glucose.

One-minute recap

Allolactose is the inducer and works on the repressor. cAMP is a coactivator signal that works through CAP and rises when glucose is low. Full expression needs lactose present and glucose low. See the full lac operon note for mutants and partial diploids.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is the inducer of the lac operon?

Allolactose, which binds the repressor and releases it from the operator.

2. What does cAMP bind, and what does it do?

It binds CAP, and CAP-cAMP binds near the promoter to boost transcription.

3. How does glucose affect cAMP levels?

High glucose lowers cAMP; low glucose raises it.

4. With lactose present and glucose high, is lac output high?

No, it is low, because CAP-cAMP is not active.

5. Why is the lac operon described as under both negative and positive control?

The repressor is negative control and CAP-cAMP is positive control.

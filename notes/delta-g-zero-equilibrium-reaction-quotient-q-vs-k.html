ΔG = 0 means the system is at equilibrium. There is no net driving force in either direction, the forward and reverse rates are equal, and the reaction quotient Q equals the equilibrium constant K.

The master equation

ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q

At equilibrium, ΔG = 0 and Q = K, so ΔG° = -RT ln K. That is the link between standard free energy and K.

Q vs K tells you the direction

Comparisonln(Q/K)ΔGNet direction
Q < KNegativeNegativeForward, toward products
Q = KZeroZeroNone, at equilibrium
Q > KPositivePositiveReverse, toward reactants

You can rewrite the master equation as ΔG = RT ln(Q/K). It makes the table easy to rebuild: if there are fewer products than equilibrium needs, Q is smaller than K and the reaction runs forward.

ΔG vs ΔG°

  • ΔG° is fixed for a reaction at given conditions and tells you K. It is the value at 1 M and standard state, not at equilibrium.
  • ΔG depends on the current mixture through Q and is the quantity that predicts the real direction.
  • A reaction with positive ΔG° (K less than 1) can still run forward if Q is small enough that ΔG is negative. This is how cells drive unfavorable steps by keeping product concentrations low.

Worked example

A reaction has K = 10 and Q = 0.1 at 298 K. Q is 100 times smaller than K, so ln(Q/K) = ln(0.01), about -4.6. With RT near 2.5 kJ/mol, ΔG is about -11.5 kJ/mol. It is negative, so the reaction proceeds forward until Q rises to 10.

Common traps

  • Saying a reaction with ΔG° = 0 is at equilibrium. That means K = 1, not that Q = K.
  • Using ΔG° to predict direction in a cell. Use ΔG with the real concentrations.
  • Linking ΔG = 0 to no reaction. The reaction continues in both directions at equal rates.

One-minute recap

ΔG = 0 means equilibrium and Q = K. If Q is below K, ΔG is negative and the reaction goes forward. If Q is above K, ΔG is positive and it goes in reverse. ΔG° sets K; ΔG sets the direction now. The broader picture is in the Gibbs free energy note.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What does delta G = 0 tell you?

The system is at equilibrium, with Q = K and equal forward and reverse rates.

2. If Q is less than K, what is the sign of delta G and the net direction?

Delta G is negative and the reaction runs forward.

3. What does delta G standard = 0 mean for K?

K = 1, since delta G standard = -RT ln K.

4. Can a reaction with positive delta G standard run forward in a cell?

Yes, if the real Q is low enough that delta G is negative.

5. Write delta G in terms of Q and K.

Delta G = RT ln(Q/K).

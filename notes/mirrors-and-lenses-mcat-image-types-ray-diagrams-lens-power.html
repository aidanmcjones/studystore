For mirrors and lenses, you can predict the image before you calculate anything. The object's position relative to the focal point and the center of curvature decides whether the image is real or virtual, upright or inverted, and enlarged or reduced. Convex lenses and concave mirrors are converging and can produce either kind of image. Concave lenses and convex mirrors are diverging and always produce a virtual, upright, reduced image.

Three rays that build any image

For a converging element with focal length f:

  1. A ray parallel to the axis passes through (or appears to come from) the focal point after the element.
  2. A ray through the focal point leaves parallel to the axis.
  3. A ray through the center of a lens continues straight. For a mirror, a ray through the center of curvature reflects straight back on itself.

Where the rays cross, the image forms. If they cross in front of a mirror or behind a lens, it is real and can be projected on a screen. If the rays only appear to cross when traced backward, it is virtual.

Converging element: object position table

The same table works for a concave mirror and a convex lens. The center of curvature is at 2f.

Object positionImageOrientationSize
Beyond 2fReal, between f and 2fInvertedReduced
At 2fReal, at 2fInvertedSame size
Between f and 2fReal, beyond 2fInvertedEnlarged
At fNo image (rays parallel)NoneNone
Inside fVirtual, same side as the objectUprightEnlarged

The magnifying glass uses the last row, and a camera or projector uses the first three.

Diverging element

A convex mirror or a concave lens has negative focal length. For every object position the image is virtual, upright and reduced, and it lies between the element and the focal point. This is why a convex mirror gives a wide field of view on a car's passenger side.

Quick sign rules

Use 1/f = 1/do + 1/di and m = -di/do, where positive di means a real image and negative m means inverted. The full convention is in the <a href="thin-lens-mirror-equation-sign-conventions">thin lens and mirror equation note</a>. Remember that a real image on a lens is on the opposite side from the object, while for a mirror it is on the same side.

Lens power

Optometrists describe lenses by power, the inverse of focal length in meters:

P = 1/f, measured in diopters (D)

A convex lens with f = 0.25 m has P = +4 D. A concave lens with f = -0.50 m has P = -2 D. When thin lenses are placed in contact, their powers add: Ptotal = P1 + P2. Nearsightedness is corrected with a negative (diverging) lens, and farsightedness with a positive (converging) lens.

Two-lens systems

Treat the image from the first lens as the object for the second:

  1. Find di1 using the first lens.
  2. Set do2 = (separation) - di1. If the first image lies beyond the second lens, do2 is negative.
  3. Solve for di2.
  4. The total magnification is m1 × m2.

Worked example 1: concave mirror

A concave mirror has f = 10 cm and an object sits 30 cm away. Then 1/di = 1/10 - 1/30 = 2/30, so di = 15 cm. It is positive, so the image is real, and m = -15/30 = -0.5, so it is inverted and half size. This matches the first row of the table, since 30 cm is beyond 2f = 20 cm.

Worked example 2: magnifying glass

A convex lens has f = 5 cm, and an object is 3 cm away. Then 1/di = 1/5 - 1/3 = -2/15, so di = -7.5 cm. The negative sign means a virtual image on the object's side, and m = -(-7.5)/3 = +2.5, so it is upright and enlarged 2.5 times.

Worked example 3: diverging lens

A concave lens with f = -20 cm has an object 40 cm away. Then 1/di = -1/20 - 1/40 = -3/40, so di = -13.3 cm. The image is virtual, with m = +0.33, so it is upright and reduced to one third.

Common traps

  • Using f > 0 for every lens. Concave lenses and convex mirrors have negative f.
  • Mixing "real" with "inverted." A real image from a converging element is inverted, but a virtual image is upright. Check the sign of di and of m separately.
  • Expecting an image at the focal point. Rays emerge parallel, so no image forms.
  • Forgetting the lens power unit. Convert centimeters to meters before using P = 1/f.
  • Placing the real image on the wrong side. For lenses it is on the far side, and for mirrors it is on the near side.

One-minute recap

Beyond f a converging element makes a real, inverted image, enlarged if the object is between f and 2f and reduced if it is beyond 2f. Inside f it makes a virtual, upright, enlarged one. Diverging elements always give virtual, upright, reduced images. Power is 1/f in diopters and adds for lenses in contact. For two lenses, the first image is the second object, and magnifications multiply.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. An object is placed 30 cm from a converging lens with f = 10 cm. Describe the image.

1/di = 1/10 - 1/30, so di = 15 cm. It is real, inverted and reduced (m = -0.5).

2. What kind of image does a convex mirror always form?

A virtual, upright, reduced image, for any object position.

3. Where must an object be placed relative to a converging lens to act as a magnifying glass?

Inside the focal length, which gives a virtual, upright, enlarged image.

4. What is the power of a lens with f = -0.25 m?

P = 1/f = -4 diopters, a diverging lens.

5. Two thin lenses of +2 D and +3 D are in contact. What is their combined focal length?

Powers add to +5 D, so f = 1/5 = 0.20 m, or 20 cm.

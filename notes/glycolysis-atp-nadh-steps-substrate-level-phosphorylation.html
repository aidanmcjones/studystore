Glycolysis has only four ATP or NADH events. Two steps spend ATP, one step makes NADH, and two steps make ATP, and the last three happen twice per glucose because the sugar splits into two three-carbon pieces.

The four steps to memorize

StepEnzymeEvent
1. Glucose to glucose-6-phosphateHexokinase (glucokinase in liver)Uses 1 ATP
3. Fructose-6-phosphate to fructose-1,6-bisphosphatePFK-1Uses 1 ATP
6. G3P to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerateGAPDHMakes 1 NADH (x2)
7. 1,3-BPG to 3-phosphoglyceratePhosphoglycerate kinaseMakes 1 ATP (x2)
10. Phosphoenolpyruvate to pyruvatePyruvate kinaseMakes 1 ATP (x2)

What substrate-level phosphorylation means

The ATP made at steps 7 and 10 comes from transferring a phosphate directly from a high-energy substrate to ADP. No electron transport chain is involved. Oxidative phosphorylation is different: it uses the proton gradient made by the NADH and FADH2 that the electron transport chain reoxidizes.

Net yield per glucose

Spent: 2 ATP. Made: 4 ATP. Net: 2 ATP and 2 NADH, plus 2 pyruvate.

Common traps

  • Counting 4 ATP as the net. Subtract the 2 spent in the investment phase.
  • Forgetting to double the later steps. One glucose gives two G3P.
  • Saying NADH is made at the ATP steps. NADH forms at GAPDH only.

One-minute recap

  • ATP used: hexokinase and PFK-1.
  • NADH made: GAPDH. ATP made: PGK and pyruvate kinase.
  • Net per glucose: 2 ATP, 2 NADH, 2 pyruvate.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Which two glycolysis enzymes use ATP?

Hexokinase and PFK-1.

2. Which enzyme produces NADH in glycolysis?

Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH).

3. Which two enzymes make ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation?

Phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase.

4. What is the net ATP per glucose?

2 ATP (4 made minus 2 spent).

5. Why are the later steps counted twice?

One glucose splits into two three-carbon molecules of G3P.

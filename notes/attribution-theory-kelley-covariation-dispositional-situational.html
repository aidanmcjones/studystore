Attribution Theory: Dispositional vs Situational and Kelley's Covariation Model

By StudyStore · Published 2026-11-04 · 1 min read · 271 words

Attribution theory is how people explain behavior. A dispositional (internal) attribution blames traits, motives or character. A situational (external) attribution blames circumstances.

Kelley's covariation model

Kelley proposed that we judge cause by three kinds of information:

FactorQuestionHigh means
ConsensusDo other people act this way?Points to the situation
DistinctivenessDoes this person act this way only in this case?Points to the situation
ConsistencyDoes this person act this way every time?Points to the person (when others do not)

Quick patterns:

  • Low consensus, low distinctiveness, high consistency: dispositional.
  • High consensus, high distinctiveness, high consistency: situational (the stimulus).
  • Low consistency: a one-off circumstance.

Example: Sam laughs at a comedian. If everyone laughs (high consensus), Sam laughs at no other comedian (high distinctiveness) and always laughs at this one (high consistency), the cause is the comedian.

Biases in attribution

  • Fundamental attribution error: overweighting disposition when explaining other people.
  • Actor-observer bias: explain your own behavior situationally, others' dispositionally.
  • Self-serving bias: credit success to yourself, blame failures on the situation.
  • Just-world hypothesis: assume people get what they deserve, so victims are blamed.

The fundamental attribution error is weaker in collectivist cultures than in individualist ones.

Common traps

  • Treating high consistency alone as dispositional. Combine all three factors.
  • Applying self-serving bias to others. It concerns your own outcomes.
  • Mixing up consensus and consistency. Consensus is across people; consistency is across time.

One-minute recap

  • Internal = traits; external = situation.
  • Kelley: consensus, distinctiveness, consistency.
  • Biases skew who gets blamed.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is a dispositional attribution?

Explaining behavior by internal traits or character.

2. Which Kelley factor compares a person with others?

Consensus.

3. What pattern indicates a dispositional cause?

Low consensus, low distinctiveness and high consistency.

4. What is the fundamental attribution error?

Overweighting personality and underweighting the situation for others' behavior.

5. Which attribution bias protects self-esteem after failure?

Self-serving bias.

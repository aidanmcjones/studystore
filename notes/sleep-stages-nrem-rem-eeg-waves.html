Sleep moves through four stages in a cycle: N1, N2, N3 (all non-REM) and REM. The MCAT mostly asks you to match a stage to its EEG wave or a behavior.

Stages and waves

StateEEG patternKey features
Awake, alertBeta (fast, low amplitude)Active thinking
Awake, relaxed, eyes closedAlphaDrowsy calm
N1ThetaLight sleep, hypnagogic imagery, hypnic jerks
N2Theta with sleep spindles and K complexesLargest share of the night, sleep spindles bursts
N3Delta (slow, high amplitude)Slow-wave sleep, hardest to wake, sleepwalking and night terrors
REMFast, desynchronized, resembles wakingVivid dreams, rapid eye movements, muscle atonia

The cycle

A full cycle lasts about 90 minutes and repeats four to six times a night. Early cycles have more N3. Later cycles have longer REM periods, so cutting sleep short removes disproportionately more REM.

REM details

  • Brain activity looks awake, but skeletal muscles are largely paralyzed, so it is sometimes called paradoxical sleep.
  • Heart rate and breathing become irregular.
  • Dreaming is most vivid, though dreams occur in other stages too.

Common traps

  • Calling delta waves a feature of REM. Delta belongs to N3.
  • Forgetting that REM EEG looks like waking.
  • Placing sleep spindles in N3. They mark N2.

One-minute recap

  • Awake: beta; relaxed: alpha.
  • N1 theta, N2 spindles and K complexes, N3 delta.
  • REM: fast EEG, atonia, vivid dreams, more at the end of the night.

2. Which stage contains sleep spindles and K complexes?

3. Why is REM called paradoxical sleep?

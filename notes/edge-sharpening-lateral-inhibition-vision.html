Edge sharpening is the exaggeration of contrast at a boundary between light and dark. It results from lateral inhibition: an active neuron reduces the activity of its neighbors.

The mechanism

  • Photoreceptors signal to bipolar and ganglion cells.
  • Horizontal cells in the retina spread inhibition sideways to neighboring photoreceptor-bipolar connections.
  • This produces center-surround receptive fields. An ON-center ganglion cell fires most when light hits the center and the surround is dark. An OFF-center cell does the reverse.

What happens at an edge

A cell just inside the bright side of an edge receives little inhibition from its darker neighbors, so it responds strongly. A cell just inside the dark side receives strong inhibition from bright neighbors, so it responds weakly. The result is a perceived bright band and dark band at the boundary (Mach bands), which makes the edge look sharper than the physical difference.

Beyond the retina

Hubel and Wiesel found that neurons in primary visual cortex respond to specific features. Simple cells prefer bars or edges at a particular orientation and location. This is a bottom-up, feature-detection step in perception.

Common traps

  • Calling lateral inhibition an effect of the brain's expectations. It is a bottom-up mechanism.
  • Placing it in the cortex only. It begins in the retina.
  • Confusing ON-center and OFF-center. Check which part of the field light must hit.

One-minute recap

  • Lateral inhibition: active cells suppress neighbors.
  • Result: center-surround fields and sharper edges.
  • Cortical feature detectors build on this.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is lateral inhibition?

An active neuron reducing the activity of its neighbors.

2. Which retinal cells spread inhibition sideways?

Horizontal cells.

3. When does an ON-center ganglion cell fire most?

When light falls on the center of its field and the surround is dark.

4. What perceptual effect does lateral inhibition produce at a boundary?

Exaggerated contrast, seen as Mach bands.

5. What did Hubel and Wiesel find in visual cortex?

Neurons that respond to specific features such as oriented edges.

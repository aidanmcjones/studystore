When a weak acid (HA) is titrated with a strong base, the curve has four landmarks. The most tested one is the half-equivalence point, where pH = pKa.

The four regions

PointWhat is in the flaskpH
StartOnly HAWeak acid pH, from Ka
Buffer regionHA and A-Henderson-Hasselbalch
Half-equivalence[HA] = [A-]pH = pKa
EquivalenceOnly A- (a weak base)Above 7
Past equivalenceExcess OH-Set by excess strong base

Why pH = pKa at the half point

Half of the HA has been converted to A-, so the ratio [A-]/[HA] is 1. In pH = pKa + log([A-]/[HA]), log(1) = 0.

This is also where the curve is flattest, so the solution resists pH change best (maximum buffer capacity).

Why equivalence is above 7

At equivalence all the acid is now its conjugate base. A- accepts protons from water, making OH-. So the equivalence point of a weak acid with a strong base is basic. For a weak base with a strong acid it is acidic. Strong with strong gives pH 7.

Using the curve

  • Read the equivalence volume from the steepest part of the jump.
  • Half that volume gives the half-equivalence point; the pH there is the pKa.
  • Moles of acid = moles of base added at equivalence (for a monoprotic acid).
  • A polyprotic acid shows one half-equivalence point and one equivalence point per proton.

Common traps

  • Assuming equivalence is at pH 7. That holds only for strong acid with strong base.
  • Using the equivalence volume for pKa. Use half of it.
  • Calling the start a buffer. Before any base is added there is only HA.

One-minute recap

  • Buffer region lies between start and equivalence.
  • Half-equivalence: pH = pKa.
  • Equivalence: only conjugate base, pH above 7.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is the pH at the half-equivalence point?

It equals the pKa of the weak acid.

2. Why is the equivalence point above 7 for a weak acid and strong base?

The conjugate base A- reacts with water to make OH-.

3. What is the ratio [A-]/[HA] at the half-equivalence point?

One.

4. Where is the buffer capacity greatest?

Near the half-equivalence point, where pH = pKa.

5. Which titration has an equivalence point at pH 7?

Strong acid with strong base.

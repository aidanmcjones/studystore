Fed vs Fasted State: Insulin, Glucagon and Metabolic Switches

By StudyStore · Published 2026-11-04 · 1 min read · 262 words

After a meal, insulin dominates and the body stores fuel. During fasting, glucagon (with epinephrine and cortisol) dominates and the body releases it.

Hormone comparison

Insulin (fed)Glucagon (fasting)
SourcePancreatic beta cellsPancreatic alpha cells
Blood glucoseLowersRaises
Liver glycogenSynthesis (glycogen synthase)Breakdown (glycogen phosphorylase)
Liver glucoseGlycolysis, fatty acid synthesisGluconeogenesis
AdiposeStores triglycerides (lipoprotein lipase)Lipolysis (hormone-sensitive lipase)
SignalingReceptor tyrosine kinaseG protein, cAMP, PKA

Insulin promotes dephosphorylation of key enzymes (active glycogen synthase). Glucagon promotes phosphorylation (active glycogen phosphorylase).

Time course of fasting

StageGlucose sourceMain fuel for other tissues
Fed (0 to 4 h)DietGlucose
Early fasting (4 to 16 h)Liver glycogenolysisGlucose; fatty acids in muscle
Fasting (about 1 to 3 days)Gluconeogenesis (lactate, glycerol, amino acids)Fatty acids; brain still uses glucose
Prolonged starvationGluconeogenesis, less protein breakdownKetone bodies used by brain

Organ roles

  • Liver: the glucose supplier; the only organ that releases free glucose (it has glucose-6-phosphatase) besides kidney.
  • Muscle: keeps its own glycogen; lacks glucose-6-phosphatase, so it cannot export glucose. Releases lactate and alanine.
  • Adipose: releases fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Brain: glucose first, ketones in long fasts. It cannot use fatty acids directly.
  • Red blood cells: always glucose (no mitochondria).

Common traps

  • Letting muscle glycogen raise blood glucose. It cannot.
  • Saying the brain burns fatty acids. It uses ketones.
  • Mixing up the cell types. Beta makes insulin; alpha makes glucagon.

One-minute recap

  • Fed: insulin, store. Fasted: glucagon, release.
  • Glycogen first, then gluconeogenesis, then ketones.
  • Only liver (and kidney) export glucose.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Which hormone activates glycogen phosphorylase in the liver?

Glucagon, through cAMP and PKA.

2. Which tissue cannot release glucose from glycogen into blood?

Muscle, which lacks glucose-6-phosphatase.

3. What is the main glucose source in the first hours of fasting?

Liver glycogenolysis.

4. What fuel does the brain use in prolonged starvation?

Ketone bodies, along with some glucose.

5. Which cells secrete insulin?

Pancreatic beta cells.

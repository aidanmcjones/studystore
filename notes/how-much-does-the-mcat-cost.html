Short answer: The cost of the MCAT is the AAMC exam fee plus whatever you add around it: change fees if your date moves, an international fee if you test abroad, and study materials. The exam fee is set by the AAMC and is public; the study-materials part is your choice and varies widely. This page lists every category so you can see where the money goes. For the current exact figures, use the AAMC fee page and the free MCAT cost calculator, which adds them up for your situation. This is general information, not financial advice.

What the AAMC controls

The registration fee, the international fee and the change fees are all published by the AAMC and apply to every test taker on the same date. Because fees are updated between testing years, always read the AAMC page for the year you are booking rather than relying on a number from a forum post or an older article. Our 2027 registration note walks through the 2027 fee tables and the three change windows.

The window you change in matters more than most people expect. Moving your date early is the cheap option; moving it close to the exam costs more and, past a point, the AAMC allows no changes at all. Booking a date with a realistic backup behind it is the simplest way to keep this line item small.

Fee assistance

The AAMC Fee Assistance Program offers a reduced registration fee and other benefits to people who qualify on financial grounds. Eligibility is decided by the AAMC through an application, so read the program's own page and apply early: approval takes time, and you want the decision before you register.

What you choose: study materials

Everything outside the AAMC's own fees is a decision, not a requirement. Students spend very different amounts here: some use only free and library resources, others buy several books, a question bank and a prep course. Two ways to keep it proportionate:

  • Match spend to your weak spots. Take a diagnostic, find the sections that cost you points, and buy for those.
  • Prefer practice you can review. Questions are only worth the money if you read why each wrong answer is wrong. See how to review hard MCAT questions.

Free options exist too: this site publishes free notes and a free sampler of practice questions so you can test a format before paying for anything.

Time is a cost too

A longer study period lowers the risk of a retake, which is its own expense, but it also delays your application timeline. How long you need depends on your starting point; see how long to study for the MCAT and a weekly study plan.

A simple way to build your budget

  1. Open the MCAT cost calculator and enter your situation.
  2. Check the AAMC fee page for your test year to confirm the fees.
  3. Write down your 60-day and 30-day dates the day you register, since those decide what a date change would cost.
  4. List the study materials you already own before buying more.

One-minute recap

MCAT cost has three parts: AAMC fees (public, check the AAMC page for your year), possible change or international fees, and study materials (your choice). Reduced fees exist through the Fee Assistance Program. Use the calculator for a total and the AAMC for the official figures. Choose a date you can keep, and spend on materials where your diagnostic shows weak points.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Who sets the MCAT registration fee?

The AAMC. It is published on the AAMC's MCAT scheduling fees page.

2. Name two costs besides the registration fee.

Any two of: reschedule or cancellation fees, the international fee, study materials, travel, a retake fee.

3. Why is the reschedule window worth planning around?

Fees rise as the exam gets closer, and past a cutoff the AAMC allows no changes at all.

4. Is it safe to rely on a fee number from an older article?

No. Fees are updated between testing years, so check the AAMC page for the year you are booking.

5. Which MCAT cost is your own choice?

Study materials: books, question banks, flashcards and practice exams vary by student.

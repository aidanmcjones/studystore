The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC) links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle. It runs in the mitochondrial matrix and converts pyruvate (3 carbons) to acetyl-CoA (2 carbons).

Net reaction

Pyruvate + CoA + NAD+ gives acetyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH.

Per pyruvate: one CO2 lost, one NADH made. Per glucose (two pyruvate): 2 CO2, 2 NADH, 2 acetyl-CoA.

Cofactors

Five are used: thiamine pyrophosphate (B1), lipoic acid, CoA (B5, pantothenate), FAD (B2) and NAD+ (B3). A shortage of B1 stalls the complex, so pyruvate and lactate build up.

Regulation

EffectSignal
ActivatedPyruvate, ADP, NAD+, CoA, insulin (via phosphatase)
InhibitedAcetyl-CoA, NADH, ATP
CovalentPDH kinase phosphorylates and inactivates; PDH phosphatase activates

Products inhibit the complex, which makes sense: plenty of acetyl-CoA and NADH means the cell has fuel and reducing power.

Irreversible

The reaction has a large negative delta G, and animals cannot make glucose from acetyl-CoA. Fatty acids, which break down to acetyl-CoA, therefore cannot become net glucose.

Arsenic and PDC

Arsenic binds lipoic acid and inhibits the complex. Expect a clinical vignette that links it to high blood pyruvate.

Common traps

  • Counting CO2 per glucose as one. It is two, because there are two pyruvate.
  • Placing PDC in the cytosol. It is in the matrix.
  • Saying ATP is made directly. PDC makes NADH, which pays off later in the electron transport chain.

One-minute recap

  • Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, CO2 and NADH, in the matrix.
  • Five cofactors; B1 deficiency stalls it.
  • Inhibited by acetyl-CoA, NADH and ATP; irreversible.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Where does the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex work?

The mitochondrial matrix.

2. What are its products per pyruvate?

Acetyl-CoA, CO2 and NADH.

3. Name three cofactors.

Any three of thiamine pyrophosphate, lipoic acid, CoA, FAD, NAD+.

4. What inhibits the complex?

Acetyl-CoA, NADH and ATP, plus phosphorylation by PDH kinase.

5. Can fatty acids make net glucose?

No. The PDC step is irreversible, so acetyl-CoA cannot return to pyruvate.

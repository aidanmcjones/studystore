Projectile motion is two separate problems joined by time. Horizontal motion has constant velocity (no air resistance). Vertical motion has constant acceleration of g = 9.8 m/s2 downward (use 10 on the MCAT).

Components

For launch speed v at angle θ above horizontal: vx = v cos θ and vy = v sin θ.

Formulas (launch and landing at the same height)

QuantityFormula
Time to peakv sin θ / g
Time of flight2 v sin θ / g
Max height(v sin θ)2 / 2g
Rangev2 sin 2θ / g

At the peak, vy = 0 but vx is unchanged, so the speed is not zero.

Worked example

A ball is launched at 20 m/s and 30 degrees, with g = 10. vy = 10 m/s, vx = 17.3 m/s. Time of flight = 2(10)/10 = 2 s. Max height = 102/20 = 5 m. Range = 17.3 x 2 = 34.6 m.

Angles

Range is maximum at 45 degrees. Complementary angles (such as 30 and 60) give the same range. Higher angles give more height and more time.

Common traps

  • Setting total velocity to zero at the peak. Only vy is zero.
  • Giving the horizontal part an acceleration. It has none.
  • Using the range formula for different launch and landing heights. Solve the vertical part for time instead.

One-minute recap

  • x: constant velocity. y: acceleration g.
  • Peak: vy = 0, vx constant.
  • Max range at 45 degrees; complementary angles tie.

Pick the mechanism by three questions: how substituted is the carbon, is the reagent a strong nucleophile or strong base, and what is the solvent.

Decision rule

  1. Tertiary halide with strong base: E2. Tertiary with weak nucleophile: SN1 and E1.
  2. Methyl or primary with a good nucleophile: SN2.
  3. Secondary: strong nucleophile gives SN2; strong bulky base gives E2.

Notes

Heat favors elimination. Carbocations can rearrange in SN1 and E1. A good leaving group (weak base such as I-, Br-, tosylate) is needed in all four.

Common traps

  • SN2 at a tertiary carbon. Steric hindrance blocks backside attack.
  • Expecting retention in SN2. It is inversion.
  • Forgetting rearrangements in SN1 and E1.

One-minute recap

  • SN2: one step, inversion, unhindered carbon.
  • SN1/E1: carbocation, first order.
  • E2: strong base, anti-periplanar.

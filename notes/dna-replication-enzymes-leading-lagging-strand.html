DNA replication is semiconservative (each new double helix keeps one old strand) and bidirectional from each origin. DNA polymerase only adds nucleotides to a 3' end, so it builds 5' to 3'. That one rule explains why one strand is continuous and the other is made in pieces.

Enzymes in order

StepEnzyme or proteinJob
1HelicaseUnwinds the helix at the origin, using ATP
2Single-strand binding proteinsKeep separated strands from re-annealing
3Topoisomerase (gyrase in bacteria)Relieves supercoiling ahead of the fork
4PrimaseMakes a short RNA primer, giving polymerase a 3' end
5DNA polymerase III (bacteria)Extends the primer 5' to 3'
6DNA polymerase I (bacteria)Removes RNA primers, fills the gap with DNA
7DNA ligaseSeals the remaining nick between fragments

Leading vs lagging strand

  • Leading strand: template runs so the polymerase follows the fork. Continuous synthesis, one primer.
  • Lagging strand: template runs the other way, so synthesis goes away from the fork. It is made as Okazaki fragments, each needing its own primer, joined later by ligase.

Proofreading

DNA polymerase has 3' to 5' exonuclease activity. It backs up and removes a mismatched base before continuing. Mismatch repair fixes errors it misses.

Prokaryote vs eukaryote

ProkaryoteEukaryote
OriginsOne (oriC)Many
Main polymerasesIII (replication), I (primers)Delta and epsilon (replication), alpha with primase
Okazaki fragmentsLongerShorter
EndsCircular chromosome, no end problemLinear, telomerase extends telomeres

Common traps

  • Saying polymerase builds 3' to 5'. It reads the template 3' to 5' and builds 5' to 3'.
  • Forgetting the primer. DNA polymerase cannot start from nothing.
  • Mixing up polymerases I and III. III does most synthesis; I replaces primers.

One-minute recap

  • Helicase unwinds, primase primes, polymerase extends, ligase seals.
  • Leading is continuous; lagging is Okazaki fragments.
  • Polymerase proofreads 3' to 5'.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Which enzyme unwinds the DNA helix at the fork?

Helicase.

2. Why is the lagging strand made in fragments?

Polymerase only builds 5' to 3', so that strand is synthesized away from the fork in short pieces.

3. What does primase make?

A short RNA primer that gives polymerase a 3' end.

4. Which enzyme seals the nick between Okazaki fragments?

DNA ligase.

5. What enzyme extends telomeres in eukaryotes?

Telomerase.

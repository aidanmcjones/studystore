A concave mirror curves inward like a bowl and has a positive focal length. A convex mirror bulges outward and has a negative focal length. The focal length is half the radius of curvature: f = r/2.

The equations

  • Mirror equation: 1/f = 1/o + 1/i
  • Magnification: m = -i/o = hi/ho

Sign conventions (mirrors)

QuantityPositiveNegative
f, rConcave (center of curvature in front)Convex (behind the mirror)
oObject in front (almost always)Rare
iReal image, in front of the mirrorVirtual image, behind the mirror
mUprightInverted

Real images form where light actually converges, so they can be projected on a screen.

Concave mirror images

Object positionImage
Beyond C (o > 2f)Real, inverted, smaller
At CReal, inverted, same size
Between C and FReal, inverted, larger
At FNo image (rays parallel)
Inside F (o < f)Virtual, upright, larger

Convex mirror images

Always virtual, upright and smaller, wherever the object sits. That is why they are used for wide-angle views such as car side mirrors.

Worked example

A concave mirror has f = +10 cm and an object at 30 cm. 1/i = 1/10 - 1/30 = 2/30, so i = +15 cm (real). m = -15/30 = -0.5: inverted and half size.

Common traps

  • Mixing lens and mirror conventions. For lenses a positive image distance is on the far side; for mirrors a positive image distance is in front.
  • Forgetting the minus in m = -i/o.
  • Thinking a plane mirror has f = 0. Its focal length is infinite.

One-minute recap

  • Concave: f positive. Convex: f negative.
  • Positive i means real; negative i means virtual.
  • Convex mirrors always give small, upright, virtual images.

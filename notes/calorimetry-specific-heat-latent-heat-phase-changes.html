Calorimetry measures heat by watching temperature change. Two equations cover almost every question: one for heating a substance, one for changing its phase. Add conservation of energy and you can solve any mixing problem.

Core formulas

SituationFormula
Temperature changeq = mcΔT
Phase change (T constant)q = mL
Heat lost = heat gainedqlost = qgained (isolated system)
Water specific heatc = 4.18 J/(g K) = 1 cal/(g K)

c is specific heat and L is latent heat of fusion or vaporization. Heat flows from the hotter object to the colder one until temperatures match.

Heating curve

On a plot of temperature against heat added, slopes are single phases and flat sections are phase changes. On a slope use mcΔT. On a flat section the temperature does not change because the heat goes into breaking intermolecular attractions, so use mL. The vaporization plateau is much longer than the fusion plateau because Lv is larger than Lf.

Worked example

Find the final temperature when 100 g of water at 80 C is mixed with 100 g of water at 20 C.

  • Equal masses and equal c, so the final temperature is the average, 50 C.
  • For unequal masses: m1c(80 - T) = m2c(T - 20), and c cancels.
  • Heat needed to melt 10 g of ice at 0 C (Lf = 334 J/g): q = 10 x 334 = 3340 J, with no temperature rise.

Common traps

  • Using mcΔT during a phase change. The temperature is flat, so use mL.
  • Mixing signs. Set heat lost equal to heat gained with positive magnitudes.
  • Mixing units of J and cal, or K and C. A change of 1 K equals a change of 1 C.
  • Thinking high specific heat means heating fast. It means slow temperature change.

One-minute recap

q = mcΔT on slopes, q = mL on plateaus. Heat lost equals heat gained. Water has a high specific heat, so it resists temperature change. Vaporization needs more heat than fusion.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. Which equation applies while ice melts at 0 C?

q = mL, since the temperature stays constant during the phase change.

2. Two equal masses of water at 90 C and 10 C are mixed. What is the final temperature?

50 C.

3. Which has more latent heat for water, fusion or vaporization?

Vaporization.

4. A metal has a lower specific heat than water. Equal heat is added to equal masses. Which warms more?

The metal.

5. Is a temperature change of 5 K larger, smaller or equal to 5 C?

Equal.

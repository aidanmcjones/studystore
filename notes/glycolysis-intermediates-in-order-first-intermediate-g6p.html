The first intermediate in glycolysis is glucose-6-phosphate, made when hexokinase (or glucokinase in liver) phosphorylates glucose and traps it in the cell.

Intermediates in order

StepEnzymeProductATP or NADH
1Hexokinase / glucokinaseGlucose-6-phosphateUses 1 ATP
2Phosphoglucose isomeraseFructose-6-phosphate
3PFK-1Fructose-1,6-bisphosphateUses 1 ATP
4AldolaseDHAP + glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate
5Triose phosphate isomeraseInterconverts DHAP and G3P
6GAPDH1,3-bisphosphoglycerateMakes 1 NADH
7Phosphoglycerate kinase3-phosphoglycerateMakes 1 ATP
8Phosphoglycerate mutase2-phosphoglycerate
9EnolasePhosphoenolpyruvate
10Pyruvate kinasePyruvateMakes 1 ATP

Steps 6 to 10 run twice per glucose because step 4 yields two three-carbon pieces.

Which steps produce ATP

Steps 7 and 10 make ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation. That is 2 ATP per triose, so 4 ATP per glucose. Steps 1 and 3 spend 2 ATP, so the net is 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose.

Irreversible steps

Steps 1, 3 and 10 are the large negative delta G steps and the main control points.

Common traps

  • Counting 4 ATP as the net. Subtract the 2 invested.
  • Forgetting that steps 6 to 10 double.
  • Placing the NADH step with ATP use. NADH is made at GAPDH.

One-minute recap

  • First intermediate: glucose-6-phosphate.
  • ATP spent at steps 1 and 3; made at steps 7 and 10.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is the first intermediate in glycolysis?

Glucose-6-phosphate.

2. Which two steps consume ATP?

Hexokinase (step 1) and PFK-1 (step 3).

3. Which two steps make ATP?

Phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase.

4. Which enzyme produces NADH?

Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

5. What is the net ATP per glucose?

2 ATP.

A positive delta G means the reaction is nonspontaneous as written: it is endergonic, and it will not proceed forward on its own under those conditions. A negative delta G is spontaneous (exergonic). Zero is equilibrium.

What it does and does not mean

  • It means the forward reaction needs an input of free energy.
  • It does not mean the reaction cannot happen. Coupling, concentration changes and temperature can all change the outcome.
  • It does not mean slow. Delta G says direction, not rate. Rate depends on the activation energy and on enzymes.

Delta G vs delta G naught

SymbolConditionsUse
ΔG°Standard state, all 1 MCompare reactions, find K
ΔGActual concentrationsDecide the real direction

ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q. A reaction with a positive ΔG° can have a negative ΔG if Q is small enough, meaning products are kept low. This is how many metabolic steps run forward in cells. Also ΔG° = -RT ln K, so a positive ΔG° means K is below 1.

Coupling to ATP hydrolysis

ATP hydrolysis has a ΔG° of about -30 kJ/mol (-7.3 kcal/mol). Free energies of coupled reactions add:

  • Glucose + Pi → glucose-6-phosphate: ΔG° about +14 kJ/mol
  • ATP → ADP + Pi: ΔG° about -30 kJ/mol
  • Net (hexokinase): about -16 kJ/mol, spontaneous

The two reactions must share an intermediate, usually a phosphorylated enzyme or substrate. This is what hexokinase does in the first step of glycolysis.

When a positive delta G can flip with temperature

Use ΔG = ΔH - TΔS. If ΔH is positive and ΔS is positive, ΔG is positive at low T and negative at high T (ice melting above 0 degrees C). If ΔH is positive and ΔS is negative, ΔG is positive at every temperature. The full sign table is in the Gibbs free energy note.

Common traps

  • Equating positive ΔG with a rate of zero. It is about direction.
  • Using ΔG° to decide direction in a cell. Use ΔG with actual concentrations.
  • Adding coupled reactions' K values. Add ΔG values; multiply K values.
  • Mixing up the sign in electrochemistry. A galvanic cell has E positive and ΔG negative.

One-minute recap

Positive ΔG is endergonic and nonspontaneous as written. It says nothing about speed and it can be overcome by coupling to a strongly negative ΔG such as ATP hydrolysis, by keeping Q low, or sometimes by changing temperature.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What does a positive delta G tell you about a reaction?

It is nonspontaneous and endergonic as written, so the forward reaction needs free energy input.

2. Does a positive delta G mean the reaction is slow?

No. Delta G gives direction, not rate; rate depends on activation energy.

3. A reaction has delta G naught of +14 kJ/mol and is coupled to ATP hydrolysis at -30 kJ/mol. What is the net?

-16 kJ/mol, which is spontaneous.

4. How can a reaction with a positive delta G naught run forward in a cell?

Delta G also depends on Q. Keeping products low makes Q small and delta G negative.

5. What is the relationship between a positive delta G naught and K?

K is less than 1, because delta G naught = -RT ln K.

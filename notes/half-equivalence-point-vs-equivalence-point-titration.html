The equivalence point is where moles of titrant equal the moles of analyte you started with. The half-equivalence point is where you have added exactly half of that titrant. They sit at different volumes and tell you different things.

Side by side (weak acid, strong base)

Half-equivalence pointEquivalence point
Titrant addedHalf the volume neededThe full volume needed
What is presentEqual HA and A-Only A- (the conjugate base)
pHpH = pKaAbove 7
Region of the curveMiddle of the buffer regionSteep vertical rise
What it is used forReading off pKaFinding the unknown concentration

Why pH equals pKa at the half-equivalence point

Half of the acid has been converted to conjugate base, so [HA] = [A-]. In the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation the log term becomes log(1) = 0, leaving pH = pKa.

Why the equivalence point is above 7 for a weak acid

All the acid has become its conjugate base, which is a weak base and raises the pH. For a strong acid with a strong base the equivalence point is exactly 7.

Common traps

  • Reading pKa at the equivalence point. Use the half-equivalence point.
  • Assuming the equivalence point is always pH 7. That holds only for strong acid with strong base.
  • Forgetting a diprotic acid has two of each. Each proton has its own half-equivalence and equivalence point.

One-minute recap

  • Half-equivalence: half the titrant, [HA] = [A-], pH = pKa.
  • Equivalence: full titrant, only conjugate base, pH above 7 for a weak acid.
  • Use the first to find pKa and the second to find concentration.

Check yourself: 5-question mini-quiz

Try each question first, then click it to reveal the answer.

1. What is the pH at the half-equivalence point of a weak acid titration?

pH = pKa.

2. At the half-equivalence point, how do [HA] and [A-] compare?

They are equal.

3. Is the equivalence point of a weak acid with a strong base above, below or at pH 7?

Above 7.

4. Which point do you use to find the unknown concentration?

The equivalence point.

5. How many equivalence points does a diprotic acid titration have?

Two.

Describe Broadbent's filter model of attention.

Early selection: a filter passes one channel by physical features (such as location or pitch) and blocks the rest before meaning is analyzed.

Front · Cloze

Piaget's four stages in order: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational.

  1. A. Selective attention
  2. B. Divided attention
  3. C. Inattentional blindness
  4. D. Automatic processing

Back

A driver tunes out the radio to concentrate on merging into fast traffic. Which process is she using?

A. Selective attention Focusing on one input while filtering the rest is selective attention. Divided attention would mean following the radio and the road at once.

Sample questions

5 real questions from this pack, with answers

Q1 A person at a party follows one conversation yet turns when her name is spoken across the room. This is best explained by:

  • A. an all-or-none early filter based on physical features
  • B. attenuation, which lowers but does not block unattended input
  • C. controlled processing only
  • D. change blindness

Answer: B. Treisman's attenuation allows highly meaningful stimuli such as one's name to pass. An all-or-none filter would block it. The phenomenon is not controlled processing or a failure to notice a change.

Q2 A disease affects 2 percent of a population and a test detects 90 percent of cases with a 10 percent false positive rate. Among 1000 people, about what fraction of those who test positive have the disease?

  • A. About 15 percent
  • B. About 90 percent
  • C. About 50 percent
  • D. About 2 percent

Answer: A. Of 1000, 20 have the disease and 18 test positive. Of the 980 healthy, 98 test positive. So 18 / (18 + 98) = 18 / 116, about 15 percent. Ninety percent ignores the base rate.

Q3 In Libet's experiment, the readiness potential began before participants reported awareness of the urge to move. This is often interpreted as raising questions about:

  • A. selective attention
  • B. conscious free will
  • C. semantic encoding
  • D. circadian entrainment

Answer: B. Brain activity preceding reported intention suggests that the decision may begin unconsciously. The other options do not concern the timing of conscious intention.

Q4 A patient with slow respiration and pinpoint pupils is given a drug that reverses the symptoms. The drug is most likely:

  • A. naloxone
  • B. flumazenil
  • C. haloperidol
  • D. physostigmine

Answer: A. Opioid overdose is reversed by the mu antagonist naloxone. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines, haloperidol is an antipsychotic, and physostigmine is an anticholinesterase.

Q5 After seeing a video, witnesses asked how fast the cars 'smashed' report higher speeds and glass that was not present. This demonstrates the:

  • A. primacy effect
  • B. misinformation effect
  • C. encoding specificity principle
  • D. spacing effect

Answer: B. A leading verb changed reconstruction of the event. The other options concern list position, context match, and distributed practice.

Five questions from the pack.

0 of 5 answered

Q1A person at a party follows one conversation yet turns when her name is spoken across the room. This is best explained by:

B. Treisman's attenuation allows highly meaningful stimuli such as one's name to pass. An all-or-none filter would block it. The phenomenon is not controlled processing or a failure to notice a change.

Q2A disease affects 2 percent of a population and a test detects 90 percent of cases with a 10 percent false positive rate. Among 1000 people, about what fraction of those who test positive have the disease?

A. Of 1000, 20 have the disease and 18 test positive. Of the 980 healthy, 98 test positive. So 18 / (18 + 98) = 18 / 116, about 15 percent. Ninety percent ignores the base rate.

Q3In Libet's experiment, the readiness potential began before participants reported awareness of the urge to move. This is often interpreted as raising questions about:

B. Brain activity preceding reported intention suggests that the decision may begin unconsciously. The other options do not concern the timing of conscious intention.

Q4A patient with slow respiration and pinpoint pupils is given a drug that reverses the symptoms. The drug is most likely:

A. Opioid overdose is reversed by the mu antagonist naloxone. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines, haloperidol is an antipsychotic, and physostigmine is an anticholinesterase.

Q5After seeing a video, witnesses asked how fast the cars 'smashed' report higher speeds and glass that was not present. This demonstrates the:

B. A leading verb changed reconstruction of the event. The other options concern list position, context match, and distributed practice.

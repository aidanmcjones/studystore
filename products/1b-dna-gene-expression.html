Nucleoside versus nucleotide: what is the difference?

A nucleoside is sugar plus base. Adding one or more phosphate groups makes it a nucleotide.

Front · Cloze

A nucleotide has a phosphate group, a pentose sugar, and a nitrogenous base.

Front · Multiple choice

In a nucleotide, the nitrogenous base is attached to which sugar carbon?

  1. A. The 2' carbon
  2. B. The 1' carbon
  3. C. The 3' carbon
  4. D. The 5' carbon

Back

In a nucleotide, the nitrogenous base is attached to which sugar carbon?

B. The 1' carbon The base sits on C1' through an N-glycosidic bond. C5' carries the phosphate and C3' carries the OH that accepts the next nucleotide.

Sample questions

5 real questions from this pack, with answers

Q1 Two DNA duplexes of equal length have melting temperatures of 85 C and 72 C. The higher Tm sample most likely has

  • A. a higher percentage of A-T base pairs
  • B. more single-strand breaks
  • C. a lower salt concentration
  • D. a higher percentage of G-C base pairs

Answer: D. G-C pairs have three hydrogen bonds and stack more strongly, so more G-C raises Tm. More A-T lowers Tm, nicks do not raise stability, and lower salt would lower Tm by increasing phosphate repulsion.

Q2 Why do human chromosomes need many replication origins?

  • A. Eukaryotic polymerases lack processivity entirely
  • B. Human DNA is circular
  • C. Eukaryotic forks are slow relative to genome size
  • D. Origins must fire many times per S phase

Answer: C. At about 50 nt/s, a single origin could not copy hundreds of millions of base pairs in S phase, so many origins fire in parallel. Human chromosomes are linear, and origins fire once per cycle because of licensing.

Q3 The template strand of a gene is 3'-TACGTTAA-5'. What is the mRNA?

  • A. 5'-UACGUUAA-3'
  • B. 5'-ATGCAATT-3'
  • C. 5'-AUGCAAUU-3'
  • D. 3'-AUGCAAUU-5'

Answer: C. RNA pairs antiparallel with the template and uses U for T: 5'-AUGCAAUU-3'. The first option copies the template, the second contains T, and the last is written in the wrong direction.

Q4 A mutant activator keeps its DNA-binding domain but lacks its activation domain. In the cell it would most likely

  • A. Activate transcription more strongly than normal
  • B. Cleave the DNA at the enhancer site
  • C. Be exported from the nucleus and degraded
  • D. Bind the enhancer without activating transcription

Answer: D. The DNA-binding domain still docks on the enhancer, but without the activation domain it cannot recruit coactivators. It can even compete with the normal activator.

Q5 In a Sanger reaction the ddNTP to dNTP ratio is increased tenfold. The fragments will

  • A. be longer on average
  • B. all be the same length
  • C. be shorter on average
  • D. stop appearing

Answer: C. A higher ddNTP fraction terminates chains earlier, producing shorter fragments. Lower ddNTP would give longer fragments, and termination still occurs at multiple positions.

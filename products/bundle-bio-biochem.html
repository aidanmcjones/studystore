Sample questions

5 real questions from the packs in this bundle, with answers

Q1 What is the approximate net charge of the tripeptide Lys-Asp-Gly at pH 7.4?

  • A. -1
  • B. 0
  • C. +1
  • D. +2

Answer: B. At pH 7.4 the free N-terminal amine is +1, the Lys side chain is +1, the Asp side chain is -1, and the free C-terminal carboxylate is -1, for a net charge of 0. Answering +1 or +2 forgets one or both negative groups, and -1 forgets the N-terminus.

Q2 A reaction has a standard delta G of +4.2 kcal/mol. It is coupled to ATP hydrolysis (-7.3 kcal/mol). What is the delta G of the coupled reaction?

  • A. +11.5 kcal/mol
  • B. -11.5 kcal/mol
  • C. +3.1 kcal/mol
  • D. -3.1 kcal/mol

Answer: D. Coupled reactions add: +4.2 + (-7.3) = -3.1 kcal/mol, so it is favorable. +11.5 and -11.5 result from adding magnitudes with the wrong signs, and +3.1 flips the sign of the sum.

Q3 Two DNA duplexes of equal length have melting temperatures of 85 C and 72 C. The higher Tm sample most likely has

  • A. a higher percentage of A-T base pairs
  • B. more single-strand breaks
  • C. a lower salt concentration
  • D. a higher percentage of G-C base pairs

Answer: D. G-C pairs have three hydrogen bonds and stack more strongly, so more G-C raises Tm. More A-T lowers Tm, nicks do not raise stability, and lower salt would lower Tm by increasing phosphate repulsion.

Q4 A patient develops ascending muscle weakness two weeks after a viral infection, and nerve conduction is slowed. Which cells are the most likely target?

  • A. Schwann cells
  • B. Oligodendrocytes
  • C. Astrocytes
  • D. Microglia

Answer: A. Guillain-Barre syndrome attacks PNS myelin, made by Schwann cells, which slows conduction. Oligodendrocytes make CNS myelin and are involved in multiple sclerosis. Astrocytes and microglia do not form myelin.

Q5 A patient with pulmonary fibrosis has a normal alveolar PO2 but a low arterial PO2. What is the best explanation?

  • A. Loss of surfactant raises the lung's compliance
  • B. A thickened alveolar-capillary membrane slows O2 diffusion
  • C. Increased anatomic dead space limits ventilation
  • D. Carbonic anhydrase deficiency slows O2 loading

Answer: B. Fick's law says diffusion falls as membrane thickness increases, so equilibration is incomplete and PaO2 lags behind alveolar PO2. Surfactant loss lowers compliance, dead space affects ventilation not diffusion, and carbonic anhydrase handles CO2.

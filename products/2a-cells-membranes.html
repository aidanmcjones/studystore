Try it · sample practice questions

Five questions from the pack.

0 of 5 answered

Q1A new antibiotic selectively blocks the 30S ribosomal subunit. Why is it relatively safe for human cells?

C. Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes (50S + 30S). Eukaryotic cytosolic ribosomes are 80S (60S + 40S) and are not blocked. Human cells do have ribosomes, they are in the cytoplasm and on the ER, and drug entry is not the selectivity mechanism.

Q2A membrane permeable only to water separates 0.2 M glucose on the left from 0.1 M NaCl on the right. What happens?

A. NaCl gives two particles, so 0.1 M NaCl is 0.2 Osm, equal to 0.2 M glucose (0.2 Osm). With equal osmolarity there is no net water flow, so neither direction is favored. Solutes cannot cross, so NaCl does not move either.

Q3A mutation prevents the LDL receptor from binding clathrin adaptors. LDL will

C. The receptor still recognizes LDL, but clathrin-coated pit formation needs the adaptor interaction, so uptake fails. Binding is intact, so it does bind, internalization is not normal, and extracellular degradation is not a feature.

Q4Severe diarrhea occurs from a mutation that makes intestinal tight junctions leaky. The most likely explanation is

A. Tight junctions limit paracellular movement. When leaky, solutes and water pass between cells. Gap junctions do not seal the epithelium, hemidesmosomes anchor to the matrix, and endocytosis is unrelated.

Q5Which finding best supports the bacterial origin of mitochondria?

C. A circular genome and binary-fission-like division resemble bacteria. ATP production and widespread distribution do not show origin. Nuclear-encoded mitochondrial proteins reflect gene transfer, not an origin as such.

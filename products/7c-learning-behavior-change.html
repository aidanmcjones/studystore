Front · Short answer

Why are habituation and sensitization called nonassociative learning?

Back

Why are habituation and sensitization called nonassociative learning?

The organism changes its response to a single stimulus without linking two stimuli or linking a response to a consequence.

Front · Cloze

A decline in response to a repeated, harmless stimulus is called [...], the simplest form of nonassociative learning.

Back

A decline in response to a repeated, harmless stimulus is called habituation, the simplest form of nonassociative learning.

A decline in response to a repeated, harmless stimulus. It is the simplest form of nonassociative learning.

Front · Multiple choice

After a loud crash, a person jumps at a quiet footstep. This is

  1. A. habituation
  2. B. classical conditioning
  3. C. negative reinforcement
  4. D. sensitization

Back

After a loud crash, a person jumps at a quiet footstep. This is

D. sensitization A strong stimulus raised responsiveness to a different, weak stimulus. No stimulus pairing or consequence is involved, so it is nonassociative sensitization.

Sample questions

5 real questions from this pack, with answers

Q1 In Aplysia, repeated gentle touches to the siphon reduce the gill-withdrawal reflex. The best cellular explanation is

  • A. reduced neurotransmitter release from the sensory neuron
  • B. increased serotonin release from a facilitating interneuron
  • C. desensitization of motor neuron receptors to transmitter
  • D. growth of new synapses through CREB-mediated transcription

Answer: A. Short-term habituation reflects presynaptic depression, with less transmitter released by the sensory neuron. Serotonin from a facilitating interneuron underlies sensitization, motor receptors remain responsive, and CREB-dependent growth underlies long-term sensitization.

Q2 A patient's fear of dogs is extinguished in therapy but returns when the patient visits the original place of the dog bite. This is

  • A. renewal
  • B. reinstatement
  • C. generalization
  • D. negative reinforcement

Answer: A. The CR returned when the CS was encountered in the acquisition context after extinction elsewhere, which is renewal. Reinstatement needs an unsignaled UCS, generalization needs a similar stimulus, and negative reinforcement is an operant concept.

Q3 A parent says, "Finish your homework, then you may play video games." This applies

  • A. the sleeper effect
  • B. the matching law
  • C. classical conditioning
  • D. the Premack principle

Answer: D. A more probable activity (games) is used to reinforce a less probable one (homework). The sleeper effect concerns persuasion, the matching law concerns choice among schedules, and no stimulus pairing is described.

Q4 To teach a pigeon to peck a disk that it never pecks spontaneously, a trainer should first reinforce

  • A. only full pecks at the disk
  • B. every response with a mild shock
  • C. only responses during extinction
  • D. any movement toward the disk

Answer: D. Shaping rewards successive approximations of the target. Waiting for a full peck gives nothing to reinforce, punishment would not build the response, and extinction removes reinforcement.

Q5 A student watches a demonstration of a surgical knot but cannot repeat it later because the steps are not remembered. Which of Bandura's processes failed?

  • A. Attention
  • B. Retention
  • C. Motivation
  • D. Reproduction

Answer: B. The learner noticed the demonstration but did not store the steps, so retention failed. Attention was adequate, motivation concerns incentive to perform, and reproduction concerns physical ability, which was not the problem described.

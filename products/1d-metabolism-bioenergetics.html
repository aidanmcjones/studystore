Five questions from the pack.

0 of 5 answered

Q1A reaction has a standard delta G of +4.2 kcal/mol. It is coupled to ATP hydrolysis (-7.3 kcal/mol). What is the delta G of the coupled reaction?

D. Coupled reactions add: +4.2 + (-7.3) = -3.1 kcal/mol, so it is favorable. +11.5 and -11.5 result from adding magnitudes with the wrong signs, and +3.1 flips the sign of the sum.

Q2How many high-energy phosphate bonds are consumed to make one glucose from two pyruvate?

C. Two pyruvate carboxylase steps use 2 ATP, two PEPCK steps use 2 GTP, and two phosphoglycerate kinase steps in reverse use 2 ATP, totaling 6 (4 ATP and 2 GTP).

Q3A child has hepatomegaly and fasting hypoglycemia, and glycogen has a normal structure. Which enzyme is the most likely defect?

D. G6Pase deficiency (von Gierke) causes hypoglycemia with massive hepatomegaly and normal glycogen structure. Branching enzyme gives abnormal structure, Pompe affects lysosomes and the heart, and muscle phosphorylase affects muscle only.

Q4A patient on a statin has a markedly lower hepatic cholesterol synthesis rate. Which compensatory change is expected?

C. Low sterol activates SREBP-2 processing, which raises LDL receptor expression and clears more LDL from plasma, lowering LDL. Reductase transcription rises, not falls, and SREBP-2 processing increases rather than decreases.

Q5A person has fasted for 5 days. Compared with day 1, which change is expected?

D. By day 5 ketones are the major brain fuel, and nitrogen loss is reduced. Liver glycogen is gone, ketones are high, and insulin is low.

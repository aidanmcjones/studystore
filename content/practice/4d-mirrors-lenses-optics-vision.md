Mirrors and thin lenses obey the same equation, 1/f = 1/d<sub>o</sub> + 1/d<sub>i</sub>, with magnification m = &minus;d<sub>i</sub>/d<sub>o</sub>. These questions use the common sign convention: converging elements (concave mirrors, convex lenses) have positive f, diverging elements (convex mirrors, concave lenses) have negative f, a positive image distance means a real image, and a negative one means a virtual image. A negative m means the image is inverted; |m| greater than 1 means it is enlarged. Real images form where light actually converges (in front of a mirror, behind a lens).

Lens power is P = 1/f with f in meters, measured in diopters. Thin lenses in contact add their powers. In a two-lens system, the image from the first lens is the object for the second, and the total magnification is the product m<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub>. The eye focuses by changing lens shape: for near objects the ciliary muscle contracts, the lens rounds up, and its power rises. Myopia (nearsightedness) is corrected with diverging lenses and hyperopia (farsightedness) with converging lenses.

Traps: a converging element forms a virtual, upright image only when the object is inside the focal point, a diverging element always forms a reduced virtual image of a real object, and powers add but focal lengths do not.

@@QUESTIONS

### Q1
An object is placed 15 cm in front of a converging lens with a focal length of 10 cm. Which describes the image?
A) 30 cm behind the lens, real, inverted and twice the object's height
B) 6.0 cm in front of the lens, virtual, upright and reduced
C) 30 cm in front of the lens, virtual, upright and enlarged
D) 7.5 cm behind the lens, real, inverted and reduced
ANSWER: A
WHY: 1/d<sub>i</sub> = 1/10 &minus; 1/15 = 1/30, so d<sub>i</sub> = +30 cm, a real image on the far side of the lens. The magnification is &minus;30/15 = &minus;2, so the image is inverted and twice as tall. An object between f and 2f always gives a real, inverted, enlarged image beyond 2f, which is how a projector works.

### Q2
A person holds a makeup mirror, a concave mirror with a focal length of 20 cm, so her face is 10 cm from it. Which describes the image she sees?
A) Real, inverted, 20 cm in front of the mirror
B) Real, upright, 6.7 cm in front of the mirror
C) No image forms because the object is inside the focal point
D) Virtual, upright, twice as large, 20 cm behind the mirror
ANSWER: D
WHY: 1/d<sub>i</sub> = 1/20 &minus; 1/10 = &minus;1/20, so d<sub>i</sub> = &minus;20 cm: a virtual image behind the mirror. The magnification is &minus;(&minus;20)/10 = +2, an upright image twice as large. An object inside the focal point of a concave mirror always gives an enlarged virtual image, which is why such mirrors magnify the face. An image does form, so C is wrong.

### Q3
All of the following are true of the image a convex (diverging) mirror forms of a real object EXCEPT:
A) It is upright
B) It is virtual
C) It is smaller than the object
D) It can be projected onto a screen placed in front of the mirror
ANSWER: D
WHY: A convex mirror has a negative focal length, so for any real object the image distance is negative: the image is virtual, upright and reduced, located behind the mirror between the surface and the focal point. Virtual images form where reflected rays only appear to come from, so no light actually converges there and they cannot be projected onto a screen.

### Q4
A lens has a focal length of &minus;50 cm. What is its power?
A) +2.0 diopters
B) &minus;2.0 diopters
C) &minus;0.020 diopters
D) &minus;50 diopters
ANSWER: B
WHY: Power is the reciprocal of focal length in meters: P = 1/(&minus;0.50 m) = &minus;2.0 diopters. The negative sign marks a diverging lens. Using centimeters instead of meters gives the &minus;0.020 choice, and simply copying the focal length gives &minus;50. A positive power would describe a converging lens.

### Q5
A thin lens of power +5.0 diopters is placed in contact with a thin lens of power &minus;2.0 diopters. What is the focal length of the combination?
A) 3.0 cm
B) 14 cm
C) 33 cm
D) 50 cm
ANSWER: C
WHY: Powers of thin lenses in contact add: +5.0 + (&minus;2.0) = +3.0 diopters. The focal length is 1/3.0 m, about 0.33 m or 33 cm, and the combination is converging. Adding the magnitudes as if both lenses were converging gives 7.0 diopters and about 14 cm, and 3.0 cm confuses diopters with centimeters.

### Q6
A nearsighted student cannot see objects clearly beyond 2.0 m (her far point). Treating the corrective lens as sitting at the eye, what lens lets her see distant objects clearly?
A) A diverging lens of power &minus;0.50 diopters
B) A converging lens of power +0.50 diopters
C) A diverging lens of power &minus;2.0 diopters
D) A converging lens of power +2.0 diopters
ANSWER: A
WHY: The lens must take an object at infinity and form a virtual image at her far point, d<sub>i</sub> = &minus;2.0 m. Then 1/f = 1/&infin; + 1/(&minus;2.0) = &minus;0.50 diopters, a diverging lens. Myopic eyes focus too strongly, so the correction spreads light out. The &minus;2.0 diopter choice wrongly uses the far point distance itself as the power.

### Q7
A farsighted patient's near point is 100 cm. Treating the corrective lens as sitting at the eye, what lens power lets him read a page held 25 cm away?
A) +3.0 diopters
B) +5.0 diopters
C) &minus;3.0 diopters
D) +4.0 diopters
ANSWER: A
WHY: The lens must form a virtual image of the page (d<sub>o</sub> = 0.25 m) at his near point (d<sub>i</sub> = &minus;1.0 m). Then P = 1/0.25 + 1/(&minus;1.0) = 4.0 &minus; 1.0 = +3.0 diopters, a converging lens, as expected for hyperopia. The +4.0 choice ignores the near point, and +5.0 adds the terms instead of subtracting.

### Q8
Two converging lenses share an axis. An object is placed in front of lens 1.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Focal length of lens 1 | 10 cm |
| Focal length of lens 2 | 5.0 cm |
| Object distance from lens 1 | 20 cm |
| Separation between the lenses | 30 cm |

What is the overall magnification of the final image produced by lens 2?
A) &minus;2
B) &minus;1
C) +1
D) +2
ANSWER: C
WHY: Lens 1: 1/d<sub>i</sub> = 1/10 &minus; 1/20, so d<sub>i</sub> = 20 cm and m<sub>1</sub> = &minus;1. That image lies 30 &minus; 20 = 10 cm in front of lens 2, so 1/d<sub>i</sub> = 1/5.0 &minus; 1/10 gives d<sub>i</sub> = 10 cm and m<sub>2</sub> = &minus;1. The total is m<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub> = +1: the final image is real, upright and the same size as the object.

### Q9
A student moves a candle to several positions in front of a concave mirror and, each time, slides a card until a sharp real image of the flame appears on it.

| Candle to mirror (cm) | Card to mirror (cm) |
|---|---|
| 36 | 18 |
| 24 | 24 |
| 18 | 36 |

What is the mirror's focal length?
A) 6.0 cm
B) 12 cm
C) 18 cm
D) 24 cm
ANSWER: B
WHY: Every row satisfies the mirror equation with the same f: 1/36 + 1/18 = 1/12, 1/24 + 1/24 = 1/12, and 1/18 + 1/36 = 1/12, so f = 12 cm. The middle row is the fastest check: equal object and image distances mean both sit at the center of curvature, 2f = 24 cm. Reading 24 cm as f is the common slip.

### Q10
A person shifts her gaze from a distant mountain to a book in her hands. Which change in her eye allows the book to come into focus?
A) The ciliary muscle relaxes and the lens flattens, lowering its power
B) The pupil dilates so more light reaches the retina
C) The cornea flattens, increasing its focal length
D) The ciliary muscle contracts and the lens becomes rounder, raising its power
ANSWER: D
WHY: A near object sends diverging light into the eye, so the eye needs more converging power to focus it on the retina. Contracting the ciliary muscle relaxes tension on the suspensory ligaments, letting the elastic lens round up, which shortens its focal length. Choice A describes focusing on a distant object, and the cornea's shape does not change for accommodation.

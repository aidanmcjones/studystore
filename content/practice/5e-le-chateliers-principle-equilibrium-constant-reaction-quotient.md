At equilibrium forward and reverse rates are equal and concentrations are constant. For aA + bB <-> cC + dD, K = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b). Pure solids and pure liquids are left out of K. The reaction quotient Q has the same form using current concentrations: if Q < K the reaction shifts toward products, if Q > K it shifts toward reactants, and if Q = K it is at equilibrium.

Le Chatelier's principle says a system disturbed from equilibrium shifts to partly oppose the change. Adding reactant shifts toward products; removing product does the same. Raising pressure favors the side with fewer moles of gas. Raising temperature favors the endothermic direction (and changes K). A catalyst speeds both directions and does not shift the equilibrium or change K. Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change partial pressures, so no shift occurs.

@@QUESTIONS

### Q1
For N2(g) + 3 H2(g) <-> 2 NH3(g), what is the effect of increasing the pressure by decreasing the container volume?
A) The equilibrium shifts toward reactants
B) The equilibrium shifts toward products
C) No shift occurs
D) K increases
ANSWER: B
WHY: The reactant side has 4 moles of gas and the product side has 2. Higher pressure favors the side with fewer gas moles, so the system shifts toward ammonia. Pressure changes alter the position of equilibrium but not the value of K.

### Q2
The forward reaction in N2 + 3 H2 <-> 2 NH3 is exothermic. What is the effect of raising the temperature?
A) The equilibrium shifts toward products and K increases
B) The equilibrium shifts toward products and K decreases
C) The equilibrium shifts toward reactants and K decreases
D) No shift occurs
ANSWER: C
WHY: Treat heat as a product of an exothermic reaction. Adding heat shifts the system toward reactants to absorb it, and K for the forward reaction decreases. Temperature is the only common disturbance that changes the value of K.

### Q3
A catalyst is added to a system at equilibrium. Which statement is correct?
A) The forward and reverse rates both increase and equilibrium is reached sooner
B) K increases
C) The equilibrium shifts toward products
D) The reaction quotient Q is halved
ANSWER: A
WHY: A catalyst lowers the activation energy of both directions equally, so it speeds up the approach to equilibrium but does not change the position of equilibrium or the equilibrium constant. Thermodynamic quantities such as delta G are unaffected.

### Q4
For 2 NO2(g) <-> N2O4(g), which expression gives the equilibrium constant?
A) K = [NO2]^2 / [N2O4]
B) K = [N2O4] / [NO2]^2
C) K = [N2O4] / [NO2]
D) K = [N2O4] x [NO2]^2
ANSWER: B
WHY: Products go in the numerator and reactants in the denominator, with each concentration raised to its stoichiometric coefficient. The coefficient 2 for NO2 becomes the exponent. Choice A is the reciprocal, which corresponds to the reverse reaction.

### Q5
For CaCO3(s) <-> CaO(s) + CO2(g), what is the correct form of K?
A) K = [CaO][CO2] / [CaCO3]
B) K = [CaCO3] / [CO2]
C) K = [CaO] / [CaCO3]
D) K = [CO2]
ANSWER: D
WHY: Pure solids have constant activity and are not included in K. Only the gaseous product appears, so K = [CO2] (or P_CO2 in terms of pressure). At a fixed temperature, adding more solid does not change the CO2 level.

### Q6
For a reaction with K = 50, the current mixture has Q = 20. Which statement is correct?
A) The system is at equilibrium
B) The reaction proceeds in the reverse direction
C) The reaction proceeds in the forward direction
D) K will change to 20
ANSWER: C
WHY: Because Q is less than K, there are too many reactants and too few products relative to equilibrium, so the net reaction proceeds forward until Q rises to 50. K itself remains constant at a given temperature.

### Q7
Helium gas is added at constant volume to an equilibrium mixture of gases. What happens?
A) The equilibrium shifts to the side with more moles of gas
B) No shift occurs because the partial pressures of the reacting gases do not change
C) The equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas
D) K decreases
ANSWER: B
WHY: Adding an unreactive gas at constant volume increases total pressure but leaves each reacting gas at the same concentration and partial pressure, so Q still equals K. If the volume were allowed to expand, the partial pressures would fall and a shift would occur.

### Q8
For a reaction at 25 degrees C, K is much greater than 1. What does this indicate about the standard Gibbs free energy change?
A) Delta G degrees is positive
B) Delta G degrees is zero
C) Delta G degrees is negative
D) It cannot be related to K
ANSWER: C
WHY: Delta G degrees = -RT ln K, so a large K makes ln K positive and delta G degrees negative. Products are favored at equilibrium. A K below 1 gives a positive delta G degrees, and K = 1 gives zero.

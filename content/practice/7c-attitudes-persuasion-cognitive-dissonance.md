An attitude has three parts: what you feel about something, what you believe about it, and how you tend to act toward it. The elaboration likelihood model says a message can change attitudes by two routes. When a listener is motivated and able to think, the central route applies, and the quality of the arguments decides the outcome. When motivation or ability is low, the peripheral route applies, and surface cues such as an attractive or famous source, a catchy tune, or the sheer number of claims do the work. Central route change tends to last longer and to predict behavior better.

Behavior can also shape attitudes. Cognitive dissonance is the discomfort of holding two inconsistent cognitions, or of acting against a belief. People reduce it by changing the behavior, changing the belief, adding justifying beliefs, or downplaying the conflict. Self-perception theory offers a cooler account: people infer their attitudes by watching their own actions. Small commitments also open the door to larger ones.

### Q1
A student who will personally pay a proposed campus fee next semester reads a flyer about it slowly, checking each budget figure and weighing the arguments. According to the elaboration likelihood model, which statement best describes this processing and its likely result?
A) Peripheral route processing, producing attitude change that is brief and easily reversed
B) Central route processing, producing attitude change that is relatively durable and predictive of behavior
C) Peripheral route processing, producing attitude change that depends mainly on argument quality
D) Central route processing, producing attitude change driven mainly by how likable the author seems
ANSWER: B
WHY: High personal relevance and careful scrutiny of the evidence define the central route, and attitudes formed this way tend to persist and guide behavior. Choice A names the wrong route. Choice C pairs the peripheral route with argument quality, which is a central route feature, and choice D gives a peripheral cue as the driver.

### Q2
In a study, students read a message supporting a new exam policy. Personal relevance was high (the policy starts next term at their school) or low (it starts in ten years elsewhere), and the arguments were strong or weak. The table shows mean attitude change toward the policy (positive means more favorable).

| Relevance | Strong arguments | Weak arguments |
|---|---|---|
| High | +2.6 | -0.4 |
| Low | +1.1 | +0.7 |

What is the effect of argument strength (strong minus weak) in each relevance condition, and what does it suggest?
A) 0.4 under high relevance and 3.0 under low relevance, suggesting peripheral processing when relevance is high
B) 2.2 under high relevance and 1.1 under low relevance, suggesting argument strength matters equally in both conditions
C) 3.0 under high relevance and 0.4 under low relevance, suggesting argument quality matters most when relevance is low
D) 3.0 under high relevance and 0.4 under low relevance, suggesting central route processing when relevance is high
ANSWER: D
WHY: Under high relevance the difference is 2.6 minus negative 0.4, which is 3.0, and under low relevance it is 1.1 minus 0.7, which is 0.4. Argument quality matters far more when the topic is personally relevant, which is the signature of central route processing. Choice C has the right numbers but the wrong conclusion, and choice A reverses the values.

### Q3
A commercial for a sports drink airs during a fast-paced game, when most viewers have little time or motivation to think about it. Which feature of the ad is most likely to shift viewers' attitudes toward the drink?
A) The strength of the laboratory evidence it cites
B) The logical structure of its claims about hydration
C) The famous athlete who appears drinking it
D) A detailed chart comparing its electrolytes with water
ANSWER: C
WHY: Low motivation and low ability to process push viewers onto the peripheral route, where a celebrity source acts as a cue that changes attitudes without scrutiny of the content. Choices A, B and D all depend on careful evaluation of the message itself, which these distracted viewers are unlikely to do.

### Q4
Volunteers copy rows of random digits by hand for 40 minutes, a dull task. Some are then paid $2 and others $40 to tell the next volunteer that the task was enjoyable. A control group tells no one anything. Later, everyone rates how enjoyable the task was on a 1 to 10 scale: control mean 3.1, $40 group mean 3.5, $2 group mean 6.3. Which conclusion is best supported?
A) The $2 group rated the task about 2.8 points higher than the $40 group, because the small payment gave too little justification for the false statement
B) The $40 group showed the most attitude change, because a large reward strengthens the belief it is paid to express
C) The two paid groups changed by the same amount, which argues against cognitive dissonance
D) The $2 group rated the task about 3.2 points higher than the $40 group, because the small payment gave too little justification for the false statement
ANSWER: A
WHY: The difference between the paid groups is 6.3 minus 3.5, which is 2.8 points. The $40 group had a ready external excuse, so little dissonance arose, while the $2 group reduced dissonance by deciding the task was fun. Choice D uses 3.2, which is the gap between the $2 group and the control group, not the $40 group.

### Q5
A smoker knows that smoking damages the lungs and feels uncomfortable about continuing. Each of the following would reduce this cognitive dissonance EXCEPT:
A) quitting smoking
B) deciding that studies on smoking risk are exaggerated
C) adopting the belief that smoking is the only way they can manage stress
D) seeking out new evidence that smoking causes lung disease
ANSWER: D
WHY: Gathering more evidence that the behavior is harmful adds to the inconsistency and increases dissonance. Choice A changes the behavior, choice B lowers the weight of the conflicting belief, and choice C adds a justifying cognition, and all three are standard ways that dissonance is reduced.

### Q6
According to the elaboration likelihood model, a person who scores high in need for cognition, meaning they enjoy effortful thinking, is most likely to be persuaded by which message?
A) One delivered by a highly attractive speaker with few supporting reasons
B) One that repeats the same slogan many times
C) One that presents strong, well-reasoned arguments
D) One that lists a large number of weak arguments
ANSWER: C
WHY: People high in need for cognition tend to elaborate on messages, so they follow the central route and respond to argument quality. Choices A and B rely on peripheral cues. Choice D can work as a peripheral cue for unmotivated listeners, but careful thinkers notice that the arguments are weak.

### Q7
Under which condition is a person's stated attitude most likely to predict their actual behavior?
A) The attitude is broad and general, such as liking nature, and the behavior is a specific act
B) The attitude is specific to the behavior and was formed through direct personal experience
C) Strong social pressure from peers pushes toward the opposite behavior
D) The attitude was measured many years before the behavior occurred
ANSWER: B
WHY: Attitudes predict behavior best when they match the behavior in specificity, are strong and accessible, and come from direct experience. A general attitude predicts a specific act poorly (choice A), strong outside pressure can override attitudes (choice C), and attitudes measured long before may have changed (choice D).

### Q8
A neighborhood group asks residents to put a small card in their window supporting safe driving. Two weeks later, residents who agreed are far more likely than others to agree to a large sign on their lawn. Which phenomenon does this illustrate?
A) Foot-in-the-door technique
B) Mere exposure effect
C) Door-in-the-face technique
D) Peripheral route persuasion
ANSWER: A
WHY: Agreeing to a small request first raises compliance with a larger related request later, which is the foot-in-the-door technique, often explained by self-perception or consistency pressure. Door-in-the-face starts with a large request that is refused (choice C), and mere exposure concerns liking from repeated contact (choice B).

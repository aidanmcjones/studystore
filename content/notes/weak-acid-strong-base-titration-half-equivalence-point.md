When a weak acid (HA) is titrated with a strong base, the curve has four landmarks. The most tested one is the **half-equivalence point**, where **pH = pKa**.

## The four regions

| Point | What is in the flask | pH |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Only HA | Weak acid pH, from Ka |
| Buffer region | HA and A<sup>-</sup> | Henderson-Hasselbalch |
| Half-equivalence | [HA] = [A<sup>-</sup>] | **pH = pKa** |
| Equivalence | Only A<sup>-</sup> (a weak base) | **Above 7** |
| Past equivalence | Excess OH<sup>-</sup> | Set by excess strong base |

## Why pH = pKa at the half point

Half of the HA has been converted to A<sup>-</sup>, so the ratio [A<sup>-</sup>]/[HA] is 1. In pH = pKa + log([A<sup>-</sup>]/[HA]), log(1) = 0.

This is also where the curve is flattest, so the solution resists pH change best (maximum buffer capacity).

## Why equivalence is above 7

At equivalence all the acid is now its conjugate base. A<sup>-</sup> accepts protons from water, making OH<sup>-</sup>. So the equivalence point of a weak acid with a strong base is basic. For a weak base with a strong acid it is acidic. Strong with strong gives pH 7.

## Using the curve

- Read the equivalence volume from the steepest part of the jump.
- Half that volume gives the half-equivalence point; the pH there is the pKa.
- Moles of acid = moles of base added at equivalence (for a monoprotic acid).
- A polyprotic acid shows one half-equivalence point and one equivalence point per proton.

## Common traps

- **Assuming equivalence is at pH 7.** That holds only for strong acid with strong base.
- **Using the equivalence volume for pKa.** Use half of it.
- **Calling the start a buffer.** Before any base is added there is only HA.

## One-minute recap

- Buffer region lies between start and equivalence.
- Half-equivalence: pH = pKa.
- Equivalence: only conjugate base, pH above 7.

:::quiz
Q: What is the pH at the half-equivalence point?
A: It equals the pKa of the weak acid.
Q: Why is the equivalence point above 7 for a weak acid and strong base?
A: The conjugate base A- reacts with water to make OH-.
Q: What is the ratio [A-]/[HA] at the half-equivalence point?
A: One.
Q: Where is the buffer capacity greatest?
A: Near the half-equivalence point, where pH = pKa.
Q: Which titration has an equivalence point at pH 7?
A: Strong acid with strong base.
:::

**Ohm's law**: V = IR. Power: P = IV = I<sup>2</sup>R = V<sup>2</sup>/R.

## Series vs parallel

| | Series | Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Equivalent resistance | R = R<sub>1</sub> + R<sub>2</sub> + ... | 1/R = 1/R<sub>1</sub> + 1/R<sub>2</sub> + ... |
| Current | Same through each | Splits; sums to total |
| Voltage | Splits; sums to total | Same across each |
| Adding a resistor | R goes up | R goes down |

Parallel equivalent resistance is always smaller than the smallest branch.

## Worked example

A 12 V battery drives a 4 ohm and a 12 ohm resistor in parallel. R = (4 x 12)/(4 + 12) = 3 ohm. Total current = 12 / 3 = 4 A. Branch currents: 12/4 = 3 A and 12/12 = 1 A, which sum to 4 A.

## Capacitors flip the rule

Capacitors in parallel add; in series they combine like resistors in parallel.

## Common traps

- **Adding parallel resistors directly.** Use reciprocals.
- **Assuming current is the same in parallel.** Voltage is the same.
- **Ignoring internal resistance** of the battery when a question gives it.

## One-minute recap

- V = IR.
- Series: same I, add R. Parallel: same V, add 1/R.
- Parallel lowers total R.

:::quiz
Q: What is the equivalent resistance of 4 ohm and 12 ohm in parallel?
A: 3 ohm.
Q: What stays the same across parallel branches?
A: Voltage.
Q: What happens to total resistance when a resistor is added in series?
A: It increases.
Q: Write one expression for power.
A: P = IV, I^2 R or V^2 / R.
Q: How do capacitors in parallel combine?
A: Their capacitances add.
:::

A population pyramid shows how many people are in each age band, usually split by sex. **Its width at the bottom tracks recent birth rates, and its narrowing as you go up tracks deaths.** So each stage of the demographic transition has a recognizable shape: a steep triangle, a wide-based triangle, a bell, a column, and a top-heavy column.

## Shape by stage

- **Stage 1 (high birth rate, high death rate):** a steep triangle with a very wide base. Many babies are born, but many people die at every age, so the sides slope inward fast.
- **Stage 2 (high birth rate, falling death rate):** a wide base with a broader middle. Fewer children and adults die, so more survive into each age band. The population grows fast.
- **Stage 3 (falling birth rate, low death rate):** the base stops widening and starts to narrow. The pyramid looks like a bell or a rounded shape. Growth slows.
- **Stage 4 (low birth rate, low death rate):** a column with roughly equal widths across ages, narrowing only at the oldest ages. Growth is near zero.
- **Stage 5 (birth rate below death rate, where the model includes it):** an inverted or top-heavy shape, with a base narrower than the middle. The population shrinks without migration.

Stage 5 is not in every version of the model, so check which version the passage uses. Use the passage's stage definitions if they differ from your notes.

## How to read any pyramid

1. **Look at the base.** A wide base means a high recent birth rate. A narrow base means a low one.
2. **Look at the slope of the sides.** Steep inward slopes mean high mortality. Straight sides mean most people survive to old age.
3. **Look for dents.** A notch in one cohort points to a war, famine, epidemic, or a short-lived drop in births. It travels upward as that cohort ages.
4. **Compare the sexes.** A longer female side at the top reflects higher female life expectancy in most populations.
5. **Check the dependency ratio.** A wide base means many young dependents. A wide top means many elderly dependents.

## Momentum

A population can keep growing for decades after birth rates fall, because a large young cohort is still moving into childbearing ages. This is **population momentum**. It is why a Stage 3 pyramid with a bulge in the middle still grows even though each woman has fewer children.

## Common traps

- **Mixing up the base and the top.** The base is births; the top is survival into old age.
- **Calling a wide base "high population."** It shows a high share of children, not a large total.
- **Assuming Stage 4 means no growth.** It means growth near zero, and some countries still grow through immigration.
- **Forgetting that the pyramid is a snapshot.** Cohorts shift upward each year.

## One-minute recap

- Stage 1: steep triangle. Stage 2: wide-based triangle. Stage 3: bell. Stage 4: column. Stage 5: top-heavy.
- Base width tracks births; side slope tracks deaths.
- Momentum keeps growth going after birth rates fall.

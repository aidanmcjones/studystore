The **epidemiologic transition** describes how the leading causes of death shift as a society moves through the demographic transition. It is the health companion to the birth and death rate curves.

## Causes of death by stage

| Stage | Death rate | Typical leading causes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | High, fluctuating | Famine, epidemics, infectious and parasitic disease, infant deaths |
| 2 | Falling fast | Infectious disease still common, but sanitation, vaccines and food supply cut deaths |
| 3 | Low and still falling | Infectious deaths are rarer; chronic, degenerative disease rises |
| 4 | Low and stable | Cardiovascular disease, cancer, other chronic disease of older age |
| 5 (proposed) | May rise slightly | Chronic disease of aging; a shrinking, older population, with possible new infections |

## Why deaths shift

- Better sanitation, clean water and vaccination reduce infectious causes.
- Higher food security reduces famine.
- More people reach old age, so conditions of aging become the leading causes.
- Behavior and environment (diet, smoking, inactivity) add to chronic disease in later stages.

## Link to life expectancy

Life expectancy rises across stages 2 and 3 mainly because infant and child deaths fall. Population growth in stage 2 is the gap between a falling death rate and a still-high birth rate.

## Common traps

- **Putting chronic disease in stage 1.** Few people live long enough for it to dominate.
- **Saying the death rate is highest in stage 2.** It is highest in stage 1 and falls in stage 2.
- **Confusing causes with rates.** A question may ask about the cause of death, not whether the rate rose or fell.

## One-minute recap

- Stage 1: infection and famine.
- Stage 2: infection declining as public health improves.
- Stage 3 to 4: chronic and degenerative disease.

:::quiz
Q: What is the epidemiologic transition?
A: The shift in leading causes of death from infectious disease and famine to chronic, degenerative disease.
Q: Which causes of death dominate stage 1?
A: Famine, epidemics and infectious disease.
Q: Which causes dominate stage 4?
A: Cardiovascular disease, cancer and other chronic disease.
Q: Why does life expectancy rise in stages 2 and 3?
A: Infant and child deaths from infectious disease fall.
Q: Why does the population grow fast in stage 2?
A: The death rate falls while the birth rate remains high.
:::

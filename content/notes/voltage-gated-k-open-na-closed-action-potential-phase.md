**Voltage-gated K<sup>+</sup> channels open and Na<sup>+</sup> channels closed describes repolarization** (the falling phase after the peak). The reverse, Na<sup>+</sup> mostly open and K<sup>+</sup> mostly closed, is the **rising phase (depolarization)**.

## Match the phase to the channels

- **Resting:** both voltage-gated types closed.
- **Rising phase:** Na<sup>+</sup> channels open, K<sup>+</sup> still mostly closed. Na<sup>+</sup> rushes in.
- **Peak:** Na<sup>+</sup> channels inactivate, K<sup>+</sup> channels begin opening.
- **Falling phase:** Na<sup>+</sup> channels closed (inactivated), K<sup>+</sup> open. K<sup>+</sup> leaves.
- **Undershoot:** K<sup>+</sup> channels still open, since they close slowly. Membrane dips below rest.

## Why K<sup>+</sup> lags

K<sup>+</sup> channels activate more slowly than Na<sup>+</sup> channels, so they are only open after the peak.

## Common traps

- **Calling the peak the Na<sup>+</sup>-open point.** At the peak Na<sup>+</sup> channels are inactivating.
- **Calling the undershoot depolarization.** It is hyperpolarization.
- **Confusing inactivated with closed at rest.** Inactivated channels cannot reopen until the membrane repolarizes.

:::quiz
Q: Which phase has K+ channels open and Na+ channels closed?
A: Repolarization (falling phase), including the undershoot.
Q: Which phase has Na+ mostly open and K+ mostly closed?
A: The rising phase.
Q: Why does the undershoot occur?
A: K+ channels close slowly, so extra K+ leaves.
Q: Why do K+ channels open later than Na+ channels?
A: They activate more slowly.
Q: Are Na+ channels open at the peak?
A: No, they are inactivating.
:::

Projectile motion is two separate problems joined by time. Horizontal motion has **constant velocity** (no air resistance). Vertical motion has **constant acceleration** of g = 9.8 m/s<sup>2</sup> downward (use 10 on the MCAT).

## Components

For launch speed v at angle &theta; above horizontal: v<sub>x</sub> = v cos &theta; and v<sub>y</sub> = v sin &theta;.

## Formulas (launch and landing at the same height)

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Time to peak | v sin &theta; / g |
| Time of flight | 2 v sin &theta; / g |
| Max height | (v sin &theta;)<sup>2</sup> / 2g |
| Range | v<sup>2</sup> sin 2&theta; / g |

At the peak, v<sub>y</sub> = 0 but v<sub>x</sub> is unchanged, so the speed is not zero.

## Worked example

A ball is launched at 20 m/s and 30 degrees, with g = 10. v<sub>y</sub> = 10 m/s, v<sub>x</sub> = 17.3 m/s. Time of flight = 2(10)/10 = 2 s. Max height = 10<sup>2</sup>/20 = 5 m. Range = 17.3 x 2 = 34.6 m.

## Angles

Range is maximum at 45 degrees. Complementary angles (such as 30 and 60) give the same range. Higher angles give more height and more time.

## Common traps

- **Setting total velocity to zero at the peak.** Only v<sub>y</sub> is zero.
- **Giving the horizontal part an acceleration.** It has none.
- **Using the range formula for different launch and landing heights.** Solve the vertical part for time instead.

## One-minute recap

- x: constant velocity. y: acceleration g.
- Peak: v<sub>y</sub> = 0, v<sub>x</sub> constant.
- Max range at 45 degrees; complementary angles tie.

:::quiz
Q: What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile with no air resistance?
A: Zero.
Q: What is the vertical velocity at the peak?
A: Zero.
Q: Which launch angle gives maximum range on level ground?
A: 45 degrees.
Q: Which angle gives the same range as 30 degrees?
A: 60 degrees.
Q: A ball is launched at 10 m/s vertically with g = 10. What is the time of flight?
A: 2 seconds.
:::

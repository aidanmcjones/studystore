Beta-oxidation breaks a fatty acid into **acetyl-CoA** two carbons at a time. It runs in the **mitochondrial matrix**. Synthesis is the opposite: cytosol, with malonyl-CoA and NADPH.

## Getting in: the carnitine shuttle

1. Acyl-CoA synthetase activates the fatty acid in the cytosol (uses 2 ATP equivalents, ATP to AMP).
2. **Carnitine acyltransferase I (CPT-I)** on the outer membrane swaps CoA for carnitine. This is the **rate-limiting step**, inhibited by **malonyl-CoA**.
3. The acyl-carnitine crosses the inner membrane and CPT-II restores acyl-CoA in the matrix.

## The four steps (each cycle)

| Step | Reaction | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oxidation (acyl-CoA dehydrogenase) | FADH<sub>2</sub> |
| 2 | Hydration | none |
| 3 | Oxidation (hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase) | NADH |
| 4 | Thiolysis | Acetyl-CoA, shorter acyl-CoA |

## Counting ATP

Each cycle gives 1 FADH<sub>2</sub> (1.5 ATP), 1 NADH (2.5 ATP) and 1 acetyl-CoA (about 10 ATP through the citric acid cycle), so about **14 ATP per cycle**.

Palmitate (16 carbons): 7 cycles and 8 acetyl-CoA.
- 7 x 4 = 28 from FADH<sub>2</sub> and NADH
- 8 x 10 = 80 from acetyl-CoA
- Minus 2 for activation
- Total about **106 ATP** (older ratios: 129 or 131)

An n-carbon fatty acid needs (n/2) - 1 cycles.

## Special cases

- **Odd-chain:** the last cycle leaves 3-carbon **propionyl-CoA**, which becomes succinyl-CoA (needs biotin and B12) and can make glucose.
- **Unsaturated:** extra isomerase (and reductase) steps; fewer FADH<sub>2</sub> because the first oxidation is already done at the double bond.

## Ketone bodies

During fasting the liver makes more acetyl-CoA than the citric acid cycle can use, because oxaloacetate is diverted to gluconeogenesis. The liver converts the excess to **acetoacetate** and **beta-hydroxybutyrate** (and acetone). The brain, heart and muscle use them. The liver lacks thiophorase, so it cannot use ketones itself.

## Common traps

- **Putting beta-oxidation in the cytosol.** It is in the matrix.
- **Forgetting the activation cost.**
- **Saying fatty acids make net glucose.** Only the glycerol and propionyl-CoA parts can.

## One-minute recap

- Carnitine shuttle, CPT-I is rate-limiting, malonyl-CoA inhibits.
- Each cycle: FADH<sub>2</sub>, NADH, acetyl-CoA.
- Palmitate gives about 106 ATP; ketones form when acetyl-CoA accumulates.

:::quiz
Q: What is the rate-limiting step of beta-oxidation?
A: CPT-I, inhibited by malonyl-CoA.
Q: How many cycles does palmitate (C16) need?
A: Seven, producing eight acetyl-CoA.
Q: What does each cycle produce besides acetyl-CoA?
A: One FADH2 and one NADH.
Q: Which tissue makes ketone bodies but cannot use them?
A: The liver.
Q: What does an odd-chain fatty acid yield at the end?
A: Propionyl-CoA, which becomes succinyl-CoA.

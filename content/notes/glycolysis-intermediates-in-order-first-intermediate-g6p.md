The **first intermediate in glycolysis is glucose-6-phosphate**, made when hexokinase (or glucokinase in liver) phosphorylates glucose and traps it in the cell.

## Intermediates in order

| Step | Enzyme | Product | ATP or NADH |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hexokinase / glucokinase | Glucose-6-phosphate | Uses 1 ATP |
| 2 | Phosphoglucose isomerase | Fructose-6-phosphate | |
| 3 | PFK-1 | Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate | Uses 1 ATP |
| 4 | Aldolase | DHAP + glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate | |
| 5 | Triose phosphate isomerase | Interconverts DHAP and G3P | |
| 6 | GAPDH | 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate | Makes 1 NADH |
| 7 | Phosphoglycerate kinase | 3-phosphoglycerate | Makes 1 ATP |
| 8 | Phosphoglycerate mutase | 2-phosphoglycerate | |
| 9 | Enolase | Phosphoenolpyruvate | |
| 10 | Pyruvate kinase | Pyruvate | Makes 1 ATP |

Steps 6 to 10 run twice per glucose because step 4 yields two three-carbon pieces.

## Which steps produce ATP

Steps 7 and 10 make ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation. That is 2 ATP per triose, so 4 ATP per glucose. Steps 1 and 3 spend 2 ATP, so the net is **2 ATP** and **2 NADH** per glucose.

## Irreversible steps

Steps 1, 3 and 10 are the large negative delta G steps and the main control points.

## Common traps

- **Counting 4 ATP as the net.** Subtract the 2 invested.
- **Forgetting that steps 6 to 10 double.**
- **Placing the NADH step with ATP use.** NADH is made at GAPDH.

## One-minute recap

- First intermediate: glucose-6-phosphate.
- ATP spent at steps 1 and 3; made at steps 7 and 10.

:::quiz
Q: What is the first intermediate in glycolysis?
A: Glucose-6-phosphate.
Q: Which two steps consume ATP?
A: Hexokinase (step 1) and PFK-1 (step 3).
Q: Which two steps make ATP?
A: Phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase.
Q: Which enzyme produces NADH?
A: Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.
Q: What is the net ATP per glucose?
A: 2 ATP.
:::

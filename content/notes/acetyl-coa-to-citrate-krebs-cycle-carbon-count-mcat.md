Carbon counting is one of the most reliable ways to keep the citric acid cycle straight. The cycle is a loop, so what matters is what **enters**, what **leaves**, and what is **regenerated**.

## Entry: acetyl-CoA plus oxaloacetate

**Citrate synthase** joins acetyl-CoA (2 carbons) to oxaloacetate (4 carbons) to make **citrate, which has 6 carbons**. The CoA is released. Because citrate synthase is highly exergonic, this step helps pull the cycle forward.

Acetyl-CoA comes from pyruvate by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, which also releases one CO<sub>2</sub> and makes one NADH per pyruvate (3 carbons to 2).

## Carbon tally through one turn

| Step | Carbons in the intermediate | What leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Citrate | 6 | none |
| Isocitrate | 6 | none |
| Alpha-ketoglutarate | 5 | CO<sub>2</sub> and NADH |
| Succinyl-CoA | 4 | CO<sub>2</sub> and NADH |
| Succinate | 4 | GTP or ATP |
| Fumarate | 4 | FADH<sub>2</sub> |
| Malate | 4 | none |
| Oxaloacetate | 4 | NADH |

Two carbons enter as acetyl-CoA and two leave as CO<sub>2</sub>, but they are **not the same two atoms**. The CO<sub>2</sub> released in a turn comes from the oxaloacetate-derived carbons, and the acetyl carbons are lost in later turns.

## Per acetyl-CoA and per glucose

- **Per turn (one acetyl-CoA):** 3 NADH, 1 FADH<sub>2</sub>, 1 GTP or ATP, and 2 CO<sub>2</sub>.
- **Per glucose:** one glucose gives **two pyruvate, so two acetyl-CoA**, so the cycle turns twice. Double the numbers: 6 NADH, 2 FADH<sub>2</sub>, 2 GTP or ATP, and 4 CO<sub>2</sub> from the cycle itself.
- Including pyruvate dehydrogenase, a glucose releases 6 CO<sub>2</sub> in total.

## Where the cycle is regulated

Isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase are inhibited by NADH and ATP, and citrate synthase is inhibited by its product citrate and by ATP. High ADP activates isocitrate dehydrogenase.

## Common traps

- **Saying 2 acetyl-CoA enter per turn.** One enters per turn; two per glucose.
- **Counting the carbons wrong in citrate.** It is 6, not 4 or 2.
- **Treating the cycle as direct ATP production.** Most energy leaves as NADH and FADH<sub>2</sub>.
- **Forgetting the link step.** PDH is separate from the cycle.

## One-minute recap

- Acetyl-CoA (2C) plus oxaloacetate (4C) gives citrate (6C).
- Two CO<sub>2</sub> leave per turn, at isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.
- Per glucose, the cycle turns twice.

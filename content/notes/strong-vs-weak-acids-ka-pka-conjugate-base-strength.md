A **strong acid** dissociates essentially completely in water. A **weak acid** only partly dissociates, and is described by Ka. pKa = -log Ka, so a **lower pKa means a stronger acid**.

## Key relationships

- Ka x Kb = K<sub>w</sub> = 10<sup>-14</sup> for a conjugate pair; pKa + pKb = 14.
- The stronger the acid, the weaker its conjugate base.
- Strong acids to know: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO<sub>3</sub>, H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>, HClO<sub>4</sub>.

## Worked example

Acetic acid has pKa 4.76, so Ka is about 1.7 x 10<sup>-5</sup>. Its conjugate base acetate has pKb = 14 - 4.76 = 9.24. A 0.1 M solution: [H<sup>+</sup>] is about sqrt(Ka x C) = sqrt(1.7 x 10<sup>-6</sup>), about 1.3 x 10<sup>-3</sup> M, pH about 2.9.

## What makes an acid stronger

Stability of the conjugate base: more electronegative atom, larger atom down a group, resonance, and electron-withdrawing groups nearby.

## Common traps

- **Reading a larger pKa as stronger.** Larger pKa means weaker.
- **Calling the conjugate base of a strong acid a strong base.** It is negligibly basic.
- **Using the weak acid shortcut for a strong acid.** For a strong acid, [H<sup>+</sup>] equals the concentration.

## One-minute recap

- Low pKa, high Ka, stronger acid.
- pKa + pKb = 14.
- Strong acid, spectator conjugate base.

:::quiz
Q: Which is the stronger acid, pKa 3 or pKa 5?
A: The one with pKa 3.
Q: A weak acid has pKa 4.76. What is the pKb of its conjugate base?
A: 9.24.
Q: Is the conjugate base of HCl strong or weak?
A: Very weak.
Q: What is Kw at 25 C?
A: 10^-14.
Q: Name two factors that stabilize a conjugate base.
A: Any two of electronegativity, atom size, resonance, electron-withdrawing groups.

Fatty acid synthesis builds palmitate from acetyl-CoA in the fed state. Beta-oxidation breaks fatty acids down to acetyl-CoA in the fasting state. They are not simple reverses of each other.

## Comparison

| | Synthesis | Beta-oxidation |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Cytosol | Mitochondrial matrix |
| Carrier | Acyl carrier protein (ACP) | CoA |
| Redox cofactor | Uses NADPH | Makes NADH and FADH<sub>2</sub> |
| Carbon unit | Malonyl-CoA adds 2 carbons | Removes 2 carbons as acetyl-CoA |
| Key enzyme | Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC), then fatty acid synthase | Carnitine acyltransferase I |
| Hormone | Insulin activates | Glucagon and epinephrine activate |
| End product | Palmitate (16C) | Acetyl-CoA |

## Regulation link

Malonyl-CoA, made by ACC, **inhibits carnitine acyltransferase I**. When synthesis is on, newly made fatty acids are not shuttled into the matrix to be burned. ACC is activated by citrate and insulin, and inhibited by AMPK and glucagon.

## Getting acetyl-CoA out

Acetyl-CoA cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane. It leaves as citrate, which is cleaved in the cytosol by ATP-citrate lyase.

## Common traps

- **Placing both in the mitochondria.** Synthesis is cytosolic.
- **Giving synthesis NADH.** It uses NADPH.
- **Forgetting malonyl-CoA.** It is the switch between the two pathways.

## One-minute recap

- Synthesis: cytosol, NADPH, ACP, insulin.
- Oxidation: matrix, NADH and FADH<sub>2</sub>, CoA, glucagon.
- Malonyl-CoA blocks fatty acid entry to the matrix.

:::quiz
Q: Where does fatty acid synthesis occur?
A: The cytosol.
Q: Which cofactor does synthesis use as reductant?
A: NADPH.
Q: What does malonyl-CoA inhibit?
A: Carnitine acyltransferase I.
Q: How does acetyl-CoA reach the cytosol?
A: As citrate, cleaved by ATP-citrate lyase.
Q: Which hormone favors synthesis?
A: Insulin.

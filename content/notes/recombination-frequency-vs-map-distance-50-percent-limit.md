**Recombination frequency** is the fraction of offspring that are recombinant. **Map distance** is measured in map units (centimorgans), where 1 map unit is defined as 1 percent recombination. For genes that are close together the two numbers match. For genes that are far apart they do not.

## When they are equal

If two genes are close, a single crossover between them is the main event, and percent recombinants equals map units. A 12 percent recombinant count means 12 map units.

## Why frequency caps at 50 percent

Genes on different chromosomes, or very far apart on the same chromosome, show **50 percent recombinants**, which is the same as independent assortment. Frequency can never exceed 50 percent, because an even number of crossovers (such as two) returns the parental combination.

## Double crossovers hide distance

Two crossovers between a pair of genes can restore the parental arrangement. That offspring is counted as non-recombinant, so the measured frequency **underestimates** the real distance. Map distances between far genes are better found by adding up shorter adjacent intervals in a three-point cross.

## Quick rules

| Situation | What to conclude |
|---|---|
| 5 percent recombinants | About 5 map units, tightly linked |
| 50 percent recombinants | Unlinked or very far apart |
| Over 50 percent in a stem | Check for an error; it is not possible for one pair |
| Sum of short intervals larger than the long interval | Double crossovers were missed |

## Common traps

- **Equating the two for all distances.**
- **Reporting more than 50 percent recombination.**
- **Counting parental double crossovers as recombinants.**

## One-minute recap

- 1 percent recombinants = 1 map unit for nearby genes.
- Recombination frequency maxes at 50 percent.
- Far apart genes are underestimated, so add intervals.

Q: How many map units equal 1 percent recombination?
A: One map unit (one centimorgan).
Q: What is the maximum recombination frequency for two genes?

Q: What does 50 percent recombinants indicate?
A: The genes are unlinked or very far apart and assort independently.
Q: Why does a double crossover lower the measured frequency?

In an **Hfr** (high frequency of recombination) strain, the F factor is **integrated into the bacterial chromosome**. During conjugation the Hfr cell transfers its chromosome to an F<sup>-</sup> recipient in a **fixed linear order**, starting at the origin of transfer (oriT) inside the F factor. That order is what makes interrupted mating useful as a mapping method.

## Cell types

| Cell | F factor | In a cross |
|---|---|---|
| F<sup>-</sup> | None | Recipient |
| F<sup>+</sup> | Free plasmid | Transfers the plasmid only; the recipient becomes F<sup>+</sup> |
| Hfr | Integrated in the chromosome | Transfers chromosomal genes; the recipient usually stays F<sup>-</sup> |
| F' | Plasmid carrying some chromosomal genes | Transfers those genes with the plasmid |

## Why the recipient usually stays F-

Transfer begins inside the F factor, so the F genes are at the **far end** of the transferred strand. The whole chromosome would take about 100 minutes in E. coli, and the mating bridge usually breaks before that, so the rear part of the F factor rarely arrives.

## Interrupted mating

1. Mix Hfr and F<sup>-</sup> cells.
2. At timed intervals, break the mating by blending or vortexing.
3. Plate the recipients on selective media and score which Hfr genes they received.

**Genes that enter earlier are found in more recipients and appear at earlier times.** The time of first appearance is the distance from oriT, in minutes.

| Gene | Time of first appearance |
|---|---|
| thr | 8 min |
| azi | 17 min |
| gal | 25 min |

The gene order from oriT is thr, azi, gal. The distance between thr and azi is 9 minutes, and between azi and gal is 8 minutes. Genes closer to oriT also give a higher frequency of recombinants because more of them were transferred before the bridge broke.

## Different Hfr strains, different starting points

Different Hfr strains integrate F at different sites and in different orientations, so each starts at another gene and transfers in a different direction. Overlapping orders from several strains reveal that the E. coli chromosome is circular.

## Transfer is not recombination until integration

Transferred DNA is a linear fragment. To be inherited it must **recombine** with the recipient chromosome by crossing over. Fragments that do not integrate are lost.

## Common traps

- **Thinking Hfr makes the recipient Hfr or F<sup>+</sup>.** Usually it stays F<sup>-</sup>.
- **Mixing conjugation with transformation or transduction.** Conjugation needs cell contact through the pilus. See the [three-way comparison](transformation-transduction-conjugation.html).
- **Treating minutes as map units from eukaryotic crosses.** They are a different scale.
- **Forgetting that the first gene transferred is the one nearest oriT.**

## One-minute recap

Hfr cells carry an integrated F factor and transfer chromosomal genes in a fixed order from oriT. Interrupt the mating at different times and the order of first appearance gives gene order, with time giving distance. The F genes enter last, so the recipient usually stays F<sup>-</sup>.

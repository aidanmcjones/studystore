**CARS is a reasoning section, not a content section.** There are no facts to memorize that the passage will not hand you, so a large content deck does not raise a CARS score the way it can for Bio/Biochem or Psych/Soc. Practice on new passages does.

## Why cards transfer poorly

- Each passage is new text, so a remembered card rarely applies directly.
- The skill is reading for the author's argument and tone, which is built by repetition on passages.
- Time pressure is part of the skill, and cards do not train it.

## What a small CARS deck can hold

Cards are fine for your own **error patterns**, not for content. Good cards:

- A wrong-answer type you keep choosing (too extreme, out of scope, reverses the claim).
- Words that signal the author's stance or a shift in argument (however, nevertheless, critics argue).
- Question stems and what each asks for (main idea, inference, strengthen/weaken, application).
- A short checklist: find the main idea, note the tone, predict before looking at answers.

## A routine that does move CARS

1. Do a small number of passages **every day**, timed.
2. Review every miss: write why the right answer is supported by the text and why yours was not.
3. Tag the miss by type, then turn only the recurring types into cards.
4. Raise the number of passages as test day approaches.

## Common traps

- **Memorizing instead of practicing passages.** Volume of passages matters more than card count.
- **Reviewing only wrong answers.** Also review right answers you guessed.
- **Cards of passage facts.** Passages will not repeat.

## One-minute recap

- CARS rewards passage practice, not recall.
- Keep any deck small and about your own error types.
- Daily timed passages plus a written review is the core loop.

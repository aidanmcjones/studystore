Gluconeogenesis makes glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors. It happens mainly in the **liver** (and some kidney) during fasting. It is not the reverse of glycolysis: it shares the seven reversible steps and replaces the three irreversible ones.

## Substrates

- **Lactate** (Cori cycle from muscle and red blood cells)
- **Glycerol** from triglycerides
- **Glucogenic amino acids**, especially alanine
- **Propionyl-CoA** from odd-chain fatty acids

Even-chain fatty acids give acetyl-CoA, which cannot make net glucose.

## The four bypass enzymes

| Glycolysis step | Bypass | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Pyruvate to PEP (pyruvate kinase) | **Pyruvate carboxylase** (pyruvate to oxaloacetate, needs biotin, ATP, activated by acetyl-CoA), then **PEP carboxykinase** (GTP) | Mitochondria, then cytosol |
| Fructose 1,6-bisphosphate to F6P (PFK-1) | **Fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase** | Cytosol |
| Glucose 6-phosphate to glucose (hexokinase) | **Glucose 6-phosphatase** | Endoplasmic reticulum |

Pyruvate carboxylase and PEP carboxykinase together make the first bypass, so there are four enzymes in all. Glucose 6-phosphatase is the step that lets the liver release glucose into blood. Muscle lacks it.

## Energy cost

Two pyruvate to one glucose costs **4 ATP + 2 GTP + 2 NADH**, versus 2 ATP gained in glycolysis. The pathway runs on energy from fatty acid oxidation.

## Regulation

- **Glucagon and cortisol:** raise gluconeogenesis.
- **Insulin:** lowers it.
- **Fructose 2,6-bisphosphate:** inhibits fructose 1,6-bisphosphatase and activates PFK-1. Glucagon lowers it, which switches the liver from glycolysis to gluconeogenesis.
- **Acetyl-CoA:** activates pyruvate carboxylase.

## Cori and alanine cycles

- **Cori:** muscle lactate goes to liver, becomes glucose, returns to muscle.
- **Alanine:** muscle amino groups travel to the liver as alanine for the same purpose.

## Common traps

- **Thinking glycolysis runs backwards.** Three steps differ.
- **Saying muscle exports glucose.** It lacks glucose 6-phosphatase.
- **Forgetting that fatty acids cannot make net glucose** (except odd-chain propionyl-CoA and the glycerol backbone).

## One-minute recap

- Liver makes glucose from lactate, glycerol and amino acids.
- Bypass enzymes: pyruvate carboxylase, PEP carboxykinase, F1,6BPase, glucose 6-phosphatase.
- Glucagon activates; insulin inhibits.

:::quiz
Q: Which enzyme lets the liver release free glucose?
A: Glucose 6-phosphatase.
Q: Which cofactor does pyruvate carboxylase need?
A: Biotin.
Q: What activates pyruvate carboxylase?
A: Acetyl-CoA.
Q: Why can muscle not supply blood glucose?
A: It lacks glucose 6-phosphatase.
Q: Can even-chain fatty acids make net glucose?
A: No. Their acetyl-CoA cannot be converted back to pyruvate or oxaloacetate in net terms.
:::

**Encoding** is converting information into a form the brain can store. It is the first stage of memory, before storage and retrieval. Better encoding means better recall later, and **meaning-based encoding is the strongest**.

## Automatic vs effortful

- **Automatic processing:** happens with no conscious effort, such as space, time and frequency of events.
- **Effortful processing:** needs attention and rehearsal, such as studying vocabulary.

## Types of encoding

| Type | Codes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Visual | Images | Remembering the page layout |
| Acoustic | Sounds | Repeating a phone number aloud |
| Semantic | Meaning | Understanding what a definition says |

## Levels of processing (Craik and Lockhart)

Memory depends on how deeply you process, not only how long.

1. **Structural (shallow):** what the word looks like.
2. **Phonemic:** what it sounds like.
3. **Semantic (deep):** what it means.

Deeper processing gives better retention. Related ideas: **elaborative rehearsal** (linking to meaning) beats **maintenance rehearsal** (rote repetition), and the **self-reference effect** improves memory for material tied to yourself.

## Strategies

- **Chunking:** group items into larger units.
- **Mnemonics:** acronyms, rhymes, imagery.
- **Method of loci:** place items along a familiar route.
- **Spacing effect:** spread study over time instead of cramming.
- **Dual coding:** pair words with images.

## Encoding and retrieval

The **encoding specificity principle** says recall improves when retrieval cues match the conditions at encoding, including context and mood.

## Common traps

- **Calling rote repetition deep processing.** It is maintenance rehearsal.
- **Confusing encoding with storage.** Encoding puts information in; storage keeps it.
- **Forgetting that automatic processing exists.**

## One-minute recap

- Encoding is the first stage of memory.
- Semantic beats acoustic beats visual or structural for recall.
- Chunking, mnemonics and spacing help.

**The cycle runs citrate synthase, aconitase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, succinyl-CoA synthetase, succinate dehydrogenase, fumarase, malate dehydrogenase.**

## The eight steps

1. **Citrate synthase:** acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate gives citrate.
2. **Aconitase:** citrate to isocitrate (isomerization).
3. **Isocitrate dehydrogenase:** isocitrate to alpha-ketoglutarate. Makes **NADH** and releases CO<sub>2</sub>.
4. **Alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase:** to succinyl-CoA. Makes **NADH** and releases CO<sub>2</sub>.
5. **Succinyl-CoA synthetase:** to succinate. Makes **GTP** (or ATP).
6. **Succinate dehydrogenase:** to fumarate. Makes **FADH<sub>2</sub>**. Embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane (complex II).
7. **Fumarase:** adds water, fumarate to malate.
8. **Malate dehydrogenase:** malate to oxaloacetate. Makes **NADH**.

## Per turn of the cycle

3 NADH, 1 FADH<sub>2</sub>, 1 GTP, 2 CO<sub>2</sub>. Per glucose, double it.

## Regulated steps

Citrate synthase, isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase. High NADH and ATP slow them; ADP and Ca<sup>2+</sup> speed them.

## Common traps

- **Putting FADH<sub>2</sub> at the wrong step.** Only succinate dehydrogenase.
- **Counting CO<sub>2</sub> at the wrong steps.** Only steps 3 and 4.
- **Forgetting the doubling per glucose.**

:::quiz
Q: Which enzyme makes FADH2 in the cycle?
A: Succinate dehydrogenase.
Q: Which steps release CO2?
A: Isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.
Q: Which step makes GTP?
A: Succinyl-CoA synthetase.
Q: Which enzyme regenerates oxaloacetate?
A: Malate dehydrogenase.
Q: How many NADH form per turn of the cycle?
A: 3.
:::

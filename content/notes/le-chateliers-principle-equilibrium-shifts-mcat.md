**Le Chatelier's principle** says that when a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts in the direction that partly counteracts the disturbance. On the MCAT, you apply it by asking one question: which way does the reaction go to use up what was added, or replace what was removed?

## The four disturbances

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| Add reactant or remove product | Toward products (right) |
| Add product or remove reactant | Toward reactants (left) |
| Increase pressure (decrease volume) | Toward the side with fewer moles of gas |
| Decrease pressure (increase volume) | Toward the side with more moles of gas |
| Raise temperature | Toward the endothermic direction |
| Lower temperature | Toward the exothermic direction |

Treat heat as a reactant or product. In an exothermic reaction, heat is a product, so raising the temperature acts like adding product and shifts the reaction left.

## Only temperature changes K

Changing concentration, pressure or volume moves the system to a new equilibrium position, but the equilibrium constant **K stays the same**. Temperature is the one change that alters K. Raising the temperature increases K for an endothermic reaction and decreases K for an exothermic reaction.

## Using Q versus K

The reaction quotient **Q** has the same form as K but uses the current concentrations.

- Q < K: too many reactants, the reaction proceeds forward.
- Q > K: too many products, the reaction proceeds in reverse.
- Q = K: at equilibrium.

This is the same logic as Le Chatelier, but it gives you a number to check.

## What does not shift equilibrium

- **A catalyst** speeds the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster but the position and K do not change.
- **Adding an inert gas at constant volume** does not change the partial pressures of the reacting gases, so there is no shift.
- **Changing the amount of a pure solid or liquid** does not shift equilibrium because it does not appear in K.

## Worked example

For N2(g) + 3 H2(g) <-> 2 NH3(g), which is exothermic, there are 4 moles of gas on the left and 2 on the right. Increasing the pressure shifts the reaction right, toward ammonia. Raising the temperature shifts it left and lowers K.

## Common traps

- **Saying a catalyst increases yield.** It only speeds up how fast equilibrium is reached.
- **Saying K changes when you add reactant.** Only temperature changes K.
- **Counting liquids or solids when comparing moles of gas.** Count gas moles only.

## One-minute recap

- The system shifts to oppose the change.
- Pressure favors fewer gas moles; temperature favors the endothermic direction when raised.
- Only temperature changes K; catalysts change neither K nor position.
- Compare Q with K to confirm the direction.

:::quiz
Q: Which direction does an exothermic reaction shift when the temperature is raised?
A: Toward the reactants (left), and K decreases.
Q: What happens to K when you add more reactant?
A: Nothing. K changes only with temperature.
Q: What does a catalyst do to the equilibrium position?
A: Nothing. It speeds up both directions equally.
Q: If Q is greater than K, which way does the reaction proceed?
A: In reverse, toward the reactants.
Q: How does increasing pressure shift a gas-phase equilibrium?
A: Toward the side with fewer moles of gas.
:::

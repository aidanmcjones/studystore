Innate immunity is the fast, nonspecific first line of defense that responds the same way every time. Adaptive immunity is slower on first exposure, targets one specific antigen, and leaves memory, so a second exposure is faster and stronger. Most MCAT questions ask which arm a cell belongs to, or which signal links the two.

## Side by side

| Feature | Innate | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Minutes to hours | Days on first exposure |
| Specificity | Broad patterns (pathogen-associated molecules) | One antigen per receptor |
| Memory | None | Yes (memory B and T cells) |
| Cells | Neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells, NK cells, mast cells, eosinophils, basophils | B cells, helper T cells, cytotoxic T cells |
| Barriers and soluble factors | Skin, mucus, stomach acid, lysozyme, complement, interferons | Antibodies |

## Innate players

- **Neutrophils** are the most abundant white cells and the first phagocytes to arrive.
- **Macrophages** engulf pathogens and present antigen. **Dendritic cells** are the best antigen-presenting cells and carry antigen to lymph nodes.
- **Natural killer (NK) cells** kill cells that have lost MHC I, which many virus-infected and tumor cells do.
- **Complement** is a cascade of plasma proteins that opsonizes (tags) pathogens, recruits cells, and can lyse bacteria.
- **Interferons** are released by virus-infected cells and put neighbors into an antiviral state.

## Adaptive players

**B cells** mature in bone marrow. Each makes one antibody specific to one antigen. After activation (usually with help from a T cell) they become **plasma cells**, which secrete large amounts of antibody, and **memory B cells**. This is humoral immunity, aimed at extracellular pathogens and toxins.

**T cells** mature in the thymus and are the cell-mediated arm.

- **Helper T cells (CD4)** recognize antigen on **MHC II** and secrete cytokines that activate B cells, cytotoxic T cells and macrophages. HIV destroys them.
- **Cytotoxic T cells (CD8)** recognize antigen on **MHC I** and kill infected or abnormal cells with perforin and granzymes.
- **Regulatory T cells** suppress the response and protect against autoimmunity.

**MHC I** is on every nucleated cell and shows internal (intracellular) peptides to CD8 cells. **MHC II** is only on antigen-presenting cells and shows engulfed peptides to CD4 cells. A mnemonic: CD4 x MHC II = 8, and CD8 x MHC I = 8.

## Antibodies

An antibody has two heavy and two light chains in a Y shape. The variable region at the tips binds antigen, and the constant region (Fc) sets the class and signals to other cells. Antibodies neutralize, opsonize, and activate complement.

- **IgM** is the first made in a primary response (pentamer).
- **IgG** is the most abundant, appears later, and crosses the placenta.
- **IgA** is in secretions such as breast milk and mucus.
- **IgE** drives allergy and defense against parasites through mast cells.

## Primary vs secondary response

The first exposure takes about 1 to 2 weeks to reach a peak, mostly IgM, with a low peak. A second exposure to the same antigen gives a rapid, larger response within days, mostly IgG, because memory cells are already present. Vaccines exploit this. Passive immunity (injected antibody, or maternal IgG and IgA) is immediate but temporary because no memory forms.

## Common traps

- **Mixing up MHC classes.** I is every nucleated cell and CD8. II is antigen-presenting cells and CD4.
- **Calling NK cells adaptive.** They are innate and have no antigen-specific receptor.
- **Expecting memory from innate cells.** Memory is an adaptive feature.
- **Forgetting that red blood cells have no MHC I** (no nucleus).

## One-minute recap

Innate: fast, broad, no memory (neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells, complement). Adaptive: specific, with memory. B cells make antibodies (humoral). CD4 helpers use MHC II, CD8 cytotoxic cells use MHC I. IgM first, IgG later and in memory response. Second exposure is faster and bigger.

:::quiz
Q: A virus lowers MHC I expression on infected cells. Which cell type is best placed to kill them?
A: Natural killer cells, because they attack cells that lack MHC I.
Q: Which T cell recognizes peptide on MHC II, and what does it do?
A: The CD4 helper T cell. It secretes cytokines that activate B cells, cytotoxic T cells and macrophages.
Q: Which antibody class dominates the primary response, and which the secondary?
A: IgM in the primary response and IgG in the secondary response.
Q: Why does a booster vaccine produce a faster response than the first dose?
A: Memory B and T cells made after the first dose recognize the antigen and expand rapidly.
Q: A newborn has IgG against measles but never made it. What type of immunity is this?
A: Passive immunity, from maternal IgG that crossed the placenta. It fades because no memory cells formed.
:::

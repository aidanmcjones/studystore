Noncompetitive inhibitors bind **away from the active site**, so they can bind the free enzyme (E) or the enzyme-substrate complex (ES). **Pure** and **mixed** differ in whether the inhibitor binds E and ES equally well.

## Comparison

| | Binding | Vmax | Km | Lineweaver-Burk lines intersect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pure noncompetitive | Equal affinity for E and ES | Decreases | Unchanged | On the x-axis |
| Mixed | Different affinity for E and ES | Decreases | Increases or decreases | Left of the y-axis, off the x-axis |

The Vmax drop happens in both because some enzyme is effectively removed from catalysis, and adding substrate does not rescue it.

## Why Km differs

- **Pure:** the inhibitor binds E and ES equally, so it does not change how well substrate binds. Km is unchanged.
- **Mixed, prefers E:** substrate binding is hindered, so apparent Km increases.
- **Mixed, prefers ES:** substrate binding is favored, so apparent Km decreases.

Many textbooks and the MCAT use "noncompetitive" for the pure case. Mixed inhibition is a general case; competitive and uncompetitive are its limiting forms.

## Compared with other types

- **Competitive:** binds E only. Km up, Vmax unchanged, lines meet on the y-axis.
- **Uncompetitive:** binds ES only. Km and Vmax both down, parallel lines.

## Common traps

- **Thinking more substrate overcomes noncompetitive inhibition.** It does not.
- **Saying Km changes in pure noncompetitive inhibition.**
- **Forgetting that Lineweaver-Burk uses reciprocals.** The x-intercept is -1/Km.

## One-minute recap

- Pure: Vmax down, Km same.
- Mixed: Vmax down, Km shifts either way.
- Both bind away from the active site.

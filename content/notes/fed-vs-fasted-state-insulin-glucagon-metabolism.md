After a meal, **insulin** dominates and the body stores fuel. During fasting, **glucagon** (with epinephrine and cortisol) dominates and the body releases it.

## Hormone comparison

| | Insulin (fed) | Glucagon (fasting) |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Pancreatic beta cells | Pancreatic alpha cells |
| Blood glucose | Lowers | Raises |
| Liver glycogen | Synthesis (glycogen synthase) | Breakdown (glycogen phosphorylase) |
| Liver glucose | Glycolysis, fatty acid synthesis | Gluconeogenesis |
| Adipose | Stores triglycerides (lipoprotein lipase) | Lipolysis (hormone-sensitive lipase) |
| Signaling | Receptor tyrosine kinase | G protein, cAMP, PKA |

Insulin promotes **dephosphorylation** of key enzymes (active glycogen synthase). Glucagon promotes **phosphorylation** (active glycogen phosphorylase).

## Time course of fasting

| Stage | Glucose source | Main fuel for other tissues |
|---|---|---|
| Fed (0 to 4 h) | Diet | Glucose |
| Early fasting (4 to 16 h) | **Liver glycogenolysis** | Glucose; fatty acids in muscle |
| Fasting (about 1 to 3 days) | **Gluconeogenesis** (lactate, glycerol, amino acids) | Fatty acids; brain still uses glucose |
| Prolonged starvation | Gluconeogenesis, less protein breakdown | **Ketone bodies** used by brain |

## Organ roles

- **Liver:** the glucose supplier; the only organ that releases free glucose (it has glucose-6-phosphatase) besides kidney.
- **Muscle:** keeps its own glycogen; lacks glucose-6-phosphatase, so it cannot export glucose. Releases lactate and alanine.
- **Adipose:** releases fatty acids and glycerol.
- **Brain:** glucose first, ketones in long fasts. It cannot use fatty acids directly.
- **Red blood cells:** always glucose (no mitochondria).

## Common traps

- **Letting muscle glycogen raise blood glucose.** It cannot.
- **Saying the brain burns fatty acids.** It uses ketones.
- **Mixing up the cell types.** Beta makes insulin; alpha makes glucagon.

## One-minute recap

- Fed: insulin, store. Fasted: glucagon, release.
- Glycogen first, then gluconeogenesis, then ketones.
- Only liver (and kidney) export glucose.

:::quiz
Q: Which hormone activates glycogen phosphorylase in the liver?
A: Glucagon, through cAMP and PKA.
Q: Which tissue cannot release glucose from glycogen into blood?
A: Muscle, which lacks glucose-6-phosphatase.
Q: What is the main glucose source in the first hours of fasting?
A: Liver glycogenolysis.
Q: What fuel does the brain use in prolonged starvation?
A: Ketone bodies, along with some glucose.
Q: Which cells secrete insulin?
A: Pancreatic beta cells.
:::

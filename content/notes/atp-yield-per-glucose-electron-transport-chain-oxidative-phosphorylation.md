Oxidative phosphorylation turns NADH and FADH<sub>2</sub> into ATP. Electrons pass down the chain in the inner mitochondrial membrane, protons are pumped into the intermembrane space, and **ATP synthase** lets them flow back to make ATP (chemiosmosis).

## The chain

| Complex | Takes electrons from | Pumps H<sup>+</sup> |
|---|---|---|
| I (NADH dehydrogenase) | NADH | Yes |
| II (succinate dehydrogenase) | FADH<sub>2</sub> | No |
| III (cytochrome bc1) via ubiquinone | I and II | Yes |
| IV (cytochrome c oxidase) via cytochrome c | III | Yes |

O<sub>2</sub> is the final acceptor, forming H<sub>2</sub>O. Complex IV needs O<sub>2</sub>, so no oxygen stops the whole chain.

## P/O ratios

- **NADH:** about 2.5 ATP (older texts: 3).
- **FADH<sub>2</sub>:** about 1.5 ATP (older texts: 2). It enters at complex II and skips complex I.

## Counting one glucose

| Stage | Direct ATP/GTP | NADH | FADH<sub>2</sub> |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis (net) | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA (x2) | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Citric acid cycle (x2) | 2 | 6 | 2 |
| Total | 4 | 10 | 2 |

Using 2.5 and 1.5: 10 x 2.5 + 2 x 1.5 = 28 from the chain, plus 4 substrate-level (2 net from glycolysis plus 2 from the cycle) gives **about 30**. If the glycolysis NADH enters by the malate-aspartate shuttle, it keeps its full value and the total is **32**. With the older ratios (3 and 2) the total is **36 to 38**.

## Shuttles

- **Glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle** (muscle, brain): delivers electrons as FADH<sub>2</sub>, so cytosolic NADH is worth about 1.5 ATP.
- **Malate-aspartate shuttle** (heart, liver): delivers NADH, worth about 2.5 ATP.

## Uncouplers and inhibitors

- **2,4-dinitrophenol and thermogenin (UCP1):** let protons leak back, so the chain runs but little ATP is made and heat is released.
- **Cyanide, carbon monoxide:** block complex IV.
- **Oligomycin:** blocks ATP synthase; the gradient builds up and the chain slows.

## Common traps

- **Giving FADH<sub>2</sub> the same value as NADH.** It skips complex I.
- **Forgetting to say which number the question wants.** Use the ratio the question gives.
- **Saying the chain makes ATP directly.** ATP synthase makes it from the proton gradient.

## One-minute recap

- NADH about 2.5 ATP, FADH<sub>2</sub> about 1.5.
- One glucose gives about 30 to 32 ATP (36 to 38 with older ratios).
- Uncouplers dissipate the gradient and make heat.

:::quiz
Q: Why does FADH2 yield less ATP than NADH?
A: It enters at complex II and bypasses complex I, so fewer protons are pumped.
Q: What is the final electron acceptor?
A: O2, which becomes H2O at complex IV.
Q: What does an uncoupler do?
A: It lets protons leak across the membrane, so O2 use continues but less ATP is made and heat is released.
Q: Which shuttle gives cytosolic NADH full value?
A: The malate-aspartate shuttle.
Q: What does oligomycin block?
A: ATP synthase.
:::

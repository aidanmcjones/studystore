**An inference question asks what the passage supports without saying it outright.** Stems look like "The author's assertions imply that...", "It can be inferred that...", or "Which of the following would the author most likely agree with?"

## What counts as a correct inference

The right answer is **close to the text**: a small, safe step from what the author wrote. It is not a new claim, and it does not need outside knowledge.

## Steps

1. **Restate the stem.** What exactly must be implied, and about what?
2. **Go back to the lines** the stem points to. Read the sentence before and after.
3. **Predict** a short statement of what follows.
4. **Match** to the choice that is supported by those lines. Check each choice against the text, not against what sounds true.

## Wrong-answer types

| Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Too extreme** | Always, never, only, all, completely |
| **Out of scope** | True in general but not discussed in the passage |
| **Reversed** | Flips the author's relationship or direction |
| **Right words, wrong claim** | Reuses passage language to say something else |
| **Too specific** | Takes a detail and treats it as the main point |

## Common traps

- **Choosing what is true in the real world.** The answer must be supported by this passage.
- **Choosing the strongest wording.** Moderate wording is more often correct.
- **Ignoring the author's tone.** Imply questions track attitude as well as content.

## One-minute recap

- Inference is a small step from the text.
- Predict first, then verify against the lines.
- Cut extreme, out-of-scope and reversed choices.

An MCAT "score calculator" can only do part of the job. You can add four section scores to get a total, and you can look up a percentile if you have the official table. What you **cannot** do from first principles is convert a raw number of correct answers into a scaled score, because that conversion belongs to the exam developer and varies between test forms.

## The scale

- Four sections: Chemical and Physical Foundations, Critical Analysis and Reasoning Skills (CARS), Biological and Biochemical Foundations, and Psychological, Social, and Biological Foundations.
- Each section is reported from **118 to 132**, with 125 as the midpoint.
- The total is the sum of the four, from **472 to 528**, with 500 as the midpoint.
- Questions are multiple choice and **there is no penalty for guessing**, so answer every question.

## Raw to scaled

Your raw score is the number of questions you answer correctly. It is converted to the scaled score using a process called equating, so that a score means the same thing across different test forms: a harder form gets a more generous conversion. This is why the same raw score on two practice tests can map to different scaled scores.

## Using a calculator well

1. **Enter section scores, not guesses.** Use scores from full-length, timed practice exams from official or well-calibrated sources.
2. **Use a range.** Practice test scores vary by several points between forms. A trend over three or more tests is more reliable than one result.
3. **Look up percentiles from the current official table.** Percentile ranks are published by the AAMC and are updated, so check the latest rather than quoting an old number. Our [MCAT score calculator and percentile tool](../tools/mcat-percentile.html) is a convenient place to try values.
4. **Work backward from the target.** Set the score your programs expect, divide by four for a rough section average, and see which sections lag.

## Balance matters

A total score hides the shape. A 510 built from four 127 to 128 scores looks different to an admissions reader than one with a 132 and a 120. Programs may also look at section scores, so do not trade away CARS or one science section to boost the others.

## Turning a score gap into a plan

- Gap of a few points: targeted content review and more timed passages.
- Larger gap: rebuild foundations, then add timed practice. See [how long to study for the MCAT](how-long-to-study-for-the-mcat.html) for sizing a timeline, and use the [study schedule generator](../tools/mcat-study-schedule.html) to lay it out week by week.

## Common traps

- **Treating one practice score as your score.** Use a trend.
- **Trusting raw-to-scaled charts from other forms.** Conversions differ by form.
- **Leaving questions blank.** There is no guessing penalty.
- **Quoting a percentile from memory.** Check the current official table.

## One-minute recap

- Sections run 118 to 132; the total runs 472 to 528.
- Raw correct answers are equated to a scaled score that differs by form.
- Use trends and the official percentile table, and always guess.

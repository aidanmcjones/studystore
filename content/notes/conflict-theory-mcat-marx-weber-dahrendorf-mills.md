**Conflict theory** is the sociological lens that asks one question: **who benefits, and at whose expense?** It treats society as an arena where groups compete for scarce resources such as money, power and prestige, and it explains inequality and social change better than it explains stability.

## The four names to know

| Theorist | Core idea | Key terms |
|---|---|---|
| Karl Marx | Society is split by who owns the means of production | Bourgeoisie, proletariat, class consciousness, false consciousness |
| Max Weber | Stratification has three dimensions | Class, status, party |
| Ralf Dahrendorf | Conflict comes from unequal authority, not only ownership | Those who give orders vs those who take them |
| C. Wright Mills | A small group makes the big decisions | Power elite |

## How to spot conflict theory in a passage

Look for unequal access to a scarce reward. A rule that quietly favors applicants who can afford expensive test prep is a conflict theory example, even though no one is fighting openly. If the passage asks what an institution does to keep society stable, that is functionalism. If it asks what a gesture or label means to the people involved, that is symbolic interactionism.

## Common traps

- **Assuming conflict means violence.** It means competition over resources, which can be quiet and legal.
- **Mixing up Marx and Weber.** Marx is about one axis, class. Weber adds status and party, so a person can be high on one and low on another.
- **Calling it a micro theory.** Conflict theory works at the macro level, like functionalism.

## One-minute recap

- Signature question: who benefits, and at whose expense?
- Marx: class. Weber: class, status, party. Dahrendorf: authority. Mills: power elite.
- Macro level, strong on inequality and change, weak on cohesion.

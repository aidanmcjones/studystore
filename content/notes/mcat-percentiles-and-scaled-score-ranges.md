MCAT scores come in two forms: **scaled scores** and **percentile ranks**. The percentile rank tells you where a score sits relative to other test takers.

## Score ranges

| Item | Range | Midpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Each section | 118 to 132 | 125 |
| Total (four sections) | 472 to 528 | 500 |

The total is the sum of the four section scores. Scaled scores are designed so the midpoint sits near the middle of the range.

## What a percentile rank is

A **percentile rank** is the percentage of test takers who scored **at or below** that score. A 90th percentile rank means about 90 percent scored the same or lower.

## Why percentiles change

AAMC publishes percentile tables based on recent test takers, and updates them over time. The same scaled score can map to a slightly different percentile from one table to the next. **Look up the current AAMC percentile table** before relying on a specific number from a blog or old resource.

## Using percentiles

- Compare scores against the **median of the schools you are targeting**, which AAMC data lists.
- A one-point change near the middle of the scale moves the percentile more than a one-point change near the extremes, because most scores cluster around the center.
- A balanced profile and a lopsided profile can have the same total.

## Common traps

- **Treating percentile as percent correct.** They are unrelated.
- **Using an old table.**
- **Adding percentiles.** Add scaled scores, not percentiles.

## One-minute recap

- Sections 118 to 132; total 472 to 528.
- Percentile means at or below.
- Check the current AAMC table.

**IR spectroscopy shows which bonds are present.** Bonds absorb at characteristic **wavenumbers (cm-1)**. On the MCAT you mainly need a few strong peaks and their shapes.

## Peaks to memorize

| Bond | Wavenumber (cm-1) | Shape and notes |
|---|---|---|
| **O-H (alcohol)** | about 3200 to 3600 | **Broad**, strong |
| **O-H (carboxylic acid)** | about 2500 to 3300 | **Very broad**, overlaps C-H |
| **N-H** | about 3300 to 3500 | Sharper than O-H; primary amines show two peaks, secondary one |
| **C-H (sp3)** | just under 3000 | Alkane C-H |
| **C-H (sp2)** | just over 3000 | Alkene and aromatic C-H |
| **C triple bond C or C triple bond N** | about 2100 to 2260 | Sharp, in a quiet region |
| **C=O** | about 1650 to 1750 | **Strong, sharp**, the easiest peak to spot |
| **C=C** | about 1600 to 1680 | Weaker than C=O |

## A fast routine

1. Look near **1700** for a strong C=O.
2. Look above **3200** for an O-H or N-H.
3. Combine them: C=O plus a very broad O-H is a **carboxylic acid**; C=O with no O-H or N-H is a ketone, aldehyde or ester; a broad O-H with no C=O is an alcohol.
4. Check for an aldehyde C-H near 2700 to 2850 (two small peaks).

## Common traps

- **Mixing alcohol and acid O-H.** The acid O-H is wider and extends lower.
- **Missing conjugation.** A conjugated C=O absorbs at a slightly lower wavenumber.
- **Reading only the fingerprint region.** Below about 1500 it is too complex for the MCAT.
- **Expecting an amide to look like a ketone.** Amide C=O sits lower, around 1650.

## One-minute recap

- C=O: strong, sharp, near 1700.
- O-H: broad above 3200; acid is very broad.
- Triple bonds: near 2100 to 2260.

:::quiz
Q: Where does a carbonyl C=O absorb?
A: Near 1700 cm-1, strong and sharp.
Q: How does a carboxylic acid O-H differ from an alcohol O-H?
A: It is much broader and extends lower, about 2500 to 3300.
Q: Where do alkyne and nitrile triple bonds absorb?
A: About 2100 to 2260 cm-1.
Q: What distinguishes a primary amine N-H in IR?
A: Two peaks near 3300 to 3500.
Q: Why is the fingerprint region not used on the MCAT?
A: It is too complex to assign.
:::

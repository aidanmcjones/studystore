The **pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC)** links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle. It runs in the mitochondrial matrix and converts pyruvate (3 carbons) to acetyl-CoA (2 carbons).

## Net reaction

Pyruvate + CoA + NAD<sup>+</sup> gives acetyl-CoA + CO<sub>2</sub> + NADH.

Per pyruvate: one CO<sub>2</sub> lost, one NADH made. Per glucose (two pyruvate): 2 CO<sub>2</sub>, 2 NADH, 2 acetyl-CoA.

## Cofactors

Five are used: **thiamine pyrophosphate (B1)**, **lipoic acid**, **CoA (B5, pantothenate)**, **FAD (B2)** and **NAD<sup>+</sup> (B3)**. A shortage of B1 stalls the complex, so pyruvate and lactate build up.

## Regulation

| Effect | Signal |
|---|---|
| Activated | Pyruvate, ADP, NAD<sup>+</sup>, CoA, insulin (via phosphatase) |
| Inhibited | Acetyl-CoA, NADH, ATP |
| Covalent | PDH kinase phosphorylates and inactivates; PDH phosphatase activates |

Products inhibit the complex, which makes sense: plenty of acetyl-CoA and NADH means the cell has fuel and reducing power.

## Irreversible

The reaction has a large negative delta G, and animals cannot make glucose from acetyl-CoA. Fatty acids, which break down to acetyl-CoA, therefore cannot become net glucose.

## Arsenic and PDC

Arsenic binds lipoic acid and inhibits the complex. Expect a clinical vignette that links it to high blood pyruvate.

## Common traps

- **Counting CO<sub>2</sub> per glucose as one.** It is two, because there are two pyruvate.
- **Placing PDC in the cytosol.** It is in the matrix.
- **Saying ATP is made directly.** PDC makes NADH, which pays off later in the electron transport chain.

## One-minute recap

- Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, CO<sub>2</sub> and NADH, in the matrix.
- Five cofactors; B1 deficiency stalls it.
- Inhibited by acetyl-CoA, NADH and ATP; irreversible.

:::quiz
Q: Where does the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex work?
A: The mitochondrial matrix.
Q: What are its products per pyruvate?
A: Acetyl-CoA, CO2 and NADH.
Q: Name three cofactors.
A: Any three of thiamine pyrophosphate, lipoic acid, CoA, FAD, NAD+.
Q: What inhibits the complex?
A: Acetyl-CoA, NADH and ATP, plus phosphorylation by PDH kinase.
Q: Can fatty acids make net glucose?
A: No. The PDC step is irreversible, so acetyl-CoA cannot return to pyruvate.
:::

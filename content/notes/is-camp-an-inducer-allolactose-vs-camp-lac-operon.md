**No, cAMP is not the inducer.** The inducer of the lac operon is **allolactose**, an isomer of lactose that binds the lac repressor and removes it from the operator. **cAMP** is a different kind of signal: it binds **CAP** (catabolite activator protein) and helps RNA polymerase initiate transcription.

## Two separate controls

| Signal | Binds | Effect | Type of control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allolactose | LacI repressor | Repressor leaves the operator | Negative, induction |
| cAMP | CAP | CAP-cAMP binds near the promoter and boosts transcription | Positive, activation |

## How glucose enters the story

When glucose is high, cAMP is low, so CAP is not active. When glucose is low, adenylate cyclase activity rises, cAMP is high, and CAP-cAMP binds DNA. This is **catabolite repression**: the cell prefers glucose, so it does not make strong lac operon output while glucose is available.

## Four-condition table

| Lactose | Glucose | Repressor | cAMP-CAP | lac output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absent | High | Bound | Inactive | Off |
| Absent | Low | Bound | Active | Off |
| Present | High | Released | Inactive | Low |
| Present | Low | Released | Active | High |

You need both: the repressor off the operator **and** CAP-cAMP active for high output.

## Common traps

- **Calling cAMP an inducer.** Inducer means it inactivates a repressor. Allolactose does that.
- **Thinking glucose acts on the repressor.** Glucose changes cAMP, which acts through CAP.
- **Forgetting that lactose alone gives only low output.** High output also needs low glucose.

## One-minute recap

Allolactose is the inducer and works on the repressor. cAMP is a coactivator signal that works through CAP and rises when glucose is low. Full expression needs lactose present and glucose low. :::quiz
Q: What is the inducer of the lac operon?
A: Allolactose, which binds the repressor and releases it from the operator.
Q: What does cAMP bind, and what does it do?
A: It binds CAP, and CAP-cAMP binds near the promoter to boost transcription.
Q: How does glucose affect cAMP levels?
A: High glucose lowers cAMP; low glucose raises it.
Q: With lactose present and glucose high, is lac output high?
A: No, it is low, because CAP-cAMP is not active.
Q: Why is the lac operon described as under both negative and positive control?
A: The repressor is negative control and CAP-cAMP is positive control.
:::

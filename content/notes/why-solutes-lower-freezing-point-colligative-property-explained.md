**Freezing point depression is colligative because it depends only on how many solute particles are dissolved, not on what they are.** A solute dilutes the solvent, which makes the liquid more stable relative to the pure solid, so the solution must be cooled further to freeze.

## The formula

**&Delta;T<sub>f</sub> = i &middot; K<sub>f</sub> &middot; m**

- **i:** van't Hoff factor, the number of particles per formula unit.
- **K<sub>f</sub>:** solvent constant.
- **m:** molality, mol solute per kg solvent.

The freezing point falls by &Delta;T<sub>f</sub> from the pure solvent value.

## Particle count, not identity

- 1 m glucose: i = 1.
- 1 m NaCl: i near 2.
- 1 m CaCl<sub>2</sub>: i near 3, so the largest drop.

## Why molality

Molality does not change with temperature, unlike molarity, so it is used for freezing point and boiling point problems.

## Common traps

- **Forgetting i** for ionic compounds.
- **Using molarity** instead of molality.
- **Raising instead of lowering.** Boiling point goes up; freezing point goes down.
- **Thinking the solute freezes out.** In ideal cases pure solvent freezes first.

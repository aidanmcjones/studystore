Stage 5 of the **demographic transition model** is the stage where the **crude birth rate falls below the crude death rate**, so natural increase turns negative and the population shrinks unless immigration fills the gap. Many textbooks stop at stage 4, which is why MCAT passages often add stage 5 as a "possible" extra stage and ask you to characterize it.

## Stage 4 vs stage 5

| Feature | Stage 4 | Stage 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Birth rate | Low | Very low, below replacement |
| Death rate | Low | Low to slightly rising (older population) |
| Natural increase | Near zero or slightly positive | Negative |
| Total fertility rate | About 2.1 or a little below | Well below 2.1 |
| Pyramid shape | Columnar | Inverted or top-heavy |
| Median age | High | Higher still |

## Why the death rate can rise

Death rates are already low in stage 4, but an aging population has more people in the age groups where deaths are common. The crude death rate can therefore creep up even as health care improves. The **age-specific** death rate stays low.

## Reading the pyramid

A stage 5 pyramid is **narrow at the base and wide at the top**. Fewer children are born than the number of older adults, so the dependency ratio shifts toward the elderly.

## Consequences the MCAT likes

- **Population decline** without immigration.
- **Higher old-age dependency ratio**, with a smaller working-age group supporting retirees.
- **Policy responses** such as pronatalist incentives or immigration.

## Common traps

- **Assuming the death rate must fall in stage 5.** It is low and may rise in the crude measure.
- **Confusing replacement with zero growth.** Replacement fertility is about 2.1 children per woman; below that, the population eventually shrinks.
- **Treating stage 5 as universal.** It is not in every version of the model, so read the passage definition.

## One-minute recap

Stage 5 means birth rate below death rate, negative natural increase, an inverted pyramid and an aging population. Stage 4 sits near zero growth; stage 5 sits below it. For the full set of stages see the [five-stage overview](demographic-transition-model-stages.html).

:::quiz
Q: What defines stage 5 of the demographic transition model?
A: The crude birth rate falls below the crude death rate, so natural increase is negative.
Q: What does a stage 5 population pyramid look like?
A: Narrow at the base and wide at the top, an inverted or top-heavy shape.
Q: Why might the crude death rate rise in stage 5 even though health care is good?
A: The population is older, so more people are in age groups where deaths are common.
Q: What total fertility rate is replacement level?
A: About 2.1 children per woman.
Q: How does a stage 5 country keep its population from falling?
A: Immigration, or a rise in fertility from policy or social change.

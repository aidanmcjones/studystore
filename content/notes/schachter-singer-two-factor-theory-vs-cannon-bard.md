Both theories reject the idea that the body alone defines the emotion. The difference is what else they add. **Cannon-Bard** says the thalamus sends the signal to the cortex and the body at the same time. **Schachter-Singer** says arousal is vague until you **label** it using the situation.

## Side by side

| | Cannon-Bard | Schachter-Singer (two-factor) |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence | Stimulus, then arousal and emotion at the same time | Stimulus, arousal, cognitive label, emotion |
| Role of the body | Parallel, not the cause | Necessary but nonspecific |
| Role of thinking | Not required for the feeling | Required to name the feeling |
| Key structure | Thalamus | Cortex interpreting context |
| Prediction | Same arousal can be unrelated to which emotion you feel | Same arousal can become different emotions in different settings |

## The two factors

Schachter-Singer named two ingredients: **physiological arousal** and a **cognitive label**. Without a plausible explanation for the arousal, people search the environment for one.

## The adrenaline study

Participants were injected with epinephrine. Those who were not told what to expect, and who then sat with a euphoric or an angry confederate, reported feelings that matched the confederate. Those correctly informed about the drug's effects had an explanation for the arousal and were less swayed. This supports the cognitive label idea and is the classic **misattribution of arousal** result.

## How to identify the theory in a stem

- "At the same moment" or "simultaneously": Cannon-Bard.
- "Interprets the situation," "labels," "misattributes": Schachter-Singer.
- "Because my heart was racing, I realized I was afraid": James-Lange, not either of these.

## Common traps

- **Saying Schachter-Singer has no arousal.** It needs arousal plus a label.
- **Saying Cannon-Bard needs cognition.** Its claim is parallel timing.
- **Mixing Schachter-Singer with Lazarus.** Lazarus says appraisal comes first and is the cause.

## One-minute recap

- Cannon-Bard: arousal and emotion in parallel, via the thalamus.
- Schachter-Singer: arousal plus a situational label equals emotion.

:::quiz
Q: In Cannon-Bard, what is the order of arousal and the emotional experience?
A: They occur at the same time, triggered in parallel by the thalamus.
Q: What are the two factors in the Schachter-Singer theory?
A: Physiological arousal and a cognitive label for it.
Q: What was injected in the classic Schachter-Singer study?
A: Epinephrine.
Q: A person feels a racing heart after caffeine and then blames a stressful exam for it. Which concept is this?
A: Misattribution of arousal, as in Schachter-Singer.
Q: Which theory says the feeling is simply the perception of bodily changes?
A: James-Lange.

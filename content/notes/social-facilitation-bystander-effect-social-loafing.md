Several MCAT group-behavior terms sound alike. Sort them by what changes when other people are present.

## The comparison

| Term | What happens | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Social facilitation** | Performance changes on a task in front of others | Arousal strengthens the dominant response |
| **Social loafing** | Less individual effort in a group | Diffused accountability when output is pooled |
| **Bystander effect** | Less likely to help when more people are present | Diffusion of responsibility |
| **Deindividuation** | Loss of self-awareness, norm breaking | Anonymity in a crowd |
| **Group polarization** | Group view becomes more extreme than the average starting view | Persuasive arguments and social comparison |
| **Groupthink** | Poor decisions from pressure for harmony | Cohesion, isolation, strong leader |

## Social facilitation in detail

Others' presence raises arousal. Arousal improves **easy or well-learned** tasks (the dominant response is correct) and impairs **hard or new** tasks (the dominant response is wrong). This is the Yerkes-Dodson style pattern, applied to audiences.

## Bystander effect

Darley and Latane studied it after the Kitty Genovese case. Larger groups lower the chance that any one person helps. Helping rises when the emergency is clear, a person is directly asked, or the helper feels competent.

## Social loafing

It occurs when individual contributions cannot be identified. It shrinks when each person's work is tracked, the task is meaningful, or the group is small.

## Common traps

- **Using facilitation for all audience effects.** Hard tasks get worse.
- **Mixing loafing and bystander effect.** Loafing is effort on a shared task; bystander is helping in an emergency.
- **Treating polarization as moving to the middle.** It moves to the extreme.

## One-minute recap

- Facilitation: audience helps easy tasks, hurts hard ones.
- Loafing and bystander effect: diffusion of responsibility.
- Polarization: group pushes the shared view further out.

:::quiz
Q: When does an audience impair performance?
A: On difficult or unfamiliar tasks, via social facilitation.
Q: What mechanism explains the bystander effect?
A: Diffusion of responsibility.
Q: What reduces social loafing?
A: Making individual contributions identifiable.
Q: What is group polarization?
A: A group's shared view becomes more extreme after discussion.
Q: What does deindividuation involve?
A: Reduced self-awareness and norm breaking under anonymity.
:::

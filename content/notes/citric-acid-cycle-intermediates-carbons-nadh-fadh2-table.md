Use this as a one-glance table of the cycle. Citrate and isocitrate each have **6 carbons**, and succinate has **4**.

## The cycle in order

| Intermediate | Carbons | Enzyme that makes it | Byproduct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citrate | 6 | Citrate synthase | CoA released |
| Isocitrate | 6 | Aconitase | None |
| Alpha-ketoglutarate | 5 | Isocitrate dehydrogenase | CO<sub>2</sub>, NADH |
| Succinyl-CoA | 4 | Alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase | CO<sub>2</sub>, NADH |
| Succinate | 4 | Succinyl-CoA synthetase | GTP (or ATP) |
| Fumarate | 4 | Succinate dehydrogenase | FADH<sub>2</sub> |
| Malate | 4 | Fumarase | None |
| Oxaloacetate | 4 | Malate dehydrogenase | NADH |

## Totals per turn

Each turn gives **3 NADH, 1 FADH<sub>2</sub>, 1 GTP and 2 CO<sub>2</sub>**. Per glucose, the cycle runs twice, so double each number.

## Memory aids

- The two CO<sub>2</sub> leave at isocitrate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, which is why the carbons drop from 6 to 4.
- Succinate dehydrogenase is the only cycle enzyme embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane (complex II).

## Common traps

- **Giving succinate 5 carbons.**
- **Saying FADH<sub>2</sub> comes from malate dehydrogenase.** It is succinate dehydrogenase.
- **Forgetting the doubling per glucose.**

## One-minute recap

- 6, 6, 5, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4 carbons around the loop.
- CO<sub>2</sub> twice, NADH three times, FADH<sub>2</sub> once, GTP once.

Three attribution biases get mixed up constantly. The **fundamental attribution error (FAE)** explains **other people's behavior** by their traits. **Actor-observer bias** explains **your own behavior by the situation but others' by their traits**. **Self-serving bias** explains **your own outcomes** so that wins are yours and losses are not. **Ask whose behavior is explained, then whether the outcome was good or bad.**

## All three side by side

| | FAE | Actor-observer bias | Self-serving bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whose behavior | Another person's | Yours and another person's, compared | Your own outcome |
| Favored cause | Disposition (traits) | Situation for self, disposition for others | Internal for success, external for failure |
| Needs a good or bad outcome | No | No | Yes |
| Example | "She is late because she is careless." | "I was late because of traffic; he was late because he is careless." | "I passed because I am smart; I failed because the exam was unfair." |

## A three-step decision rule

1. **Is the speaker explaining their own behavior, someone else's, or both?** Someone else's only points to FAE. Both, contrasted, points to actor-observer bias.
2. **Is there a success or failure for the speaker?** If the speaker credits themselves for wins and blames circumstances for losses, it is self-serving bias.
3. **Is the cause a trait or a circumstance?** FAE always picks the trait for the other person.

## How they overlap

The second half of actor-observer bias (blaming others' traits) is the FAE. Self-serving bias overlaps with the "self gets the situation" half of actor-observer bias only for failures. For successes, self-serving bias points to the self, while actor-observer bias would still point to the situation. That difference is the usual exam trap.

## Common traps

- **Calling any trait-based judgment of another person "actor-observer."** Without a comparison to the speaker's own behavior, it is FAE.
- **Forgetting the outcome in self-serving bias.** It needs a success or failure.
- **Assuming the three are exclusive.** One passage can contain two of them.

## One-minute recap

- FAE: others, traits over situation.
- Actor-observer: self gets the situation, others get the trait.
- Self-serving: credit for wins, excuses for losses.

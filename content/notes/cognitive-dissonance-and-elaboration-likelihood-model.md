Attitudes change through two main routes on the MCAT: reducing the discomfort of **cognitive dissonance**, and persuasion described by the **elaboration likelihood model (ELM)**. Learn to recognize which one a vignette describes.

## Components of an attitude

An attitude has three parts, often remembered as ABC:

- **Affective:** feelings toward the object.
- **Behavioral:** how you act toward it.
- **Cognitive:** beliefs and thoughts about it.

## Cognitive dissonance

Leon Festinger proposed that holding two inconsistent cognitions, or acting against a belief, creates psychological tension. People are motivated to reduce it in one of several ways:

- Change the belief to match the behavior.
- Change the behavior to match the belief.
- Add a new justifying cognition.
- Minimize the importance of the conflict.

A classic result is that people paid very little to say a boring task was fun later rated it as more enjoyable than people paid a lot, because a small reward leaves insufficient external justification and so the attitude shifts instead.

## Elaboration likelihood model

The ELM describes two routes to persuasion, depending on how motivated and able the listener is to think carefully.

| Route | When used | What persuades |
|---|---|---|
| Central | High motivation, high ability, personally relevant | Strength and quality of the arguments, evidence |
| Peripheral | Low motivation or distracted | Superficial cues: speaker attractiveness, credibility, number of arguments, emotion |

Central route change tends to be more durable and more resistant to counterargument. Peripheral route change is quicker and shorter lived.

## Other persuasion factors

Persuasion depends on the source (credible, attractive), the message (clear, balanced, emotional or logical) and the target (age, self-esteem, involvement). A vignette that stresses an expert endorsement for an uninterested audience points to the peripheral route.

## Common traps

- **Calling dissonance a persuasion route.** It is an internal drive to restore consistency.
- **Treating the peripheral route as ineffective.** It works, but the change is less lasting.
- **Mixing up the attitude components.** Feelings are affective, actions behavioral, beliefs cognitive.

## One-minute recap

- Attitude = affect, behavior, cognition.
- Dissonance creates tension that people reduce by changing attitudes or behavior.
- ELM: central route for careful thought, peripheral route for cues.

:::quiz
Q: What are the three components of an attitude?
A: Affective, behavioral and cognitive.
Q: What creates cognitive dissonance?
A: Holding inconsistent beliefs or acting against a belief.
Q: Which ELM route involves careful evaluation of arguments?
A: The central route.
Q: Which route is more likely when the listener is distracted?
A: The peripheral route.
Q: Which route tends to produce longer-lasting change?
A: The central route.
:::

VSEPR (valence shell electron pair repulsion) theory says that regions of electron density around a central atom spread as far apart as possible. You count the **electron domains** on the central atom, which fixes the electron geometry, and then ignore the lone pairs when you name the **molecular geometry**, which describes only where the atoms sit.

## Count domains first

An electron domain is a bond or a lone pair. A single, double or triple bond each counts as **one** domain, because all the electrons in a multiple bond occupy the same region. So CO<sub>2</sub> (O=C=O) has two domains on carbon, and H<sub>2</sub>C=O has three.

1. Draw the Lewis structure.
2. Count domains on the central atom.
3. Count how many of those are lone pairs.
4. Read the shape from the table below.

## The shape table

| Domains | Lone pairs | Molecular geometry | Ideal angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | Linear | 180 degrees | CO<sub>2</sub> |
| 3 | 0 | Trigonal planar | 120 degrees | BF<sub>3</sub> |
| 3 | 1 | Bent | slightly under 120 | SO<sub>2</sub> |
| 4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5 degrees | CH<sub>4</sub> |
| 4 | 1 | Trigonal pyramidal | about 107 degrees | NH<sub>3</sub> |
| 4 | 2 | Bent | about 104.5 degrees | H<sub>2</sub>O |
| 5 | 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90 and 120 degrees | PCl<sub>5</sub> |
| 6 | 0 | Octahedral | 90 degrees | SF<sub>6</sub> |

The MCAT most often tests the first six rows. Water, ammonia and methane are the standard trio: all three have four domains and tetrahedral electron geometry, but the molecular shapes differ because of lone pairs.

## Why lone pairs squeeze bond angles

A lone pair is held by one nucleus only, so its cloud is larger and repels more strongly than a bonding pair. The order is lone pair/lone pair greater than lone pair/bonding pair greater than bonding pair/bonding pair. That is why the angle shrinks from 109.5 degrees in methane to about 107 degrees in ammonia and about 104.5 degrees in water.

## From shape to polarity

A molecule is polar only if it has polar bonds **and** the bond dipoles do not cancel. The dipoles are vectors, so symmetry decides the result.

- **CO<sub>2</sub>**: two polar C=O bonds point opposite ways and cancel. Nonpolar overall.
- **H<sub>2</sub>O**: bent, so the two O-H dipoles add up. Polar.
- **BF<sub>3</sub>**: three polar bonds at 120 degrees cancel. Nonpolar.
- **NH<sub>3</sub>**: pyramidal, dipoles do not cancel. Polar.
- **CCl<sub>4</sub>**: tetrahedral and symmetric, so nonpolar even though each C-Cl bond is polar.
- **CHCl<sub>3</sub>**: tetrahedral but not symmetric, so polar.

Polarity then predicts boiling point, solubility ("like dissolves like") and the strength of intermolecular forces. Electronegativity trends from the [periodic table note](periodic-table-trends-mcat.html) tell you which bonds are polar in the first place.

## Hybridization shortcut

The number of domains matches the hybridization: 2 domains is sp, 3 is sp<sup>2</sup>, 4 is sp<sup>3</sup>. Lone pairs count, so the nitrogen in NH<sub>3</sub> and the oxygen in water are both sp<sup>3</sup>.

## Common traps

- **Naming the electron geometry when asked for the molecular geometry.** Water is bent, not tetrahedral.
- **Counting a double bond as two domains.** It is one.
- **Assuming polar bonds mean a polar molecule.** Symmetric molecules cancel.
- **Forgetting that bent molecules from the 3-domain and 4-domain rows have different angles.**

## One-minute recap

Count electron domains, subtract lone pairs to name the shape, and remember that lone pairs compress angles below the ideal value. Polarity needs polar bonds plus an asymmetric shape. Domains of 2, 3, 4 give sp, sp<sup>2</sup>, sp<sup>3</sup>.

:::quiz
Q: What is the molecular geometry of NH3 and why is its bond angle less than 109.5 degrees?
A: Trigonal pyramidal. Nitrogen has four domains, one a lone pair, and the lone pair repels the bonding pairs more strongly, compressing the angle to about 107 degrees.
Q: CCl4 has four polar bonds. Why is the molecule nonpolar?
A: It is tetrahedral and symmetric, so the four bond dipoles cancel to a net dipole of zero.
Q: How many electron domains does the carbon in H2C=O have, and what is the shape?
A: Three (two single bonds and one double bond count as three domains). The shape is trigonal planar with angles near 120 degrees.
Q: Water and methane both have tetrahedral electron geometry. Why is only water polar?
A: Water has two lone pairs, making its molecular shape bent, so its O-H dipoles add. Methane is symmetric and its dipoles cancel.
Q: What hybridization does the oxygen in water have, and why?
A: sp3. Oxygen has four electron domains (two bonds and two lone pairs), and lone pairs count toward domains.
:::

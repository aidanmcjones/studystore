Sleep moves through four stages in a cycle: **N1, N2, N3 (all non-REM) and REM**. The MCAT mostly asks you to match a stage to its EEG wave or a behavior.

## Stages and waves

| State | EEG pattern | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Awake, alert | Beta (fast, low amplitude) | Active thinking |
| Awake, relaxed, eyes closed | Alpha | Drowsy calm |
| N1 | Theta | Light sleep, hypnagogic imagery, hypnic jerks |
| N2 | Theta with sleep spindles and K complexes | Largest share of the night, sleep spindles bursts |
| N3 | Delta (slow, high amplitude) | Slow-wave sleep, hardest to wake, sleepwalking and night terrors |
| REM | Fast, desynchronized, resembles waking | Vivid dreams, rapid eye movements, muscle atonia |

## The cycle

A full cycle lasts about 90 minutes and repeats four to six times a night. Early cycles have more N3. Later cycles have longer REM periods, so cutting sleep short removes disproportionately more REM.

## REM details

- Brain activity looks awake, but skeletal muscles are largely paralyzed, so it is sometimes called paradoxical sleep.
- Heart rate and breathing become irregular.
- Dreaming is most vivid, though dreams occur in other stages too.

## Common traps

- **Calling delta waves a feature of REM.** Delta belongs to N3.
- **Forgetting that REM EEG looks like waking.**
- **Placing sleep spindles in N3.** They mark N2.

## One-minute recap

- Awake: beta; relaxed: alpha.
- N1 theta, N2 spindles and K complexes, N3 delta.
- REM: fast EEG, atonia, vivid dreams, more at the end of the night.

:::quiz
Q: Which EEG wave defines N3 sleep?
A: Delta waves.
Q: Which stage contains sleep spindles and K complexes?
A: N2.
Q: Why is REM called paradoxical sleep?
A: The EEG resembles waking while skeletal muscles are paralyzed.
Q: Which wave appears when awake and relaxed with eyes closed?
A: Alpha waves.
Q: How does the share of REM change across the night?
A: REM periods get longer in later cycles.

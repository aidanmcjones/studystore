A good MCAT study schedule is a **repeating week**, not a day-by-day script for months. Fix the structure once, then change only the topics and the amount of practice. This note gives a template you can size to your own test date. It does not prescribe a total number of weeks; see [how long to study](how-long-to-study-for-the-mcat.html) to size that yourself.

## The weekly structure

Each week has four kinds of blocks:

1. **Content review.** Read or watch one topic cluster, then write what you cannot recall.
2. **Practice questions.** Do timed sets on the same topics, then more on mixed topics.
3. **Review.** For each missed question, write why you missed it: content gap, misread, or timing.
4. **Recall.** Short daily flashcard sessions for facts that must stay fresh.

A day off is part of the plan. Without one, the schedule tends to collapse by itself.

## How the mix shifts over time

- **Early weeks:** mostly content review, with short practice sets to check understanding.
- **Middle weeks:** about equal content and practice, with topic-mixed sets.
- **Final weeks:** mostly practice and error review, with full-length exams.

## Full-length exams

Take them under test conditions: same start time as your exam, only allowed breaks, no pausing. Then spend at least as long on review as the exam took. A full-length exam you do not review is mostly wasted.

Spacing is a judgment call. Many students take one per week in the last stretch, but only if there is time to review each properly. If not, take fewer and review deeper.

## A sample week

- **Mon to Thu:** one content block, one practice block, a short recall session.
- **Fri:** mixed practice and error review.
- **Sat:** a long block: either a full-length exam or a catch-up and review session.
- **Sun:** rest, or only recall cards.

Adjust the times to your life; the proportions are what matter.

## What to do when you fall behind

- **Cut content, not review.** Skip low-yield topics before skipping error review.
- **Do not double up days.** Move the missed block to the next free slot.
- **Re-check your target date.** If you are repeatedly behind, the schedule was too ambitious. Rebuild it from what you actually completed.

## Common traps

- **Planning every hour.** Plans this tight break on day three.
- **Only reading.** Without practice questions, recall stays weak.
- **Skipping error review.** The same mistakes repeat.
- **No rest day.** Fatigue lowers the quality of everything else.

## One-minute recap

- Use a repeating week with content, practice, review and recall blocks.
- Shift from content to practice as the test nears.
- Review every exam and every miss.

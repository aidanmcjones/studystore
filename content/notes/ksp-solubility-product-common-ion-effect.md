Ksp is the equilibrium constant for a slightly soluble salt dissolving. It tells you how much dissolves and whether mixing two solutions makes a precipitate. The common ion effect is Le Chatelier's principle applied to it.

## Writing Ksp

For A<sub>m</sub>B<sub>n</sub>(s) &harr; mA<sup>n+</sup> + nB<sup>m-</sup>, Ksp = [A<sup>n+</sup>]<sup>m</sup>[B<sup>m-</sup>]<sup>n</sup>. The solid is left out of the expression.

| Salt | Dissolves to | Ksp in terms of molar solubility s |
|---|---|---|
| AgCl | Ag<sup>+</sup> + Cl<sup>-</sup> | s<sup>2</sup> |
| CaF<sub>2</sub> | Ca<sup>2+</sup> + 2F<sup>-</sup> | (s)(2s)<sup>2</sup> = 4s<sup>3</sup> |
| Ag<sub>2</sub>CrO<sub>4</sub> | 2Ag<sup>+</sup> + CrO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> | (2s)<sup>2</sup>(s) = 4s<sup>3</sup> |

Only compare Ksp values directly for salts with the same ion ratio.

## Q vs Ksp

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Q &lt; Ksp | Unsaturated, more dissolves |
| Q = Ksp | Saturated, at equilibrium |
| Q &gt; Ksp | Supersaturated, precipitate forms |

## Common ion effect

Adding an ion the salt already produces pushes the equilibrium toward the solid, so solubility falls. AgCl is less soluble in 0.1 M NaCl than in pure water. Acidic solutions raise the solubility of salts whose anion is a weak base, such as fluorides and carbonates, because H<sup>+</sup> removes the anion.

## Worked example

AgCl has Ksp = 1.8 x 10<sup>-10</sup>.

- In pure water: s = &radic;(1.8 x 10<sup>-10</sup>) = 1.3 x 10<sup>-5</sup> M.
- In 0.10 M NaCl: [Cl<sup>-</sup>] is about 0.10, so s = Ksp/0.10 = 1.8 x 10<sup>-9</sup> M.
- Solubility drops by a factor of about 7000.

## Common traps

- **Including the solid** in the Ksp expression.
- **Forgetting stoichiometric powers**, and the factor in 2s, when solving for s.
- **Comparing Ksp across different ratios** to rank solubility.
- **Using the pure water s** when a common ion is present.

## One-minute recap

Ksp = product of ion concentrations raised to their coefficients. Q &gt; Ksp precipitates. A common ion lowers solubility. Acid raises solubility of salts with basic anions.

:::quiz
Q: What is the Ksp expression for CaF2?
A: Ksp = [Ca2+][F-]^2.
Q: Q for a mixture is larger than Ksp. What happens?
A: A precipitate forms until Q equals Ksp.
Q: How does adding NaCl change the solubility of AgCl?
A: It decreases, by the common ion effect.
Q: A salt AB has Ksp = 4 x 10^-10. What is its molar solubility?
A: 2 x 10^-5 M, the square root of Ksp.
Q: Why does acid raise the solubility of CaCO3?
A: H+ reacts with carbonate, lowering its concentration and pulling the equilibrium toward dissolving.
:::

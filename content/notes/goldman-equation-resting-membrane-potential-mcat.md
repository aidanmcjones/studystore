The **Nernst equation** gives the equilibrium potential of one ion. The **Goldman equation** gives the membrane potential when several ions can cross, weighting each ion by its **permeability**.

## The idea

V<sub>m</sub> = 61.5 log [ (P<sub>K</sub>[K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>out</sub> + P<sub>Na</sub>[Na<sup>+</sup>]<sub>out</sub> + P<sub>Cl</sub>[Cl<sup>-</sup>]<sub>in</sub>) / (P<sub>K</sub>[K<sup>+</sup>]<sub>in</sub> + P<sub>Na</sub>[Na<sup>+</sup>]<sub>in</sub> + P<sub>Cl</sub>[Cl<sup>-</sup>]<sub>out</sub>) ] mV at 37 C.

Anions have inside and outside swapped. You do not need to memorize it. You need the logic: **the membrane potential is pulled toward the equilibrium potential of the most permeable ion.**

## Typical equilibrium potentials

| Ion | E (mV) |
|---|---|
| K<sup>+</sup> | about -90 |
| Na<sup>+</sup> | about +60 |
| Cl<sup>-</sup> | about -70 |

At rest the neuron is far more permeable to K<sup>+</sup> (leak channels) than Na<sup>+</sup>, so V<sub>m</sub> sits near **-70 mV**, close to E<sub>K</sub> but not equal to it.

## Why it is not exactly E<sub>K</sub>

A small Na<sup>+</sup> leak pulls V<sub>m</sub> up from -90 mV. That is the whole difference.

## Role of Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup> ATPase

It pumps 3 Na<sup>+</sup> out and 2 K<sup>+</sup> in per ATP. It contributes a few mV of negative charge, but its main job is to maintain the gradients that the leak channels use. Block it and the gradients slowly run down, not instantly.

## What shifts V<sub>m</sub>

- Open voltage-gated Na<sup>+</sup> channels: P<sub>Na</sub> rises, V<sub>m</sub> moves toward +60 (depolarization).
- Open K<sup>+</sup> channels more: toward -90 (hyperpolarization).
- Raise extracellular K<sup>+</sup>: E<sub>K</sub> becomes less negative, so the cell depolarizes.

## Common traps

- **Saying V<sub>m</sub> equals E<sub>K</sub>.** It is close, not equal.
- **Using Nernst for a multi-ion membrane.** Nernst needs a membrane permeable to one ion.
- **Blaming the pump alone for the resting potential.** Gradients plus K<sup>+</sup> permeability set it.

## One-minute recap

- Goldman: permeability-weighted version of Nernst.
- Resting potential near -70 mV because K<sup>+</sup> permeability dominates.
- Raising extracellular K<sup>+</sup> depolarizes the cell.

DNA replication is **semiconservative** (each new double helix keeps one old strand) and **bidirectional** from each origin. DNA polymerase only adds nucleotides to a 3' end, so it builds **5' to 3'**. That one rule explains why one strand is continuous and the other is made in pieces.

## Enzymes in order

| Step | Enzyme or protein | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Helicase | Unwinds the helix at the origin, using ATP |
| 2 | Single-strand binding proteins | Keep separated strands from re-annealing |
| 3 | Topoisomerase (gyrase in bacteria) | Relieves supercoiling ahead of the fork |
| 4 | Primase | Makes a short RNA primer, giving polymerase a 3' end |
| 5 | DNA polymerase III (bacteria) | Extends the primer 5' to 3' |
| 6 | DNA polymerase I (bacteria) | Removes RNA primers, fills the gap with DNA |
| 7 | DNA ligase | Seals the remaining nick between fragments |

## Leading vs lagging strand

- **Leading strand:** template runs so the polymerase follows the fork. Continuous synthesis, one primer.
- **Lagging strand:** template runs the other way, so synthesis goes away from the fork. It is made as **Okazaki fragments**, each needing its own primer, joined later by ligase.

## Proofreading

DNA polymerase has **3' to 5' exonuclease** activity. It backs up and removes a mismatched base before continuing. Mismatch repair fixes errors it misses.

## Prokaryote vs eukaryote

| | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Origins | One (oriC) | Many |
| Main polymerases | III (replication), I (primers) | Delta and epsilon (replication), alpha with primase |
| Okazaki fragments | Longer | Shorter |
| Ends | Circular chromosome, no end problem | Linear, telomerase extends telomeres |

## Common traps

- **Saying polymerase builds 3' to 5'.** It reads the template 3' to 5' and builds 5' to 3'.
- **Forgetting the primer.** DNA polymerase cannot start from nothing.
- **Mixing up polymerases I and III.** III does most synthesis; I replaces primers.

## One-minute recap

- Helicase unwinds, primase primes, polymerase extends, ligase seals.
- Leading is continuous; lagging is Okazaki fragments.
- Polymerase proofreads 3' to 5'.

:::quiz
Q: Which enzyme unwinds the DNA helix at the fork?
A: Helicase.
Q: Why is the lagging strand made in fragments?
A: Polymerase only builds 5' to 3', so that strand is synthesized away from the fork in short pieces.
Q: What does primase make?
A: A short RNA primer that gives polymerase a 3' end.
Q: Which enzyme seals the nick between Okazaki fragments?
A: DNA ligase.
Q: What enzyme extends telomeres in eukaryotes?
A: Telomerase.
:::

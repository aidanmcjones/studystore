The **lac operon** is **inducible**: it is normally off and is switched on when lactose is present. The **trp operon** is **repressible**: it is normally on and is switched off when tryptophan is plentiful. Both are negative control systems in which a repressor protein blocks transcription.

## Side by side

| Feature | Lac operon | Trp operon |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Inducible | Repressible |
| Default state | Off | On |
| Pathway | Catabolic (breaks down lactose) | Anabolic (makes tryptophan) |
| Small molecule | Allolactose (inducer) | Tryptophan (corepressor) |
| Effect on repressor | Inducer binds repressor and it leaves the operator | Corepressor binds repressor and it can bind the operator |
| Free repressor | Active, binds operator | Inactive without tryptophan |
| Signal to turn on | Lactose present | Tryptophan scarce |

## How the trp operon works

The trp repressor is made in an inactive form. When tryptophan is abundant, it binds the repressor, the complex binds the operator, and RNA polymerase is blocked. When tryptophan is scarce, the repressor stays inactive, the operator is free, and the enzymes for tryptophan synthesis are made. This makes sense because the cell should not spend energy making an amino acid it already has.

## Mutation logic

- A mutation that stops the trp repressor from binding tryptophan leaves the operon on all the time (constitutive).
- A mutation in the operator that prevents repressor binding also leaves the operon on.
- A mutation that makes the repressor bind the operator regardless of tryptophan keeps the operon off.

## Attenuation

Bacteria also regulate the trp operon by attenuation, because transcription and translation are coupled. When tryptophan is high, the ribosome moves through the leader sequence quickly, a terminator hairpin forms, and transcription stops early.

## Common traps

- **Calling tryptophan an inducer.** It is a corepressor.
- **Saying the trp operon is turned on by its product.** High tryptophan turns it off.
- **Forgetting that repressible operons are usually anabolic and inducible operons catabolic.**

## One-minute recap

- Lac: inducible, off by default, lactose turns it on.
- Trp: repressible, on by default, tryptophan turns it off.
- Inducer releases the repressor; corepressor activates it.

Glycolysis has only four ATP or NADH events. Two steps **spend** ATP, one step **makes** NADH, and two steps **make** ATP, and the last three happen twice per glucose because the sugar splits into two three-carbon pieces.

## The four steps to memorize

| Step | Enzyme | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Glucose to glucose-6-phosphate | Hexokinase (glucokinase in liver) | Uses 1 ATP |
| 3. Fructose-6-phosphate to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate | PFK-1 | Uses 1 ATP |
| 6. G3P to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate | GAPDH | Makes 1 NADH (x2) |
| 7. 1,3-BPG to 3-phosphoglycerate | Phosphoglycerate kinase | Makes 1 ATP (x2) |
| 10. Phosphoenolpyruvate to pyruvate | Pyruvate kinase | Makes 1 ATP (x2) |

## What substrate-level phosphorylation means

The ATP made at steps 7 and 10 comes from transferring a phosphate directly from a high-energy substrate to ADP. No electron transport chain is involved. Oxidative phosphorylation is different: it uses the proton gradient made by the NADH and FADH<sub>2</sub> that the electron transport chain reoxidizes.

## Net yield per glucose

Spent: 2 ATP. Made: 4 ATP. Net: **2 ATP** and **2 NADH**, plus 2 pyruvate.

## Common traps

- **Counting 4 ATP as the net.** Subtract the 2 spent in the investment phase.
- **Forgetting to double the later steps.** One glucose gives two G3P.
- **Saying NADH is made at the ATP steps.** NADH forms at GAPDH only.

## One-minute recap

- ATP used: hexokinase and PFK-1.
- NADH made: GAPDH. ATP made: PGK and pyruvate kinase.
- Net per glucose: 2 ATP, 2 NADH, 2 pyruvate.

:::quiz
Q: Which two glycolysis enzymes use ATP?
A: Hexokinase and PFK-1.
Q: Which enzyme produces NADH in glycolysis?
A: Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH).
Q: Which two enzymes make ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation?
A: Phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase.
Q: What is the net ATP per glucose?
A: 2 ATP (4 made minus 2 spent).
Q: Why are the later steps counted twice?
A: One glucose splits into two three-carbon molecules of G3P.
:::

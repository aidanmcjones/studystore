A **positive delta G** means the reaction is **nonspontaneous as written**: it is **endergonic**, and it will not proceed forward on its own under those conditions. A **negative delta G** is spontaneous (**exergonic**). Zero is equilibrium.

## What it does and does not mean

- **It means** the forward reaction needs an input of free energy.
- **It does not mean** the reaction cannot happen. Coupling, concentration changes and temperature can all change the outcome.
- **It does not mean** slow. Delta G says direction, not rate. Rate depends on the activation energy and on enzymes.

## Delta G vs delta G naught

| Symbol | Conditions | Use |
|---|---|---|
| &Delta;G&deg; | Standard state, all 1 M | Compare reactions, find K |
| &Delta;G | Actual concentrations | Decide the real direction |

&Delta;G = &Delta;G&deg; + RT ln Q. A reaction with a positive &Delta;G&deg; can have a negative &Delta;G if Q is small enough, meaning products are kept low. This is how many metabolic steps run forward in cells. Also &Delta;G&deg; = -RT ln K, so a positive &Delta;G&deg; means K is below 1.

## Coupling to ATP hydrolysis

ATP hydrolysis has a &Delta;G&deg; of about -30 kJ/mol (-7.3 kcal/mol). Free energies of coupled reactions add:

- Glucose + Pi &rarr; glucose-6-phosphate: &Delta;G&deg; about +14 kJ/mol
- ATP &rarr; ADP + Pi: &Delta;G&deg; about -30 kJ/mol
- **Net (hexokinase):** about -16 kJ/mol, spontaneous

The two reactions must share an intermediate, usually a phosphorylated enzyme or substrate. This is what hexokinase does in the first step of [glycolysis](glycolysis-regulation-irreversible-steps-hexokinase-pfk-1.html).

## When a positive delta G can flip with temperature

Use &Delta;G = &Delta;H - T&Delta;S. If &Delta;H is positive and &Delta;S is positive, &Delta;G is positive at low T and negative at high T (ice melting above 0 degrees C). If &Delta;H is positive and &Delta;S is negative, &Delta;G is positive at every temperature. The full sign table is in the [Gibbs free energy note](gibbs-free-energy-delta-g-spontaneity.html).

## Common traps

- **Equating positive &Delta;G with a rate of zero.** It is about direction.
- **Using &Delta;G&deg; to decide direction in a cell.** Use &Delta;G with actual concentrations.
- **Adding coupled reactions' K values.** Add &Delta;G values; multiply K values.
- **Mixing up the sign in electrochemistry.** A galvanic cell has E positive and &Delta;G negative.

## One-minute recap

Positive &Delta;G is endergonic and nonspontaneous as written. It says nothing about speed and it can be overcome by coupling to a strongly negative &Delta;G such as ATP hydrolysis, by keeping Q low, or sometimes by changing temperature.

:::quiz
Q: What does a positive delta G tell you about a reaction?
A: It is nonspontaneous and endergonic as written, so the forward reaction needs free energy input.
Q: Does a positive delta G mean the reaction is slow?
A: No. Delta G gives direction, not rate; rate depends on activation energy.
Q: A reaction has delta G naught of +14 kJ/mol and is coupled to ATP hydrolysis at -30 kJ/mol. What is the net?
A: -16 kJ/mol, which is spontaneous.
Q: How can a reaction with a positive delta G naught run forward in a cell?
A: Delta G also depends on Q. Keeping products low makes Q small and delta G negative.
Q: What is the relationship between a positive delta G naught and K?
A: K is less than 1, because delta G naught = -RT ln K.
:::

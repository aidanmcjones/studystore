**Myoglobin** stores oxygen in muscle. **Hemoglobin** transports oxygen in blood. Their different structures give them different binding curves.

## Comparison

| | Myoglobin | Hemoglobin |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Muscle | Red blood cells |
| Structure | Monomer, one heme | Tetramer (2 alpha, 2 beta), four hemes |
| Curve | Hyperbolic | Sigmoidal |
| Cooperativity | None | Positive |
| O<sub>2</sub> affinity | Higher (lower P50) | Lower (higher P50) |
| Regulated by H<sup>+</sup>, CO<sub>2</sub>, 2,3-BPG | No | Yes |
| Role | Storage and release in working muscle | Loading in lungs, unloading in tissues |

## Why the curves differ

Hemoglobin shifts between a low-affinity **T state** and a high-affinity **R state**. Binding one O<sub>2</sub> favors the R state, so the next oxygens bind more easily. That cooperativity gives the S shape.

Myoglobin has a single binding site, so each molecule binds oxygen independently, giving a hyperbola.

## Why it makes sense physiologically

- In the lungs, high pO<sub>2</sub> loads hemoglobin nearly fully.
- In tissues, hemoglobin gives up oxygen over a range of pO<sub>2</sub>.
- Myoglobin holds oxygen more tightly and releases it mainly when pO<sub>2</sub> falls very low, as in exercising muscle.

## Fetal hemoglobin

Fetal hemoglobin has a higher O<sub>2</sub> affinity than adult hemoglobin because it binds 2,3-BPG less strongly. Oxygen therefore moves from maternal to fetal blood.

## Common traps

- **Giving myoglobin a sigmoidal curve.**
- **Saying myoglobin is lower affinity.** It binds oxygen more tightly.
- **Applying Bohr or 2,3-BPG effects to myoglobin.**

## One-minute recap

- Myoglobin: monomer, hyperbolic, higher affinity, storage.
- Hemoglobin: tetramer, sigmoidal, cooperative, transport.

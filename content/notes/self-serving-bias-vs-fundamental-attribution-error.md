Both are attribution errors, but they answer different questions. The **fundamental attribution error (FAE)** is about **other people's behavior**: we over-credit their disposition and under-credit their situation. **Self-serving bias** is about **your own outcomes**: you take credit for success and blame circumstances for failure. **Ask whose behavior is being explained, then ask what cause is favored.**

## Side by side

| | Fundamental attribution error | Self-serving bias |
|---|---|---|
| Explains | Another person's behavior | Your own success or failure |
| Favored cause | Dispositional (traits) | Internal for success, external for failure |
| Driver | Situation is less visible than the person | Protecting self-esteem |
| Example | "He cut me off because he is a reckless driver." | "I aced the exam because I am smart; I failed because the test was unfair." |

## Fundamental attribution error

When you watch someone act, the person is the most noticeable thing in view and the situation is easy to miss. So a late coworker is "irresponsible" rather than "stuck in a traffic jam." The error is a tilt toward **dispositional attribution** when situational causes are likely. It is also called correspondence bias. It tends to be weaker in collectivist cultures than in individualist ones.

## Self-serving bias

The bias is asymmetric:

- **Success:** attributed to internal factors (ability, effort).
- **Failure:** attributed to external factors (bad luck, a hard test, an unfair grader).

It maintains self-esteem. It is typically weaker in people with depression, who may blame themselves for failure and not credit themselves for success.

## Where actor-observer bias fits

**Actor-observer bias** compares two perspectives: you explain your own behavior by the **situation** but explain others' behavior by their **disposition**. The second half is the FAE. Self-serving bias is a different pattern, since it depends on whether the outcome was good or bad. See [FAE vs actor-observer bias](fundamental-attribution-error-vs-actor-observer-bias.html).

## Common traps

- **Applying FAE to yourself.** If the sentence is about the speaker's own behavior, it is not FAE.
- **Missing the outcome.** Self-serving bias needs a success or failure to explain.
- **Mixing up the two when the speaker blames others.** "My partner failed because they are lazy" is FAE. "I failed because the exam was unfair" is self-serving bias.

## One-minute recap

- FAE: others, disposition over situation.
- Self-serving bias: self, credit for wins, excuses for losses.
- Actor-observer: self gets the situation, others get the disposition.

Sample: MCAT CARS Humanities Passage Practice Questions

In the marginalia passage above, which statement best expresses the author's central thesis?

  1. Readers today should annotate the library books they borrow, because their notes will one day help historians.
  2. Fermat's famous marginal note shows that annotated books can be more valuable than clean ones.
  3. Libraries should preserve and record old annotations as evidence of reading rather than treat them as damage.
  4. Digital reading services have made printed books obsolete as a source for the history of reading.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Libraries should preserve and record old annotations as evidence of reading rather than treat them as damage.

C states the claim the author narrows to in paragraph 4 and supports throughout: old annotations are evidence of how people read and should be recorded, not erased. A is too broad and conflicts with paragraph 4, which accepts reasons to discourage new marks. B is too narrow, since paragraph 3 says the point is not famous annotators. D distorts paragraph 5, which calls the printed record fragile, not obsolete.

Sample: MCAT CARS Social Science Passage Practice Questions

In the queue passage above, which statement best expresses the author's central thesis?

  1. Queues are an inefficient way to allocate service and should be replaced by systems that cut average waiting time.
  2. A line is sustained by a shared norm of equal treatment, so changes to it should weigh its social meaning, not only its efficiency.
  3. People resent line-cutters mainly because even short delays add up to a large total cost for everyone waiting.
  4. Paid fast lanes show that people no longer care whether service is distributed equally.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A line is sustained by a shared norm of equal treatment, so changes to it should weigh its social meaning, not only its efficiency.

B joins the claim of paragraphs 2 to 4, that the line is an order of equals kept up by its members, with the advice of paragraph 6, to ask what waiting means before redesigning it. A reverses the author, who calls throughput models useful but incomplete. C is the explanation paragraph 4 rejects. D distorts paragraph 5, which says the fast lane complicates the account without overturning it.

MCAT Sleep and Consciousness Practice Questions: Stages, Rhythms and Dreams

Quick concept primer

Consciousness ranges from alert wakefulness through sleep, and the EEG tracks the shift. Alert waking shows fast, low-amplitude beta waves; relaxed waking with eyes closed shows alpha. Sleep stage N1 brings theta waves, N2 adds sleep spindles and K complexes, and N3 (slow-wave sleep) is dominated by high-amplitude delta waves. REM sleep has a fast, wake-like EEG, rapid eye movements, vivid dreams and near paralysis of skeletal muscles, which is why it is called paradoxical sleep. A cycle lasts about 90 minutes; N3 is concentrated early in the night and REM periods lengthen toward morning.

The sleep-wake cycle follows a circadian rhythm set by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the hypothalamus. Light detected by the retina reaches the SCN, which controls melatonin release from the pineal gland; darkness raises melatonin. Major disorders include insomnia, narcolepsy, sleep apnea, sleepwalking and REM sleep behavior disorder. Dream theories range from Freud's wish fulfillment to activation-synthesis and memory-processing views. Hypnosis is explained either as dissociation or as social role-playing, and meditation tends to increase alpha and theta activity.

Traps: sleepwalking and night terrors arise from N3, not REM, and REM's EEG looks awake even though the body is nearly paralyzed.

Question 1

A sleep lab summarizes four segments of one participant's overnight recording.

SegmentDominant EEG patternOther observations
1Alpha waves, about 10 HzEyes closed, lying quietly before sleep
2Theta background with sleep spindles and K complexesSlow eye movements absent
3High-amplitude delta waves, about 1 to 2 HzVery hard to wake
4Low-amplitude, mixed fast activityRapid eye movements, minimal muscle tone

During which segment is an episode of sleepwalking most likely to begin?

  1. Segment 1
  2. Segment 2
  3. Segment 3
  4. Segment 4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Segment 3

Segment 3 shows delta-dominated slow-wave sleep (N3), the stage from which sleepwalking and night terrors usually arise. Segment 4 is REM, and during REM the near paralysis of skeletal muscles normally prevents walking. Segment 2 is N2, marked by spindles and K complexes, and segment 1 is relaxed wakefulness.

Question 2

A participant sleeps a full 8 hours (480 minutes). The lab reports minutes of N3 and REM in each 2-hour block of the night.

BlockN3 (min)REM (min)
Hours 0 to 25010
Hours 2 to 42520
Hours 4 to 6530
Hours 6 to 8040

About what percentage of total sleep time was REM sleep?

  1. 10 percent
  2. 21 percent
  3. 33 percent
  4. 50 percent
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 21 percent

Total REM is 10 + 20 + 30 + 40 = 100 minutes, and 100/480 = 0.208, about 21 percent. The table also shows the typical pattern, with N3 front-loaded early in the night and REM periods growing later. The 33 percent and 50 percent choices would require far more REM than the table lists.

Question 3

A student scrolls on a brightly lit phone screen in bed late each evening. Through its effect on the circadian system, what is this light exposure most likely to do?

  1. Suppress melatonin release from the pineal gland and tend to delay sleep onset
  2. Increase melatonin release from the pineal gland and make the student sleepier
  3. Shorten REM periods directly without affecting the circadian clock
  4. Shift the circadian clock earlier, so the student wakes earlier the next morning
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Suppress melatonin release from the pineal gland and tend to delay sleep onset

Retinal cells signal light to the suprachiasmatic nucleus, which in turn reduces melatonin release from the pineal gland. Light late in the evening suppresses melatonin and shifts the clock later, delaying sleep onset. Melatonin rises in darkness, not light, so choice B is reversed, and evening light delays the clock rather than advancing it (D).

Question 4

A 19-year-old reports overwhelming daytime sleepiness, brief episodes of sudden muscle weakness when she laughs, and vivid dreamlike images as she falls asleep. A sleep study shows that she enters REM sleep within a few minutes of falling asleep. Which disorder best fits this picture?

  1. Obstructive sleep apnea
  2. Insomnia
  3. Somnambulism
  4. Narcolepsy
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Narcolepsy

Excessive daytime sleepiness, cataplexy triggered by emotion, hypnagogic hallucinations and REM onset soon after falling asleep are hallmarks of narcolepsy, often linked to loss of orexin (hypocretin) neurons in the hypothalamus. Insomnia is trouble falling or staying asleep, somnambulism is sleepwalking from N3, and apnea causes sleepiness through repeated breathing interruptions.

Question 5

A 68-year-old man punches and kicks during sleep, and when woken he describes a dream of fighting off an attacker that matches his movements. What is the most likely explanation?

  1. Night terrors arising from stage N3
  2. Sleepwalking arising from stage N2
  3. Loss of the normal muscle atonia of REM sleep
  4. Narcolepsy with cataplexy during waking hours
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Loss of the normal muscle atonia of REM sleep

Acting out a vivid, recalled dream points to REM sleep behavior disorder, in which the brainstem mechanism that normally paralyzes skeletal muscles during REM fails. Night terrors arise from N3 and usually leave little dream recall, and sleepwalking also comes from N3. Narcolepsy involves cataplexy while awake, not acting out dreams.

Question 6

A researcher argues that dreams arise when the cortex tries to build a story out of random bursts of activity sent up from the pons during REM sleep. Which theory of dreaming does this describe?

  1. Freud's wish fulfillment theory
  2. Information-processing theory
  3. Neurocognitive theory
  4. Activation-synthesis theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Activation-synthesis theory

Activation-synthesis theory holds that brainstem activity during REM is random and that the cortex synthesizes it into a dream narrative after the fact. Freud saw dreams as disguised wishes with hidden latent content, information-processing theory links dreams to sorting and consolidating the day's memories, and neurocognitive theory ties dreams to waking cognition and brain development.

Question 7

A psychologist argues that hypnotized people are not in an altered state at all, but are acting out the role they expect a hypnotized person to play, shaped by the hypnotist's suggestions and their own expectations. This view is best described as:

  1. Dissociation theory
  2. Social-cognitive (role) theory
  3. Activation-synthesis theory
  4. Psychoanalytic theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Social-cognitive (role) theory

The social-cognitive, or role, theory treats hypnotic behavior as people fulfilling the social role of a hypnotized subject. Dissociation theory instead says hypnosis splits consciousness into separate streams, as in the hidden observer idea. Activation-synthesis is a theory of dreaming, and psychoanalytic theory concerns unconscious conflict, not hypnosis.

Question 8

Each of the following is typical of REM sleep EXCEPT:

  1. An EEG dominated by high-amplitude delta waves
  2. Near paralysis of most skeletal muscles
  3. Vivid, story-like dreams
  4. An EEG that resembles wakefulness
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. An EEG dominated by high-amplitude delta waves

High-amplitude delta waves define N3, slow-wave sleep, not REM. During REM the EEG is fast and low in amplitude, much like an awake brain, while skeletal muscles are nearly paralyzed and the most vivid, narrative dreams occur. That mismatch between an active brain and an inactive body is why REM is called paradoxical sleep.

Question 9

An EEG study compares volunteers sitting quietly with eyes open and alert to the same volunteers during focused meditation. Which change during meditation is most consistent with commonly reported findings?

  1. Sleep spindles and K complexes appear
  2. Delta waves become dominant, as in slow-wave sleep
  3. Beta activity increases above the alert waking level
  4. Alpha and theta activity increase
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Alpha and theta activity increase

Meditation is a relaxed but awake state, and it is most often associated with more alpha and theta activity than alert waking. Spindles and K complexes mark N2 sleep, and dominant delta waves mark N3, so those would mean the person had fallen asleep. Beta activity is typical of alert, focused waking, not relaxation.

Question 10

A man who snores loudly wakes many times a night gasping. A sleep study shows that during each pause in airflow, his chest and abdomen keep making breathing movements. Which condition is most likely?

  1. Central sleep apnea
  2. Narcolepsy
  3. Obstructive sleep apnea
  4. REM sleep behavior disorder
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Obstructive sleep apnea

Continued breathing effort without airflow means the airway is physically blocked, which defines obstructive sleep apnea; loud snoring fits a narrowed upper airway. In central sleep apnea the brainstem stops sending the signal to breathe, so breathing effort pauses too. Narcolepsy and REM sleep behavior disorder do not cause these airflow pauses.

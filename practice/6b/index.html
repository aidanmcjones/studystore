Sample: MCAT Cognition and Problem Solving Practice Questions

A researcher pours juice from a short, wide cup into a tall, narrow cup while a child watches. The child now insists the tall cup holds more juice. The child also cannot yet explain the scene from the viewpoint of a doll seated across the table. Which Piaget stage best fits this child?

  1. Preoperational
  2. Sensorimotor
  3. Concrete operational
  4. Formal operational
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Preoperational

Failing conservation of liquid volume and showing egocentrism are the classic markers of the preoperational stage, roughly ages 2 to 7. Sensorimotor infants are still building object permanence, concrete operational children pass conservation tasks, and formal operational thinking adds abstract reasoning.

Sample: MCAT Memory Encoding, Retrieval and Forgetting Practice Questions

Participants heard 15-word lists. In condition 1 they recalled each list immediately. In condition 2 they counted backward by threes for 30 seconds before recalling. Compared with condition 1, which change is most likely in condition 2?

  1. Recall of the first few words drops, while recall of the last few words is unchanged
  2. Recall of the last few words drops, while recall of the first few words is largely unchanged
  3. Recall of all positions rises because the delay allows consolidation
  4. Recall of middle words rises above recall of the first words
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Recall of the last few words drops, while recall of the first few words is largely unchanged

The recency effect reflects words still held in short-term memory, and a filled delay displaces them, so recency disappears. The primacy effect reflects early words that received extra rehearsal and reached long-term memory, so it survives the delay. Choice A reverses the two effects, and a 30 second distractor task does not improve recall.

Sample: MCAT Sleep Stages and Circadian Rhythm: 10 Questions

A student scrolls on a brightly lit phone screen in bed late each evening. Through its effect on the circadian system, what is this light exposure most likely to do?

  1. Suppress melatonin release from the pineal gland and tend to delay sleep onset
  2. Increase melatonin release from the pineal gland and make the student sleepier
  3. Shorten REM periods directly without affecting the circadian clock
  4. Shift the circadian clock earlier, so the student wakes earlier the next morning
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Suppress melatonin release from the pineal gland and tend to delay sleep onset

Retinal cells signal light to the suprachiasmatic nucleus, which in turn reduces melatonin release from the pineal gland. Light late in the evening suppresses melatonin and shifts the clock later, delaying sleep onset. Melatonin rises in darkness, not light, so choice B is reversed, and evening light delays the clock rather than advancing it (D).

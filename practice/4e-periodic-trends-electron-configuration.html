Quick concept primer

Most periodic trends come from two quantities: effective nuclear charge and the shell holding the valence electrons. Moving right across a period adds protons to the same shell, so effective nuclear charge rises, atoms shrink, and ionization energy and electronegativity generally climb. Moving down a group adds a new shell, so atoms grow and their outer electrons are held less tightly. Among ions with the same electron count, the one with more protons is smallest.

Electrons fill orbitals from low to high energy, one electron per orbital of a subshell before any pairing, with paired electrons of opposite spin. Chromium and copper move one 4s electron into 3d to reach a half-filled or filled d subshell. Transition metals lose 4s electrons before 3d electrons when they form cations. A species with any unpaired electron is paramagnetic. A sharp jump in successive ionization energies marks the move into a core shell, which reveals the valence count. Traps: the dips in ionization energy at groups 13 and 16, and removing 3d electrons before 4s.

Question 1

Successive ionization energies of an unknown period 3 element are listed below.

IonizationEnergy (kJ/mol)
First600
Second1700
Third2600
Fourth11800

To which group does the element most likely belong?

  1. Group 1
  2. Group 13
  3. Group 2
  4. Group 15
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Group 13

The fourth ionization energy is about 4.5 times the third, a jump of 9200 kJ/mol, far larger than the earlier steps of 1100 and 900 kJ/mol. That jump means the fourth electron comes from a core shell, so the atom has three valence electrons and sits in group 13. A group 1 or group 2 element would show the big jump after the first or second electron.

Question 2

Iron has atomic number 26. How many unpaired electrons does a ground-state Fe3+ ion have?

  1. 3
  2. 1
  3. 4
  4. 5
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 5

Neutral iron is [Ar]4s23d6. Forming the cation removes both 4s electrons and then one 3d electron, giving [Ar]3d5. By Hund's rule each of the five d orbitals holds one electron, so there are 5 unpaired electrons. Choice C is the count for Fe2+, which keeps six d electrons.

Question 3

A ground-state chromium atom (atomic number 24) is examined. How many of its electrons occupy 3d orbitals?

  1. 5
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 3
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 5

Chromium shifts one electron from 4s into 3d, giving one 4s electron and five 3d electrons, a half-filled d subshell that is lower in energy than the simple filling-order prediction of two 4s and four 3d electrons. Choice B is that naive prediction, and choices C and D do not fit the 6 electrons beyond the argon core.

Question 4

Which of these atoms has the largest atomic radius?

  1. Sodium
  2. Bromine
  3. Potassium
  4. Calcium
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Potassium

Potassium is in period 4, so its valence electron is in a higher shell than sodium's, and it lies at the far left of its period, where effective nuclear charge is lowest. Calcium and bromine are in the same period as potassium but have more protons pulling the same shell inward, so both are smaller. Sodium is one shell smaller.

Question 5

Moving from sodium to chlorine across period 3, each of the following generally increases EXCEPT:

  1. Atomic radius
  2. Effective nuclear charge felt by valence electrons
  3. Electronegativity
  4. Nonmetallic character
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Atomic radius

Across a period, protons are added while the valence shell stays the same, so effective nuclear charge rises and pulls the electron cloud inward, making atoms smaller. That same stronger pull raises electronegativity and moves the elements from metals such as sodium toward nonmetals such as chlorine. Only atomic radius decreases.

Question 6

Oxygen has a lower first ionization energy than nitrogen even though it lies to the right of nitrogen. What best explains this?

  1. Oxygen has a smaller effective nuclear charge than nitrogen
  2. Oxygen's outermost electron is in the third shell
  3. Oxygen has a larger atomic radius than nitrogen
  4. One of oxygen's 2p orbitals holds a pair of electrons, and repulsion within that pair makes one of them easier to remove
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. One of oxygen's 2p orbitals holds a pair of electrons, and repulsion within that pair makes one of them easier to remove

Nitrogen has three unpaired 2p electrons, one per orbital. Oxygen's fourth 2p electron must pair up in an orbital that already holds one, and the extra repulsion between the paired electrons lowers the energy needed to remove one. Oxygen actually has a higher effective nuclear charge and a slightly smaller radius, and both atoms use the second shell for valence electrons.

Question 7

Which ion is diamagnetic in its ground state?

  1. Ti2+
  2. Zn2+
  3. Mn2+
  4. Cr3+
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Zn2+

Zinc is [Ar]4s23d10, and losing the two 4s electrons leaves a full 3d10 subshell with every electron paired, so Zn2+ is diamagnetic. Ti2+ is d2, Mn2+ is d5 and Cr3+ is d3, and each has unpaired d electrons, so each is paramagnetic.

Question 8

A sulfide ion, a chloride ion, a potassium ion and a calcium ion all hold 18 electrons. Which of these ions is smallest?

  1. The potassium ion
  2. The chloride ion
  3. The calcium ion
  4. The sulfide ion
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The calcium ion

With the electron count fixed at 18, size is set by nuclear charge. Calcium has 20 protons, the most of the four, so it draws the electron cloud in most tightly and its 2+ ion is smallest. Sulfide, with only 16 protons for 18 electrons, is the largest, and chloride and potassium fall in between.

Question 9

An element is a soft, silvery solid that is cut easily with a knife, reacts vigorously with cold water to release hydrogen gas and form a strongly basic solution, and forms only a +1 ion in its compounds. Which element fits this description?

  1. Sodium
  2. Magnesium
  3. Aluminum
  4. Silicon
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Sodium

These are the traits of an alkali metal: one valence electron that is lost easily, giving a +1 ion and a fast reaction with water that yields hydrogen and hydroxide. Magnesium forms a +2 ion and reacts only slowly with cold water, aluminum forms a +3 ion and is protected by an oxide layer, and silicon is a metalloid.

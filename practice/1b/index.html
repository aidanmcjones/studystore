Sample: DNA Replication and Repair: 8 Free MCAT Questions

E. coli grown for many generations in medium containing 15N are transferred to medium containing only 14N and allowed to divide exactly twice. What fraction of the DNA duplexes is expected to be hybrid (one 15N strand and one 14N strand)?

  1. One quarter
  2. One half
  3. All of them
  4. None of them
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. One half

Replication is semiconservative. After one division, every duplex is 15N/14N. Each of those separates into one 15N strand and one 14N strand; each strand gets a new 14N partner in the second division. The result is four duplexes from one original: two hybrids (15N/14N) and two fully light (14N/14N), so half are hybrid.

Sample: MCAT Epigenetics, Chromatin and DNA Methylation Practice Questions

Which statement best describes heterochromatin?

  1. Loosely packed and transcriptionally active
  2. Tightly packed and generally transcriptionally silent
  3. Found only in prokaryotes
  4. Packed without histones
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Tightly packed and generally transcriptionally silent

Heterochromatin is condensed, so transcription factors and RNA polymerase have limited access to the DNA and gene expression is low. Euchromatin is the loosely packed, active form. Both forms contain histones, and prokaryotes do not have nucleosomes in the same way.

Sample: MCAT Gene Regulation, PCR and Molecular Techniques Practice Questions

A cell is treated with a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor. Which effect is expected?

  1. No change in chromatin
  2. Increased histone acetylation, a more open chromatin structure and increased transcription of many genes
  3. Decreased histone acetylation and gene silencing
  4. Increased DNA methylation at all promoters
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Increased histone acetylation, a more open chromatin structure and increased transcription of many genes

Acetylation of lysine residues on histone tails neutralizes their positive charge and weakens the interaction with the negatively charged DNA, loosening chromatin. HDACs remove acetyl groups, so inhibiting them leaves histones hyperacetylated and favors a more open, transcriptionally active state. This is why HDAC inhibitors are studied as cancer drugs.

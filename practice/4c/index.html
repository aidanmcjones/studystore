Sample: MCAT Capacitors Practice Questions: Capacitance, Dielectrics, Series and Parallel

The separation between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor is doubled while the plate area stays the same. What happens to the capacitance?

  1. It is halved
  2. It is doubled
  3. It is unchanged
  4. It is quadrupled
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. It is halved

Capacitance is inversely proportional to plate separation (C = epsilon_0 A / d), so doubling d halves C. Capacitance increases only if the area grows, the plates move closer, or a dielectric is added.

Sample: MCAT Circuits Practice Questions: Ohm's Law, Resistors and Kirchhoff

A steady current of 4.0 mA runs through a heating element for 20 s. Roughly how many electrons pass a given point during that time? Use e = 1.6 × 10−19 C.

  1. 1.3 × 10−20
  2. 5.0 × 1014
  3. 5.0 × 1017
  4. 8.0 × 1017
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 5.0 × 1017

The charge is Q = It = (0.0040 A)(20 s) = 0.080 C. Dividing by the charge on one electron gives 0.080 / (1.6 × 10−19) = 5.0 × 1017 electrons. The 5.0 × 1014 choice forgets to convert milliamperes to amperes, 1.3 × 10−20 multiplies by e instead of dividing, and 8.0 × 1017 drops the 1.6.

Sample: MCAT Electrochemistry Practice Questions: Galvanic and Electrolytic Cells

A student builds a galvanic cell from a silver electrode in Ag+ solution and a nickel electrode in Ni2+ solution. Take E° for Ag+/Ag as +0.80 V and for Ni2+/Ni as −0.25 V. What is the standard cell potential?

  1. 0.55 V
  2. 1.85 V
  3. 0.30 V
  4. 1.05 V
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. 1.05 V

Silver has the higher reduction potential, so it is the cathode and nickel is oxidized at the anode. Ecell = 0.80 − (−0.25) = 1.05 V. The 0.55 V choice adds the two values with the wrong sign, 1.85 V doubles the silver potential because two Ag+ ions are reduced per Ni, which is wrong because potentials are intensive, and 0.30 V is not a valid combination.

Quick concept primer

Sociologists use several paradigms, each asking a different question about the same facts. Functionalism (Durkheim, Parsons, Merton) is a macro view that asks how parts of society contribute to stability; Merton distinguished manifest (intended) functions, latent (unintended) functions and dysfunctions. Durkheim contrasted mechanical solidarity, based on similarity in simple societies, with organic solidarity, based on interdependence in specialized ones. Conflict theory (Marx, Weber, Dahrendorf, Mills) asks who benefits from an arrangement and how groups compete for scarce resources and power.

Symbolic interactionism (Mead, Cooley, Blumer, Goffman) is a micro view of how people create and interpret meaning in everyday interaction. Social constructionism (Berger and Luckmann) asks how categories that seem natural, such as childhood or race, come to be shared and taken for granted. Rational choice and exchange theory (Homans, Blau) model behavior as weighing rewards against costs. Feminist theory examines how gender structures power, work and opportunity, including unpaid work at home.

Traps: "who benefits" points to conflict theory and "what purpose does it serve" to functionalism. A latent function can be positive; harm is a dysfunction. Micro studies of meaning point to interactionism, while studies of how a category came to exist point to constructionism.

Question 1

A study of public high schools finds that, in addition to teaching academic subjects, schools are where many students meet future spouses and form friendship networks that last into adulthood. From a functionalist perspective, the partner-matching role of schools is best described as a:

  1. manifest function.
  2. latent function.
  3. dysfunction.
  4. social construction.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. latent function.

Merton defined latent functions as unintended and often unrecognized consequences of an institution. Schools are designed to educate, which is their manifest function, but matching future partners is a byproduct. A dysfunction is a consequence that disrupts social stability, and social construction is a different paradigm's concept.

Question 2

A researcher argues that a city's zoning rules, written largely by property-owning residents, keep low-income families out of the best-funded school districts and so preserve the advantages of wealthy households. This argument most directly reflects which perspective?

  1. Symbolic interactionism
  2. Functionalism
  3. Rational choice theory
  4. Conflict theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Conflict theory

Conflict theory explains social arrangements as the outcome of competition between groups with unequal power, asking who benefits. Here a powerful group shapes rules that protect its access to a scarce resource. Functionalism would ask how zoning promotes stability, interactionism would study face-to-face meaning, and rational choice would model individual cost-benefit decisions.

Question 3

Over six months, an ethnographer records how members of an online forum for new parents negotiate what counts as a "good enough" parent, and how individual posters change what they share after receiving praise or criticism from others. Which perspective guides this research most directly?

  1. Functionalism
  2. Conflict theory
  3. Symbolic interactionism
  4. Exchange theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Symbolic interactionism

Symbolic interactionism focuses on how people construct and interpret shared meanings in interaction and adjust their behavior in response to others, studied at the micro level. The ethnography tracks how a definition is negotiated and how individuals respond to feedback. Functionalism and conflict theory are macro perspectives about stability and power.

Question 4

A historian of the family traces how "adolescence" came to be recognized as a distinct life stage with its own expectations only after compulsory schooling and child labor laws spread, and how the category then came to seem natural. Which perspective best fits this analysis?

  1. Functionalism
  2. Social constructionism
  3. Exchange theory
  4. Conflict theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Social constructionism

Social constructionism examines how categories are created through shared social processes and then come to be treated as objective features of reality. The study shows a life stage emerging from institutional change and becoming taken for granted. Exchange theory models cost-benefit choices, and neither functionalism nor conflict theory centers on how the category itself was produced.

Question 5

A researcher models a volunteer's choice using exchange theory, assigning point values to the expected rewards and costs of each option.

OptionExpected rewardsExpected costs
Stay at Clinic A4025
Move to Clinic B5545
Stop volunteering100

Which option does exchange theory predict, and why?

  1. Move to Clinic B, because it offers the largest rewards
  2. Stay at Clinic A, because it yields the largest net benefit, 15 points
  3. Stop volunteering, because it has the lowest cost
  4. No prediction, because all options have equal net benefit
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Stay at Clinic A, because it yields the largest net benefit, 15 points

Exchange theory predicts people choose the option with the greatest profit, rewards minus costs. Clinic A gives 40 minus 25, or 15; Clinic B gives 55 minus 45, or 10; stopping gives 10 minus 0, or 10. Choosing by rewards alone or by costs alone ignores the other half of the calculation.

Question 6

A hypothetical time-use survey of dual-earner couples reports average weekly hours.

GroupPaid workUnpaid household and care work
Women3822
Men4210

Which perspective most directly frames this pattern as a "second shift," and how many more total hours of work per week do women report than men?

  1. Feminist theory; 8 hours
  2. Functionalism; 12 hours
  3. Feminist theory; 12 hours
  4. Rational choice theory; 4 hours
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Feminist theory; 8 hours

Women report 38 plus 22, or 60 total hours, and men 42 plus 10, or 52, a gap of 8 hours. Hochschild's concept of the second shift comes from feminist sociology, which analyzes how gender shapes the division of paid and unpaid labor. 12 hours is the gap in unpaid work alone, ignoring that men report more paid hours.

Question 7

Each of the following pairs a theorist with the perspective most associated with that theorist EXCEPT:

  1. Emile Durkheim and functionalism
  2. Karl Marx and conflict theory
  3. George Herbert Mead and symbolic interactionism
  4. George Homans and conflict theory
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. George Homans and conflict theory

Homans, along with Blau, is a founder of social exchange theory, which models interaction as an exchange of rewards and costs. Durkheim is a founding functionalist, Marx is the central figure of conflict theory, and Mead's work on the self and interaction is the foundation of symbolic interactionism, a term coined by his student Blumer.

Question 8

After a public scandal in a small town, residents gather, condemn the wrongdoing and reaffirm their shared expectations of honesty. Which statement would a functionalist most likely make about this event?

  1. Deviance and the reaction to it can strengthen cohesion by clarifying moral boundaries.
  2. The label of deviance was imposed by powerful groups to protect their interests.
  3. Residents condemned the act only because doing so brought them personal rewards that outweighed the costs.
  4. The meaning of the scandal was negotiated differently in each face-to-face conversation.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Deviance and the reaction to it can strengthen cohesion by clarifying moral boundaries.

Durkheim argued that deviance can serve a function: the community's response marks the limits of acceptable behavior and draws members together. Choice B reflects conflict theory and labeling, choice C reflects rational choice theory, and choice D reflects symbolic interactionism, so only choice A expresses the functionalist view.

Question 9

A sociologist compares a small farming village, where nearly everyone does similar work and shares the same beliefs, with a large city where people hold highly specialized jobs and depend on one another's work. In Durkheim's terms, which type of solidarity characterizes each?

  1. Village: organic; city: mechanical
  2. Village: organic; city: organic
  3. Village: mechanical; city: organic
  4. Village: mechanical; city: mechanical
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Village: mechanical; city: organic

Durkheim described mechanical solidarity as cohesion based on similarity and shared beliefs, typical of small traditional communities, and organic solidarity as cohesion based on interdependence created by a complex division of labor, typical of modern urban societies. The names are often reversed by students, which is the trap in choice A.

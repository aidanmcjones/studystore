Sample: MCAT Cancer, Oncogenes, Tumor Suppressors and Apoptosis Practice Questions

A mutation changes a proto-oncogene so that it is permanently active. How many mutated alleles are generally enough to promote uncontrolled growth?

  1. Two, because both alleles must be mutated
  2. One, because the mutation is gain of function
  3. Zero, because proto-oncogenes are never involved in cancer
  4. Three, one for each checkpoint
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. One, because the mutation is gain of function

An oncogene arises from a gain-of-function mutation, so a single mutated copy can drive growth even if the other allele is normal. Tumor suppressors typically behave in the opposite way and require loss of both functional copies.

Sample: MCAT Cell Cycle, Mitosis and Meiosis Practice Questions

In a mammalian species, a diploid cell in G1 contains 6 pg of nuclear DNA. Assuming no DNA is lost, what DNA content is expected in a primary spermatocyte at metaphase I, a secondary spermatocyte at metaphase II, and a spermatid, in that order?

  1. 6 pg, 3 pg, 1.5 pg
  2. 12 pg, 12 pg, 6 pg
  3. 12 pg, 6 pg, 3 pg
  4. 6 pg, 6 pg, 3 pg
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 12 pg, 6 pg, 3 pg

After S phase the primary spermatocyte has doubled its DNA to 12 pg. Meiosis I splits homologs between two cells, so each secondary spermatocyte holds 6 pg, and meiosis II splits chromatids, leaving 3 pg in each spermatid, half the G1 amount. Choices A and D forget the S phase doubling, and B forgets that meiosis I already halves the DNA.

Sample: MCAT Embryology, Germ Layers and Stem Cell Potency Practice Questions

Which sequence lists events of early human development in the correct order?

  1. Fertilization, gastrulation, cleavage, implantation, neurulation
  2. Cleavage, fertilization, blastocyst formation, neurulation, gastrulation
  3. Fertilization, implantation, cleavage, blastocyst formation, gastrulation
  4. Fertilization, cleavage, blastocyst formation, implantation, gastrulation, neurulation
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Fertilization, cleavage, blastocyst formation, implantation, gastrulation, neurulation

The zygote forms at fertilization and then undergoes cleavage divisions to a morula and a blastocyst, which implants in the uterine wall about a week after fertilization. Gastrulation then creates the three germ layers, and neurulation follows as the notochord induces the neural plate. Each other option places cleavage, implantation or gastrulation out of order.

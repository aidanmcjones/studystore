Sample: MCAT Action Potential and Synaptic Transmission Practice Questions

Tetrodotoxin blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels. When applied to a nerve axon, which effect is expected?

  1. Action potentials fail to be generated, while the resting membrane potential is largely unchanged
  2. The resting membrane potential collapses to 0 mV
  3. Action potentials become larger
  4. The refractory period disappears
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Action potentials fail to be generated, while the resting membrane potential is largely unchanged

The resting potential depends mainly on K+ leak channels, so blocking the voltage-gated Na+ channels barely changes it. Without those channels, a depolarizing stimulus cannot trigger the regenerative Na+ influx of the rising phase, and no action potential occurs. Local anesthetics such as lidocaine work by a similar mechanism.

Sample: MCAT Autonomic Nervous System Practice Questions: Sympathetic vs Parasympathetic

Which neurotransmitter is released by parasympathetic postganglionic neurons onto their target organs?

  1. Norepinephrine
  2. Epinephrine
  3. Dopamine
  4. Acetylcholine
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Acetylcholine

Both parasympathetic preganglionic and postganglionic neurons are cholinergic. The postganglionic neuron acts on muscarinic receptors at the target. Sympathetic postganglionic neurons usually release norepinephrine instead.

Sample: MCAT Endocrine System, Hormone Axes and Feedback Practice Questions

Which of the following hormones acts mainly through an intracellular receptor and alters gene transcription?

  1. Epinephrine
  2. Glucagon
  3. Insulin
  4. Cortisol
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Cortisol

Cortisol is a steroid hormone; it is lipid soluble, crosses the plasma membrane and binds a cytoplasmic receptor that moves to the nucleus to regulate transcription. Insulin, glucagon and epinephrine bind receptors on the cell surface and act through second messengers or receptor kinases, producing faster effects.

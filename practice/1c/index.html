Sample questions

Sample: MCAT Population Genetics Practice Questions: Hardy-Weinberg, Drift and Selection

In a meadow of 2,000 plants, flower color is controlled by one gene with two alleles, and white flowers are recessive. A survey counts 180 white-flowered plants. Assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, about how many plants in the meadow are heterozygous?

  1. 180
  2. 420
  3. 840
  4. 980
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. 840

White plants are homozygous recessive, so q2 = 180/2000 = 0.09, giving q = 0.3 and p = 0.7. Heterozygotes are 2pq = 2 x 0.7 x 0.3 = 0.42 of the population, or 0.42 x 2000 = 840 plants. The 980 choice is p2 (homozygous dominant), and 420 forgets the factor of 2 in 2pq.

Sample: MCAT Mendelian Genetics, Pedigrees and Linkage Practice Questions

In a plant, purple flowers (P) are dominant to white (p) and round seeds (R) are dominant to wrinkled (r). The two genes are on different chromosomes. Two PpRr plants are crossed. What fraction of the offspring is expected to have purple flowers and wrinkled seeds?

  1. 1/16
  2. 3/16
  3. 9/16
  4. 1/4
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. 3/16

Treat each gene separately. Pp by Pp gives purple in 3/4 of offspring, and Rr by Rr gives wrinkled (rr) in 1/4. Because the genes assort independently, multiply: 3/4 x 1/4 = 3/16. The value 9/16 is the fraction with both dominant phenotypes, 1/16 is the doubly recessive class, and 1/4 ignores the flower gene.

Sample: MCAT Speciation, Reproductive Isolation and Phylogenetic Trees Practice Questions

Two populations of squirrels are separated when a canyon forms, and over time they can no longer interbreed. This is an example of

  1. sympatric speciation
  2. allopatric speciation
  3. convergent evolution
  4. genetic drift without selection
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. allopatric speciation

A geographic barrier that separates populations, followed by divergence and reproductive isolation, defines allopatric speciation. Sympatric speciation occurs without geographic separation. Convergent evolution produces similar traits in unrelated species, which is not described here.

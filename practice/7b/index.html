Sample: MCAT Conformity, Obedience and Group Behavior Practice Questions

In a follow-up to a line-judgment conformity study, participants heard the unanimous wrong answers of the group but then wrote their own answers privately, knowing no one would see them. Conformity dropped sharply. This result indicates that conformity in the public condition was driven mainly by

  1. normative social influence
  2. informational social influence
  3. obedience to authority
  4. group polarization
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. normative social influence

If people had truly come to believe the group was correct (informational influence), their private answers would also follow the group. The drop in private conformity shows that the public agreement mainly reflected a wish to be accepted and avoid disapproval, which is normative influence. No authority gave orders, and polarization concerns shifts after group discussion.

Sample: MCAT Socialization, Norms and Deviance Practice Questions

In a hypothetical follow-up of 250 adults after a first arrest, 150 came to describe themselves mainly as "criminals," and 60 of them were arrested again within three years. Of the 100 who did not adopt that self-description, 15 were arrested again. What was the rearrest rate among those who adopted the label, and which theory is this pattern most consistent with?

  1. 40 percent; labeling theory
  2. 60 percent; labeling theory
  3. 24 percent; strain theory
  4. 15 percent; differential association
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 40 percent; labeling theory

The rate is 60 divided by 150, which is 40 percent, compared with 15 percent in the other group. Higher reoffending among people who accepted a deviant identity fits labeling theory's secondary deviance. Twenty-four percent wrongly divides by all 250 people, and 60 is a count, not a rate.
